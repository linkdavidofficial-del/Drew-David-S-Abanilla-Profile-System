Chapter 2: The OMP-Site

The drive out of the city limits was agonizingly long and suffocatingly dull. The initial adrenaline of the street brawl faded into a monotonous hum as the neon glare of Manhattan gave way to dark, industrial blackness, and finally to the desolate, unpaved borders of the state. The tall woman sat in the back of the transport, her long legs cramped against the steel partition, staring out the window with an expression of profound boredom.

Eventually, the truck slowed, its tires crunching over loose gravel. Ahead, looming out of the pitch-black night, stood a massive perimeter. Iron fences stretched as far as the eye could see, topped with dense, glittering coils of razor wire. A lone checkpoint cut through the barrier, guarded by a soldier holding an assault rifle at the low ready. Three more soldiers stood a few paces back, their silhouettes sharp and rigid under the harsh security floodlights.

The police transport crept forward and ground to a halt right at the gate.

The primary guard stepped up to the driver's side. His combat boots clicked against the gravel. The officer rolled down the window, the cool night air rushing into the cabin, carrying the scent of pine and ozone.

"Where to?" the soldier asked, his voice flat, muffled slightly by a ballistic neck gaiter.

The driver didn't say a word. He simply jerked his thumb toward the back seat.

Two of the backup soldiers moved to the rear window, flicking on high-intensity tactical lights. The white beams sliced through the tint, cutting across the darkness of the holding cell to pin the woman in their glare. The moment the light hit her face—illuminating her slit-like pupils and the long, sharp horns curving elegantly from her brow—the soldiers visibly tensed. Their boots shifted into firing stances, their rifles rising in a synchronized, lethal arc, aiming directly through the reinforced glass.

"Whoa, relax," the driver officer called out, leaning his head slightly out the window. "She's cuffed. Special suppressive cuffs. She ain't doing jack." He shifted into the park. "We're sending this thing somewhere else. We're hoping to call the OMP-Site to send a team to pick her up, and then we'll be on our way."

The lead soldier remained silent, his eyes cutting back to the driver. "On whose orders?"

"Our chief got the directive straight from the higher-ups," the officer replied smoothly. "The OMP-Site should already be expecting the call."

The soldier stared at him for a long beat, assessing the cop's credentials. Satisfied, he turned his head slightly and gave a sharp jerk of his chin to the guard behind him. The second soldier unclipped a secure military walkie-talkie from his vest, speaking into it in low, muffled bursts of static. A few moments passed in tense silence before the radio crackled a final response. The guard cut the feed and gave a sharp, affirmative nod.

The lead guard looked back at the officer. "Confirmed. Drive straight out for three kilometers. Stay off-road, close to the tree line, and wait there. They will send their men to meet you."

"Appreciate it," the driver said.

Behind the guard, a heavy mechanical groan echoed as the iron gates parted automatically. The transport rolled through the threshold, leaving the last shred of regular civilization behind. For the next few minutes, the cabin was dead silent, the atmosphere awkward and heavy. Nobody spoke. The truck jolted violently as the driver veered off the asphalt, steering the vehicle into the rough, uneven terrain right along the dense, black edge of the forest.

The truck hissed to a final stop, its headlights cutting into the thick timber.

Suddenly, the air in front of the vehicle began to warp. A violent spike of pressure popped everyone's ears as a localized transport portal tore open in the dark, humming with a low, terrifying vibration. Out of the shimmering rift stepped fourteen men. They were a strike team, heavily armed with high-tech, blacked-out rifles, reinforced full-body ballistic armor, and sealed tactical helmets that obscured any trace of humanity.

The strike leader stepped forward, his heavy boots sinking into the mud as he approached the police vehicle. The two officers immediately opened their doors and stepped out, hands raised slightly to show they were non-hostile.

"Is this the mutant?" the strike leader's voice echoed through a vocal synthesizer in his helmet.

"Yeah," the driver said, gesturing to the rear. "She's all yours. Careful though, she might find a way out of those cuffs if you give her a second."

"Regular cuffs?" the leader asked, his helmet tilting.

"Mutant special issue," the passenger officer chimed in, crossing his arms. "We're professionals, you know."

The soldier gave a cold, dismissive nod. He raised a hand, gesturing to his squad. "Circle it. Secure the perimeter."

In an instant, the twelve heavily armed men fanned out, forming a tight, impenetrable ring around the police transport, their high-tech rifles raised and aimed dead-center at the chassis. Two other soldiers stepped up to the rear doors, unlocking the heavy latches with a loud, metallic clack. They threw the doors open, staring up at the muscular woman.

"Out," one of them barked.

She didn't hesitate. She stepped down from the cabin with an easy, fluid grace, her chains rattling against the metal step. The moment her boots hit the dirt, the two guards grabbed her arms with iron grips. In a swift, practiced motion, they snapped off the police restraints and instantly replaced them with OMP-issue cuffs—thicker, heavier blocks of dark, dampening metal that locked around her wrists with a heavy pneumatic hiss, neutralizing her physical leverage.

A third soldier stepped in closer to conduct the intake sweep. His hands were blunt and forceful, tapping down her body, checking the pockets of her open red jacket, running down her muscular ribs, and searching the lining of her black crop top for concealed weapons or tech. Finding nothing, he stepped up directly to her face.

"Mouth," he ordered.

The woman smirked, casually dropping her jaw open without a hint of resistance. The soldier flicked on a small, intense tactical flashlight, thrusting the beam into her oral cavity. He aggressively inspected the structure of her tongue, her razor-sharp teeth, and the roof of her mouth, ensuring no hidden capsules or tools were tucked away.

Satisfied, he clicked the light off and turned to the waiting police officers. "Good work. You can all go now. Forget this ever happened."

The officers didn't need to be told twice. They nodded quickly, scrambled back into their transport, and began backing away into the darkness.

The strike leader gripped the woman’s shoulder, spinning her around toward the humming rift. The fourteen soldiers marched her directly into the shimmering light of the portal. The moment they stepped through, the outer portal slammed shut behind them with a definitive, deafening pop that severed them from the outside world entirely.

The woman instinctively turned her head to look back at the space where the portal had just vanished, her eyes narrowing at the smooth concrete wall that now stood in its place.

"Face front, mutie, and keep walking," a guard growled, slamming the butt of his rifle hard into her shoulder blade to force her forward.

She remained silent, absorbing the strike without a flinch, and continued her stride. As her eyes adjusted to the blinding fluorescent lights overhead, the sheer scale of the processing facility revealed itself. They were standing in a massive, high-security intake terminal. Dozens of other portals were actively cycling, depositing convicted mutants from all over the globe into the central holding area.

The room was a cacophony of shouted orders, wet impacts, and the low hum of active suppression fields. To her left, a heavily built mutant man was on his knees, tears streaming down his face as three guards surrounded him.

"I'm innocent!" the man wailed, his voice echoing off the metallic walls. "I didn't do anything wrong! It was an accident!"

"Shut your mouth," a guard barked, swinging a heavy, high-voltage riot baton directly into the man's ribs. The blunt crack of the plastic against bone was accompanied by a violent blue spark. The man grunted, collapsing into a heap on the floor. "You're a mutant with a threat level high enough to bring down a skyscraper. Move."

A few paces ahead, a small, frail teenage girl with striking, crystalline eyes stumbled under the weight of her dampening collar, her boots dragging against the floor. A guard walking behind her didn't hesitate; he brought his heavy combat boot forward, kicking her sharply in the lower back. When the guard's boot connected, her eyes flashed a brilliant, refractive white, and a sound like tapping glass escaped her throat.

"Keep moving, brat," he muttered as the girl gasped, tumbling forward onto her hands and knees before scrambling back up in terror.

The column of prisoners pressed onward, driven by the relentless prodding of armored guards. The woman kept her pace, her slit-like eyes scanning the brutal efficiency of the intake line. They marched out of the processing terminal and through a set of huge, blast-resistant inner walls, stepping into the true heart of the prison site. Looming directly ahead of them, dominating the bleak landscape under a synthetic sky, was an immense, terrifyingly sterile central tower that cast a long, dark shadow over every single prisoner below.
