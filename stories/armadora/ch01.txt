Chapter 1: Rogue

The screech of locked brakes tore through the humid night air, followed instantly by the rising wail of sirens and the raw, unbridled terror of civilian screams. The street was a theater of chaotic violence. Police officers yelled commands, their voices cracking under the weight of sheer panic, but those orders were drowned out by the heavy, sickening thuds of flesh striking flesh.

"No, please, please!" a man begged, his plea cut short by a brutal, bone-shattering punch that sent him crashing into the asphalt.

A manic, echoing laughter pierced the din. It belonged to her.

She moved with terrifying speed, catching an oncoming officer by the vest and tossing him effortlessly across the pavement like a sack of discarded laundry.

"It's a mutie! Get her! Shut her down!"

Muzzle flashes strobed against the dark brick walls as a volley of bullets flew through the air. The woman didn’t flinch. A sharp, mocking smirk cut across her face. In a blurred burst of speed, she sidestepped the gunfire, dropped a rushing officer with a vicious sweep, and grabbed another by the throat with her remaining hand. With a grunt of casual effort, she launched the gasping lawmen directly into the path of the shooting officers. The heavy collision sent both men tumbling to the ground in a tangled heap of body armor and groans.

Turning on her heel, she locked eyes with the remaining line of police. A lone officer fired a desperate shot; she wove her head to the side, the bullet buzzing past her ear. Before the terrified man could chamber another round, she closed the distance. Her hand clamped down on the barrel of his service weapon. With a single, crushing squeeze of her palm, the metal deformed and snapped. Before he could even process the destruction of his firearm, her fist swung forward, striking him dead in the jaw. He went out cold, dropping like a stone.

She turned her gaze back toward the road where civilians were scattering in every direction, their shrieks filling the avenues. Above, the mechanical, deafening whir of two police helicopters shattered the sky, their searchlights cutting through the smog and pinning her in a harsh, white glare. More sirens converged from every intersection.

"We got a mutie! Code red! Code red! A mutie gone rogue!" the dispatch radio blared from a crashed cruiser.

The flashing emergency lights illuminated the grime of the New York street, casting a stark glow over the graffiti scrawled across the concrete walls. “MUTANTS ARE NOT HUMAN?” was sprayed in jagged black letters across a fading mural that depicted a united world, where people held hands in a peaceful circle. Right over the faces of that painted unity, fresh, violent strokes of red paint screamed: “F*CK MUTIES” and “DIE.”

Behind her, the shattered glass storefront of a convenience store glittered under the streetlamps, pieces of the pane crunching under heavy boots. A stray brick lay amid the debris. At least four police cruisers had now formed a tight, smoking barricade around her. In the center of the ruin stood the woman, unbothered. The harsh spotlight caught the gleam of the sharp horns protruding from her brow.

"Hands on your head right now! Don't move!" a dozen voices screamed in unison, a forest of gun barrels trembling as they pointed directly at her chest.

The woman didn't fight. She simply raised her arms, her smirk never wavering, and took slow, deliberate steps forward. The officers closed in with extreme caution, hands shaking as they slapped heavy-duty tactical cuffs around her thick, muscular wrists.

Across the street, a live news truck had already deployed its mast. A camera crew scrambled into position, the bright lens focusing on the scene as a breathless reporter adjusted her earpiece and spoke rapidly into her microphone.

"This just in from New York City," the newscaster announced, her voice rising above the ambient chaos. "A violent mutant has just destroyed a local convenience store, leaving a trail of devastation. Initial reports indicate at least twenty-seven civilians and forty-three officers have been injured at the scene. And this is not an isolated incident—videos of similar mutant outbursts are going viral as we speak."

The reporter pointed a manicured finger toward the background, where the tall woman was being escorted toward a heavy transport vehicle.

"As you can see behind me, the suspect has finally surrendered peacefully, though authorities believe this was only after exhausting her mutant capabilities due to the sheer scale of the property damage and the massive response from law enforcement. She will surely be facing immediate imprisonment in a highest-priority facility. Stay tuned as we bring you more updates on this developing crisis."

The woman chuckled darkly as the officers roughly shoved her into the back of the armored transport. The heavy steel door slammed shut, cutting off the glare of the media lights, leaving her in the dim, red glow of the vehicle's interior.

Outside, the frantic coordination of the aftermath leaked through the reinforced walls.

"Damn mutie... these damn things are getting more aggressive by the day," an officer muttered, leaning against the exterior of the truck.

"It's the end of times, mate," his partner replied, spitting on the ground. "We just have to hope they wipe these things out very soon."

A sergeant’s voice barked over the noise, trying to regulate the unfolding disaster. "Count the heads! And where are the freaking ambulances at? It’s been five minutes!"

"We got reports they're on their way, sir! A dozen of them are inbound!"

"Get the hospitals on the line right now and tell them to reserve trauma bays for our men! Check the civilians! Get the first aid kits moving!"

Inside the dark compartment, the woman sat back against the metal bench. Her slit-like pupils adjusted instantly to the gloom, staring through the reinforced mesh window at the chaotic scene fading outside. The front doors of the cabin clicked open and slammed shut as two heavily armed officers took their seats in the front. The engine roared to life.

"Alright, mutie, you're going to be in big trouble this time," the driver growled, checking his rearview mirror.

The woman remained silent for a beat. She tilted her head slightly, a side glance catching the driver's eyes in the glass. Her smirk widened, relaxed despite the heavy restraints binding her.

"Hey, a girl needs to cool off some steam," she said, her voice smooth, casual, and entirely devoid of remorse. "Besides, those store deals are just rigged. They're practically stealing from people in broad daylight."

The passenger officer scoffed loudly, throwing a disgusted look over his shoulder. "Like it matters to you. It was probably something you couldn't afford anyway. Idiot... you don't even look like you have a job, dressed like that."

His eyes lingered on her reflection. She was exceptionally tall, her heavily muscled frame straining against a bright red jacket that hung open, revealing a tight black crop tank top underneath.

She chuckled, the sound vibrating in her chest. "Well, what can I say? I'm good at fighting. A good brawl is just what I needed to get a proper stretch in."

"Psycho..." The officer shook his head in disgust, turning away from her. He unclipped his heavy walkie-talkie and keyed the mic. "Chief, we got her secured in the back. Where are we dropping this one? Standard precinct?"

A moment of static buzzed through the speaker before a gravelly, exhausted voice responded. "Hmph. Don't bother placing her in any local station. This ain't our rat to catch..." A heavy silence hung on the line for a second before the Chief continued. "I've already received a call from the higher-ups. They saw the live news broadcast. We don't need this on our paperwork. Divert the transport. Take that thing directly to the OMP-Site now. This is their problem."

"Copy that, Chief. Out."

The driver spun the steering wheel, throwing the heavy transport into a hard turn as they sped away from the shattered storefront. He glanced back at the mirror one last time.

"You messed up, girl," he said, his voice dropping into a grim, dark tone. "Say goodbye to sunlight. Got any last words?"

His partner nudged him sharply in the ribs. "Forget it, man. These are muties. They're erratic, always have been. Just shut up and drive." He sighed heavily, looking out at the passing streetlights. "Besides... she's as good as dead anyway now that she's been marked for the OMP-Site."

The woman didn't respond to the threat. She simply leaned her head back against the cold steel interior, her gaze shifting away from the officers. She looked out the side window, her slit-like pupils focusing intently on the dark city streets rolling past.

A small, subtle smile crept onto her lips. Her eyes narrowed, her focus sharpening on the distant skyline as the truck sped toward the outskirts.

"Hmmm... yeah..." she whispered to herself, her voice a low, dangerous purr. "Shame indeed..."
