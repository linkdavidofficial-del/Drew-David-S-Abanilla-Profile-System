Chapter 9: Siren Song

The grand crystal chandeliers of the Sydney Opera House trembled violently as the final, haunting vibrato of Eden’s closing aria dissolved into the cavernous space. For three breathless heartbeats, the packed auditorium sat in absolute, stunned paralysis—then erupted into a deafening, thunderous explosion of applause. Thousands of flashbulbs ignited simultaneously like a frantic constellation of dying stars, illuminating the stage where her deep magenta curls shimmered brilliantly under the harsh focus of the spotlights. She bowed deeply, a single white-gloved hand pressed elegantly against her chest.

"Brava! perfection!" a senator’s wife wept openly, dabbing her tear-stained eyes with a monogrammed silk handkerchief. "I have never in my life witnessed such breathtaking vocal control—it felt like pure, liquid gold pouring directly into my soul!"

"It was transcendent," the woman continued, clutching tightly at her multi-strand pearl necklace as the residual echoes of the performance finally faded from the rafters.

Beside her, a high-ranking diplomat's aide exhaled a sharp, ragged breath through his nose, his entire body trembling slightly. "Lady Eden is a miracle of a singer... did you see how effortlessly she sustained those impossibly high notes at the climax?" His gnarled fingers twitched erratically against the plush velvet armrests of his VIP seat, imprinting the expensive fabric with a thick layer of nervous sweat.

"Yeah, I can see that clear enough," muttered a naval attaché stationed three seats down the row. His voice was oddly flat, his gaze detached from the stage. Instead, his eyes flicked repeatedly toward the shadowy exit corridors where Eden's personal security detail stood. The guards were motionless in their tailored, slate-gray corporate suits, the small metallic pins on their lapels and the glass lenses of their earpieces gleaming coldly under the dim house lights.

On stage, Eden's long, dark eyelashes fluttered open slowly, like a porcelain doll being mechanically brought to life. Her perfect, red-lipped smile bloomed with a calculated precision—first curving slightly at the left corner, then the right, as if her facial muscles were manually calibrating the exact wattage of human warmth required to disarm this specific high-society crowd. The waves of applause crested again as she offered a final, sweeping bow, her thick magenta hair cascading forward to brush the lacquered surface of the stage floor.

The velvet stage curtains had barely settled into place when the reception hall transformed into a lavish, bustling gala. Crew members in crisp black uniforms rolled out towering, shimmering pyramids of crystal champagne flutes while mutant servers, their anomalies subtly hidden beneath fine fabrics, threaded seamlessly through the elite crowd with silver trays loaded with beluga caviar canapés. Eden's melodic, bell-like laughter rang out like fine crystal as she descended the grand marble staircase of the lobby, flanked on either side by twelve identical twin bodyguards who moved in eerie, mirrored synchronization.

"So, tell us, what will today's grand charity occasion entail?" the senator's wife asked, leaning in close. Her diamond choker caught the light of the central chandelier as she eagerly clutched Eden's gloved hand.

Eden's flawless smile never wavered for a fraction of a second, though her eyes flicked minutely toward her lead bodyguard. "I have personally finalized the funding for four additional private schools dedicated to displaced orphans," she murmured, her voice a rich, velvet purr that carried effortlessly over the low murmur of the surrounding crowd. A waiter in black livery offered her a fresh flute of vintage champagne; she declined the beverage with the barest, most elegant twitch of her fingers. "Financial capital is no obstacle for my organization."

"You are truly a model philanthropist for our age," the naval attaché gushed from behind her, his posture stiffening as his eyes once again scanned the perimeter of the room, tracking the silent, unblinking movements of her security staff.

"And what of your newest commercial idol venture, Lady Eden?" a prominent entertainment journalist interjected, edging her way closer to the inner circle. Her digital audio recorder hovered near Eden's lips like a supplicant begging for crumbs from a royal table.

Eden let out a soft, melodic chuckle—a sound resembling fine ice sliding across sheets of raw silk—as she raised a hand to adjust the pearl pins nestled deep within her vibrant hair. "Ah, yes," she said smoothly, her gloved fingers tracing the rim of an untouched wineglass on a passing tray. "I simply seized a marvelous opportunity to personally mentor the next generation of raw talent. It would be a profound shame not to cultivate them, though I have already accomplished virtually everything there is to achieve in my own performance career."

"Of course, of course. You’ve already assembled multiple highly successful idol groups under the central Siren Girl Pop banner," the journalist replied quickly, her pen hovering over her notepad, the black ink bleeding deep into the paper as her grip trembled slightly.

Eden's perfume—a heavy, intoxicating scent that was bespoke and vaguely lethal—floated into the woman's nostrils, making her head spin. Eden's smile sharpened like a scalpel sliding from a velvet sheath as one of her twin bodyguards shifted his weight minutely, his large gloved hand drifting closer to the concealed holster beneath his tailored jacket.

"Mm... true talent always recognizes talent," Eden murmured, her fingers patting a pearl pin that hadn't moved a single millimeter out of place.

"Yes, yes, we all agree. We’re just incredibly curious—how do your talent scouts consistently discover so much raw vocal ability across the globe?" the senator's wife interjected again, her heavy diamond bracelets clinking loudly as she gestured toward the grand stage. "Every single one of your young protégés sings like a nightingale from their very first rehearsal!"

The ambient light of the chandelier caught Eden's pupils, causing them to contract into thin, vertical reptilian slits for a mere fraction of a second before dilating back into perfect human roundness. "Rigorous scouting protocols," she stated smoothly, snapping her fingers once.

A waiter materialized instantly with a fresh tray of hors d'oeuvres, effectively cutting off the journalist's attempt to press further. "My specialized talent acquisition teams scour local orphanages, elite musical conservatories... even forgotten street corners in the lower districts. I possess an immense network of resources, darling."

"The latest batch of international recruits numbers only four, yet they are already remarkably skilled," the naval attaché added, his voice cracking slightly mid-sentence as Eden's cold, calculated gaze slid directly toward him. He cleared his throat roughly, his sweaty fingers tightening around his champagne flute until the crystal groaned. "I mean... their vocal control during the private showcases was... hypnotic."

"A hundred registered members across your entire label is quite the ensemble—have you considered dividing the contracts into smaller subsidiaries?" the journalist asked, her pen hovering over the paper where her grip continued to shake.

Around the immediate circle, the opera house's glittering attendees seemed to freeze mid-sip, their champagne flutes suspended in mid-air as the air in the room grew inexplicably heavy. Eden, entirely unfazed by the scrutiny, flipped her dense magenta curls over her left shoulder. The elegant movement sent her pearl pins glinting under the lights, each one positioned at a mathematically precise angle to catch the light without dazzling the cameras.

"One step at a time, my dear," she murmured, her gloved fingers continuing to trace the rim of the untouched glass. A single droplet of condensation clung to the crystal edge, trembling like a trapeze artist over a void. "We take everything... one step at a time."

Twenty minutes later, the glittering facade was stripped away. Eden exited the restricted rear doors of the opera house and stepped directly into the plush, reinforced interior of her private, armored luxury car, escorted by her silent mutant guards. The heavy door sealed shut with a pressurized, mechanical thud that locked out the sounds of the Sydney night.

She sank deeply into the leather seat with a long, controlled sigh, an exhausted smile finally replacing her public mask. "People and their insatiable questions," she chuckled darkly to herself, leaning her head back against the headrest. "They always desperately want to know every single detail, chasing the cheap thrill of gossip the moment they manage to catch a high-tier celebrity's attention."

Relaxing further into the custom interior, she glanced across the spacious cabin. "Isn't that right, General Mavankill?"

Sitting directly opposite her was an intimidating, eight-foot-tall cybernetic being. He sat rigid, possessing a terrifying military precision, clad in an immaculate, dark gray formal imperial uniform. His massive, heavy robotic hands were constructed from a matte-finish dark alloy, resting heavily on his knees, and his heavy, angular head featured a black glass screen where a single, intensely bright red optical dot stared back at her.

"Please, General Eden," the machine replied, his voice a deep, synthesized baritone that vibrated through the floorboards as he gestured smoothly with his right hand. "You, too, have deliberately engineered a prodigious global audience for the sole purpose of hearing your voice and seizing their collective psychological attention." A brief processing pause ensued, the internal cooling fans of his chest plate whirring softly. "Though public attention wasn't the primary operational objective of tonight's deployment, I shall formally commend your execution. Congratulations on completing today's sub-aquatic infiltration mission with your Siren Pop units."

Eden smiled—a genuine, knowing expression that was significantly colder than anything she had displayed to the human politicians. She crossed her legs and folded her arms over her chest, exhaling a breath of pure satisfaction. "It took far longer than our usual operations. I had to establish direct neural connections through the acoustic arrangements. I had to force them to truly hear the frequency of my voice." She closed her eyes briefly, then snapped them open to lock onto his red optical sensor. "If I hadn't kept expanding the vocal range during that third chorus, I might have missed hypnotizing the naval attaché. His mental shielding was surprisingly resilient."

Mavankill gave a slow, mechanical nod of his heavy head. "Let us formally summarize today's mission metrics, General." With a faint electronic chirp, he projected a crisp, emerald-green holographic data screen into the air space between them. "How many high-value human assets did you successfully target during the live performance? I will also require the baseline telemetry reports from your subordinate Siren Pop members regarding their individual sub-missions and whether they successfully overrode the consciousness of their respective VIP audiences."

"Of course," her voice softened into a precise, professional cadence. "Let us begin the logging sequence."

The car's luxurious interior hummed quietly with the low-frequency vibrations of Mavankill's internal processors as Eden tapped her manicured nails rhythmically against the armrest. Outside, Sydney's vibrant neon skyline streaked past the thick, tinted ballistic windows like liquid electricity.

"Forty-two key assets," she stated coldly, watching her own reflection smirk in the dark glass of the window. "Federal senators, black-market arms dealers, and three lead investigative journalists representing competing global news networks. Every single one of them was actively singing my tune by the time the third chorus concluded." She flicked her wrist sharply, and the holographic display instantly reshaped into a complex constellation of pulsing crimson dots—each one representing a newly implanted, deep-layer post-hypnotic command structure within a human mind. "The naval attaché was incredibly stubborn, as I mentioned. I had to manually layer four separate sub-vocal arias into his subconscious before he finally stopped glancing toward our security exits."

Mavankill's interface screen flickered to a bright orange hue as his processors cross-referenced her spoken numbers with the live field telemetry. "Siren Unit Gamma confirms twelve high-value targets successfully compromised within the local financial sector," the machine intoned. A highly detailed structural schematic of the opera house materialized in the air, highlighting the private VIP boxes where Gamma's field members had performed their duties while disguised as catering staff. "Their recorded neural waveforms display an identical, stable hypnotic signature. Highly efficient operational execution."

Eden's glove creaked sharply as she clenched her fist into a tight ball, causing the holograph to instantly collapse back into a single, pulsing line that mirrored a heartbeat rhythm. "And what of the children we located?" she asked, gesturing toward the rear storage files. "I personally selected those specific girls from the regional sectors. I find them to be incredibly useful candidates for our new talent pool."

"Your assessment is mathematically correct," Mavankill replied. He reached into an armored compartment within his uniform chest plate and withdrew a small, reinforced glass vial containing a shimmering, blue chemical compound. "Upon performing full biometric scans of the children you selected, their unique genetic signatures confirm the presence of localized mutations within their vocal tracts—vocal hypnotic bases almost identical in structure to your own archetype and the secondary units."

Eden's smile widened, her eyes gleaming. "Excellent. I was thinking it would give them a vastly superior purpose in life rather than letting them rot away in those human run-down orphanages. So, what is their current physical status?"

"They are currently in secure transit to our extraction craft," Mavankill stated, sliding the vial back into his chassis. "The human orphanage director never questioned the validity of our mandatory 'vaccination' initiative. Their vocal cords will remain perfectly intact for the next wave of imperial singers. During the extraction phase, they will sleep soundly through the entire transport."

Eden’s expression soured slightly, a low sigh escaping her lips. "You sedated them, Mavankill?"

"My extraction personnel were experiencing significant tactical difficulties in securing the juvenile targets through more... docile containment methods," the machine explained, his red eye remaining static. "The children attempted to flee the facility, Eden."

"That is because the vast majority of your deployment personnel are eight-foot-tall, bulky tactical robots, Mavankill," she sighed heavily, her tone laced with irritation as she rubbed her temples. "Please, do not authorize the chemical sedation of my recruits again without consulting me first."

"My apologies, General Eden. I merely executed the fastest, most logistically sound protocol available to ensure zero operational delay," Mavankill responded, his internal gears clicking smoothly as he re-aligned his posture. "However, I performed the necessary diagnostics to ensure that none of the targets sustained any physical injuries. They are currently asleep and verified to be in peak physiological health."

Eden's flawless composure returned, her sharp smile locking back into place. "Excellent. We will ensure those new recruits learn their true purpose within our ranks the moment we touch down. Their voices will be developed first. Their loyalty to the crown will be solidified second. The rest of their operational training will follow naturally."

Mavankill's screen flickered through several dense processing cycles before the synthesizer engaged. "It will require a significant amount of standard training time for them to master the basic frequency layers," the mechanical voice stated flatly. A small hydraulic hiss echoed from his chest as he retrieved a black data crystal. "The primary neural conditioning protocols alone require—"

Eden's hand snapped up instantly, cutting him off mid-sentence with absolute authority. She stared directly at the machine, her slit pupils locking onto the red dot of his ocular sensor until it shrank to a tiny pinprick under her gaze. When she spoke again, her voice was a low, terrifying whisper that carried the weight of shifting glaciers.

"General Mavankill... please understand me perfectly clear, on this matter. I intend to have those children return safely to the heart of the Empire. They will not be treated as meat for the grinder. They are singers first, and strategic intelligence agents later. We are the Armadora Empire. We are not like the human factions who treat mutated children as cheap weapons of war."

For three agonizing seconds, the only sound inside the luxury vehicle was the high-pitched, frantic whirring of Mavankill's processing core. His digital display flashed through a rapid, chaotic scroll of mathematical re-calculations before finally settling back into a steady, unblinking red dot.

"Of course, General," the machine stated at last, the tone was flat and devoid of any resistance. "Your operational mandate is understandable."

Eden let out a soft sigh, relaxing her posture once more. "While I fully comprehend your strategic reasoning regarding speed and efficiency, General, I must insist that you let me handle the internal logistics of my own division."

The machine gave a singular, respectful nod. "Very well then. I shall not comment further on the recruitment process." A low mechanical whirr echoed from his joints as he crossed his enormous alloy arms over his chest plate. "In that case, we should expedite our return to the North Pole and take a step back from our foreign assets. I must return to the central citadel immediately to brief the Intelligence Department."

He paused, the data screen blinking with fresh perimeter alerts. "I have just received encrypted field reports from the northern sectors indicating that human reconnaissance groups are becoming aggressively active along our borders. General Kevin’s fleet is currently arriving back at the capital coordinates as we speak to secure the landing zones. Speaking of which... the High Empress is expecting both of us to attend the grand strategic summit next week."

Eden smiled softly, her eyes tracking the neon lights outside. "Oh? Is that so?" She gestured elegantly with her gloved hand. "I assume this urgent gathering is primarily regarding the mobilization of Phase Two?"

"That is the primary point of order, yes. But it will also encompass the formal review of all internal department logistics and resource allocations."

Eden hummed a soft, melodic note of acknowledgment, nodding her head slowly. She looked out the window at the dark Australian night, a faint sigh escaping her lips.

"Is there a logistical problem, General Eden?" Mavankill asked, his optical sensor tracking her movement.

"No, no. Not at all," she chuckled softly to herself, looking away from her reflection as she gently closed her eyes, allowing her body to finally absorb the steady, quiet drive of the armored vehicle. "It is simply... incredibly tiring to constantly keep up these human appearances."

Mavankill’s glowing red optic node remained fixed on her silent form, his processors continuing to log data in complete silence as the heavy armored car veered off the main highway, accelerating smoothly toward a highly restricted, heavily guarded private sector of the international airport where a sleek, black stealth transport aircraft sat waiting on the tarmac, its powerful engines whining in the dark.
