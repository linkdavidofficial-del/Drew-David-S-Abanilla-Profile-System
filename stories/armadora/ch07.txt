Chapter 7: Sanctuaries in the Frozen Graveyard

Deep within the darkness of the Arctic Circle, the black ocean water looked like thick, rolling oil. A modest, disjointed fleet of battered wooden fishing trawlers, rusted crabbing boats, and fiberglass lifeboats bumped against the encroaching ice floes. Every single vessel was packed to capacity, loaded with heavy crates of meager belongings and desperate, freezing passengers. Every person aboard carried some form of physical abnormality—the genetic markers of mutations that had made them outcasts in the world of ordinary men.

Down in the cramped, freezing hold of the lead trawler, a small child with an extra, malformed third arm protruding from her shoulder blades clung tightly to her mother’s waist. Her small fingers twitched against the damp fabric. "Momma? Are we there yet?" she whispered, her voice shivering violently from the damp chill.

The mother, bundled in a heavy, grease-stained weather jacket, pulled the girl closer to her chest, trying to shield her from the freezing draft leaking through the hull. "Just a bit further, sweetie... Just a bit further. Keep your eyes closed."

Up in the small navigation cabin, the thin veneer of patience among the refugees was rapidly snapping. "This is getting us absolutely nowhere!" a loud, aggressive voice boomed, cutting through the low thrum of the diesel engine. A barrel-chested mutant with thick, yellowed tusks jutting upward from his lower jaw stepped into the cramped space, glaring down at the pilot. "Are you sure about this, old man? You’re telling us this freezing void is the place?"

The old fisherman didn’t turn around. He adjusted his calloused grip on the rusted iron steering wheel, his gnarled fingers tapping rhythmically against the cold metal as if counting down the final seconds of their journey. His breath fogged heavily in the frozen air, mingling with the thick gray steam rising from a dented thermos filled with bitter, boiled kelp tea.

"Aye," the old man muttered, his voice as rough as the sea. "I only ever heard about them in whispers. Stories passed down by independent traders who vanished from the southern ports for months at a time, only to come back with severe frostbite and wide, terrified eyes. They spoke of a massive city buried deep in the north. A large city... but not one built for the likes of ordinary humans." His milky, cataract-covered left eye flicked sideways, catching the tusked mutant's reflection in the dark glass of the window. "You wanna call me a liar, boy? Go ahead. Jump off the bow, swim ahead, and see what's out there for yourself."

The tusked man snarled, the muscles in his thick neck bulging as he slammed a heavy, calloused fist against the wooden cabin wall, rattling the weak light fixture above. "I didn’t sign up to chase fairy tales in a frozen graveyard! We were promised a real sanctuary, not a—"

Before he could finish his threat, the trawler’s bow ground heavily against the snowy, jagged lip of a glacier shore. Instantly, a blinding, piercing flash of artificial light cut through the frigid darkness. The illumination didn't come from the sky or a lighthouse; it erupted from the tactical barrels of high-powered energy rifles, all leveled directly at the trembling fishing boats.

The armored figures materialized like ghosts out of the swirling white blizzard. Their bulky, insulated white camouflage suits blended seamlessly with the surrounding ice and snow, breaking up their human silhouettes. Even their faces were completely obscured behind opaque, polarized black visors, turning them into cold, faceless sentinels standing against the storm.

"Friend or foe?" one of the soldiers barked, his voice heavily distorted and deepened by his helmet's mechanical modulator.

The sharp, high-pitched hum of charging energy cells vibrated through the freezing air as the soldiers tightened their grips on their weapons.

"Wait! Wait—we're friends!" the tusked man stammered, his bravado instantly evaporating as his teeth and tusks clacked together in sudden terror. He threw his hands high above his head, stepping out onto the deck. Behind him, the cabin doors flew open, and the other mutants huddled together in the open air, their panicked breath rising in thick bursts of white fog. "We heard from the underground networks... about a place up here. A sanctuary for our kind."

An oppressive silence fell over the shoreline, broken only by the howling arctic wind. The lead soldier tilted his head slightly, his heavy rifle remaining perfectly level, trained directly between the tusked man's eyes. "Evidence?"

The soldier didn't lower his weapon, but the subtle tension in his gauntleted trigger finger shifted slightly. "Strip," he ordered, the command crackling coldly through his frost-laced helmet speakers.

The man's tusks twitched in disbelief. "What the hell do you mean—?"

"Not all of it," the soldier corrected, jerking the heavy barrel of his rifle toward the rest of the huddled passengers. "Show us your mutations. Every single one of you. Now."

A low, frightened murmur rippled through the crowd of refugees. The mother in the back clutched her three-armed child even tighter, trying to shield her from the cold, but the old fisherman was already moving. With a quiet grunt of resignation, he shrugged off his heavy oilskin coat, letting it drop to the frost-crusted deck.

Beneath the layers of clothing, his spine was revealed. It wasn't just hunched from age; it was heavily ridged and segmented like the carapace of an enormous desert insect. The thick, chitinous plates glistened a dull, dark brown under the glare of the tactical spotlights.

The lead soldier’s rifle didn’t waver an inch as the rest of the mutants reluctantly began revealing their respective deformities. Some peeled back wraps to show heavily scaled skin, others unfolded vestigial, leathery wings from beneath tattered cloaks, and a few opened secondary eyelids, revealing pupils that glowed like burning coals in the dark. The mother’s child whimpered softly as her third arm finally uncurled from its protective tuck, the tiny, grey-skinned fingers flexing uncomfortably in the freezing air. The tusked man snarled under his breath but peeled back his ragged shirt, exposing a secondary set of raw, pulsing gills flaring rhythmically along his rib cage.

The lead soldier's visor flickered with a faint, internal blue light as his integrated targeting matrix ran a rapid biometric scan of the trembling group, verifying the authenticity of their genetic anomalies. "Permission to search the vessels?"

The old fisherman exhaled a sharp, heavy gust of fog. "Y-yes... please. Search whatever you want. We mean absolutely no harm to your people."

At a silent hand signal from the leader, seven heavily armored figures boarded the fishing boats with practiced, methodical precision. Their heavy combat boots crunched loudly against the frost-crusted decks as they fanned out through the vessels. They moved like trained predators—checking the dark cargo holds, scanning primitive identification papers, and searching for hidden human technology or tracking beacons. One of the soldiers paused briefly in front of the mother, crouching low. The cold metal barrel of his rifle brushed against her knee as his visor inspected the little girl's extra limb. The child whimpered, burying her face deep into the damp wool of her mother's coat until the soldier stood back up and gave a curt, mechanical nod to his superior.

"You are here solely because you heard rumors about this location?" the lead soldier demanded, straightening his posture as the boarding party returned to the ice.

The tusked man swallowed audibly, his throat dry. "Yes. That is exactly what we were told by the underground cells."

The old fisherman stepped forward slightly, his chitinous spine flexing under the cold. "I heard the stories of this place from the others. I’ve never seen it, and I’ve never been this far north before. This is my first time. I only heard the coordinates from a reliable friend."

"A friend?" the soldier questioned.

"Yes. Many friends, actually. Merchant folk who trade in the black markets down south," the old man clarified, his body shivering as the wind picked up.

The lead soldier turned abruptly on his heel, his heavy armored glove slicing through the freezing air as he pointed out toward the vast, featureless expanse of white snow stretching into the dark polar horizon. "Go straight," he growled, his voice crackling through his helmet's internal filter. "There will be observation posts hidden deep within the drifts—iron markers, rusted but still standing. Follow them directly."

The wind howled with a sudden, deafening roar, nearly swallowing his next words until the soldier leaned closer to the bow of the boat. His dark visor reflected the terrified, frozen faces of the refugees. "No artificial lights of any kind," he emphasized, tapping the side of his helmet with a finger. "If you turn on your flashlights or lanterns, you will expose the approach vectors. They will see the light signatures from the satellites. Keep it dark."

With that final warning, the soldier stepped backward, his heavy boots sinking deep into the fresh powder. "Welcome to the Armadora Empire."

The old fisherman was the first to react. His segmented spine flexed with surprising strength as he hauled the boat's manual anchor back over the rail, securing the vessel against the ice shelf. The mutants exchanged wary, silent glances—some filled with a desperate surge of hope, others still deeply skeptical of what lay ahead—before they began shuffling out of the boats and onto the treacherous glacial ice. The mother held her child's third hand tightly, the extra fingers twitching erratically from the biting cold.

The small army of refugees was slowly herded forward through the biting, howling arctic winds, their bodies stumbling blindly across the frozen terrain. They walked with their eyes squinted shut against the blinding, wind-driven snow, their boots sinking deep into the drifts. Ahead of them, the first of the iron markers appeared—a jagged, vertical series of rusted steel plates half-buried in the white powder. The markers stood at regular, precise intervals, pointing stubbornly through the teeth of the storm toward some unseen, distant destination hidden behind the whiteout. The polar air was incredibly cold and thin, causing each ragged breath to turn into an instant explosion of thick fog before being ripped away by the gale. Visibility was atrocious, limited to barely a few feet in any direction. The wind whipped violently around them like a living, spectral presence watching their every single move.

Back near the shoreline, the armored sentry who had remained on the ridge adjusted the heavy nylon strap of his energy rifle, crunching the packed snow beneath his boots as he turned away from the coast. His breath fogged heavily against the exterior of his visor, briefly obscuring the thermal signatures of the latest group of mutants trudging through the blizzard behind them.

"It's the third batch of refugees we've processed this month alone," he muttered through the encrypted squad channel.

His partner didn’t answer immediately. He stood motionless on a high shelf of ice, scanning the bleak horizon where the tail end of the refugee column was currently vanishing into the blinding white void. The wind howled through the ice crags between them, carrying the distant, metallic creak of the rusted iron markers swaying under the tremendous force of the storm.

"...Hm," the second partner finally grunted.

The first sentry tilted his helmeted head, looking back down at the dark water where the abandoned fishing boats floated. "Should we report this influx to the command carrier?"

His partner exhaled sharply—a static-laced, heavy sigh filtering through his helmet's scrubbers. He turned sharply on his heel, the compressed snow compacting with a loud squeak under his tactical soles. "Report everything." The response was rote, an unthinking reaction drilled deeply into their minds through countless hours of rigorous Imperial briefings. He paused just long enough to glance back over his shoulder, his dark visor glinting with a dull purple hue under the aurora’s eerie, green glow overhead. "Always."

Then he strode away into the whiteout, his high-powered rifle slung tight against his back plate, leaving his partner to track the rapidly dwindling footprints of the newcomers before the fresh snow could erase them from the surface of the ice.

Miles ahead, the refugees continued to trudge onward blindly, driven forward by nothing but pure, unadulterated fear and the grim knowledge that there was absolutely no turning back for them. The human world had cast them out, and the open sea behind them was death.

"How much farther?" one of the older mutants mumbled from the middle of the line, his voice raspy and choked with frost as he strained to be heard above the deafening roar of the howling arctic winds.

No one answered him. One of the young children whimpered miserably, her small, blue-tinted hands clutching desperately at her mother's tattered coat. The mother simply wrapped her arm protectively around the girl's small frame, lowering her head into the wind, and pressed onward into the dark.

The trek became a grueling, endless march that rapidly blurred into a monolithic expanse of white. Every horizon looked identical, swallowed by the shifting curtains of the blizzard. The refugees’ eyes burned from the relentless assault of frozen needles, and the bitter wind stripped the moisture from their skin, leaving their lips split, bleeding, and crusted with frost.

"This damn snow!" the tusked man muttered, a low growl tearing through his throat as he forced his heavy legs through a deep drift. "Feels like it stretches on into eternity."

The old fisherman kept his jaw clenched tightly, his head bowed against the gale as he focused on putting one foot in front of the other. He didn't waste his breath on complaints. A few paces behind him, the mother clutched her child protectively against her torso, using her own body as a shield to deflect the worst of the sub-zero wind from the little girl.

Through the shifting veil of the storm, a sudden silhouette appeared. A woman stood on a ridge, waving her arm through the whiteout. Beside her stood a second, much slimmer figure clad in a remarkably thin, tight-fitting suit, similarly gesturing for the group to hurry. Neither of them appeared even remotely bothered by the lethal arctic environment.

"Hey, look up ahead!" one of the younger mutants called out, his voice cracking as he pointed a trembling finger toward the figures.

The entire column of refugees halted, their frozen faces turning upward. Relief and desperate hunger warred openly with deep-seated exhaustion and paranoia. The two figures beckoned from a short distance away, their arms sweeping through the air to guide the travelers inward.

The old fisherman squinted through the haze, his milky left eye narrowing to a sharp slit. The younger of the two figures was incredibly slender, her silhouette unyielding to the violent gusts that threatened to knock the refugees off their feet.

The two strangers walked down the slope to meet them. The taller woman wore a thick, heavily insulated, fluffy winter coat that was far more suited for the brutal storm. Her long blond hair whipped wildly around her face as she carried a heavy, high-caliber tactical rifle casually in her right hand. When she spoke, her thick Russian accent cut through the howling gale with clarity. "Heard your refugee fleet hit the western glacier shores a while ago," she said, her eyes scanning the group.

The slender one, clad in a vibrant pink tactical suit, adjusted her heavy earmuffs and the straps of her specialized backpack. She gestured toward a jagged, intimidating mountain range in the distance, a massive wall of ice riddled with treacherous ridges and sheer climbing paths. "Come on, follow us," she urged, pointing a gloved hand toward the peaks. "Over there—that's exactly where we're going to lead you into the heart of the Empire."

She turned back to the shivering children with a bright, contrasting smile. "Don't worry, we've got hot cocoa waiting inside."

"Hope the initial greeting with our perimeter peers wasn't too cold for you," the taller blonde added, a sharp smirk playing on her lips as she turned back toward the storm, her heavy military boots sinking deep into the fresh powder with every stride.

The refugees exchanged quiet, stunned glances. The younger children instantly perked up at the mere mention of a warm drink, their hollow eyes widening. The old fisherman, however, kept his gaze fixed warily on the heavy firearm swinging at the tall woman's side, measuring the sheer military professionalism of their guides.

The tusked man let out a rough, vibrating grumble from his chest. "To be honest, I'll take damn near any hot drink right now." He glanced up at the imposing mountain range, then turned his head to look at the mother and her three-armed child. "Come on, let's move. At least those two are heading toward actual shelter. It beats tramping blind through this endless white void."

As she led the column through the drifts, the blonde woman pulled a sleek, high-frequency communication device from her collar. "This is Captain Katyusha of the Frozen Front Battalion, currently moving with my partner Alisa. We have successfully secured the refugee detachment that followed the iron markers heading toward Entrance Elevator B."

A crisp, static-laced response immediately chirped back through her receiver from a central command post. "Affirmative, Captain. I will add the registry data to the imperial logs. Have either of you crossed paths with General Kevin on your route? We have an urgent encrypted message from the higher-ups—he is required for an immediate summons by Her Highness."

The pink-suited Alisa turned her head sharply toward her partner. "He’s currently out on a solo tracking patrol along the eastern perimeter! Send us the text of the message via Morse code encryption. We’ll relay it directly to him through one of our scout runners in that sector."

"Very well. Duly noted. Out." The communication line cut off with a clean electronic chime.

Katyusha slowed her pace slightly, allowing her heavy boots to grind to a halt as she turned her head toward Alisa. "Alisa, you break off and go find the General yourself. Our frontline forces are stretched incredibly thin right now, especially with this atmospheric thunderstorm rolling in. You have the structural speed advantage in that suit—I’ll take the responsibility of leading the refugees the rest of the way into the complex."

The smaller woman nodded sharply, her expression turning serious. "Okay! Understood." She quickly unlatched a compact submachine gun from her specialized backpack, gripping the weapon tightly as she veered off toward the far right flank, her silhouette vanishing into the thick curtain of the blizzard within a matter of seconds.

Katyusha turned back to the waiting column, her voice booming over the wind. "This way, people! Keep moving!"

The refugees followed her command without hesitation, their boots crunching rhythmically through the deep snow as they trudged behind the imposing officer. The old fisherman forced his way to the front of the line, his chitinous spine flexing under his wet coat as he fell into step right beside the blonde captain.

"Mind if I walk up here with you?" he muttered, his voice gravelly, dry, and thoroughly exhausted. Despite the physical toll of the march, his keen, milky eye remained remarkably sharp, watching her every movement.

Katyusha didn't look bothered by his proximity in the slightest. She gave a simple, firm nod while keeping her eyes locked on the path ahead. "Of course not," she said.

She moved forward effortlessly through the deep drifts, her trained gaze continuously sweeping across the white landscape before refocusing on the mountain coordinates. "It's been an incredibly busy month for our division," she muttered aloud, speaking as much to herself as to the old man. "With the vast majority of the Frozen Front Battalion being permanently relocated to the southern borders of the North. You must all be here after hearing the underground stories and news from the other mutant cells, yes?"

The old fisherman nodded slowly, his eyes fixed on the horizon as he plodded forward at her side, maintaining a steady, disciplined pace. "Aye. Word has been spreading fast through the channels... rumors floating around the black markets, frantic whispers echoing in the federal prisons. Stories about a grand place of sanctuary built exclusively for the likes of us, hidden deep within the icy wilds where humanity can't reach." He glanced sideways at the captain, studying the clean lines of her uniform before letting out a long, weary sigh. "To be completely honest with you, lady... I was starting to think the whole thing was nothing more than a beautiful pipe dream."

"Ah, the prisons—then you must have already heard about the total destruction of the OMP-Site," Katyusha chuckled, her warm breath forming thick clouds of fog in the glacial air as she adjusted the heavy nylon strap of her rifle. "Yeah, our operations are directly related to that little incident." She glanced back over her shoulder at the huddled mass of refugees, noting the thick layers of frost coating their eyelashes. The old fisherman's milky eye twitched slightly at the mention of the prison—a subtle tell that he knew far more about the underground network than he let on.

"You may not have noticed it yet down in the south," she continued, kicking a large chunk of frozen ice out of her path, "but the human world is about to descend into absolute, chaotic uproar. Six months, tops. Though—" She let out a sharp smirk. "—take that timeline with a grain of salt. I’m not authorized to spread unverified intelligence. But we've been hearing reports from the mercenary networks and independent hunters lurking around the perimeter lines, treating the borders of this place like a damn corporate gold rush."

The old fisherman gave a gruff, approving grunt, a sound caught somewhere between grim amusement and raw admiration for their foresight. "A gold rush, huh? Can't say I'm surprised by that. Desperate people will do things a hell of a lot worse than freezing their bollocks off in the Arctic for even a single glimmer of hope. Especially when they've got absolutely nothing left to lose to the system." He ran a heavily calloused, rough hand through his frozen, grizzled beard, his gaze turning distant. "Luckily for us, we managed to get out when we did."

"Luckily for you, you all came as quickly as possible," Katyusha agreed, her tone turning more serious as she scanned the gray sky. "If you hadn't made it tonight, within the next two weeks, the North Pole will be entirely closed off—and I mean completely unofficially, of course."

She smirked again, raising her free hand to sketch air quotes in the wind. "Direct orders came down from one of our high generals stationed in the Intelligence Department. The data suggests that Russia and Canada are about to initiate a hard military closure around the entire Arctic Circle, and Germany is getting dragged into the fold—some sort of, simulated diplomatic standoff."

Her chuckle was dark, muffled by the sudden roar of the wind against her hood. "Though, in reality, the whole thing was engineered through various twists and turns by our own operatives. The human governments will spend all their time blaming each other, closing off their national borders, and halting international trade..."

Her gloved fingers twitched in the air, vividly mimicking the manipulation of puppet strings. "Which means while they are busy pointing fingers at one another, the Empire gets free, unhindered access to trade routes and can drain resources from all three sides without anyone noticing a thing."

The old fisherman let out another rough grunt, his expression turning stoic but highly contemplative as he processed the sheer scale of the Empire's political chess game. "And having the human nations blame each other for the regional changes... sounds like your people play the political game remarkably well. A hell of a lot better than we ever could have managed on our own down there."

He glanced back at the shivering group of refugees behind them, then looked down at his own gnarled hands, where the dark, chitinous ridges of his mutation caught the dim arctic light. "I just hope it ain't us who end up serving as the final bargaining chips when all this political theater goes south."

"Oh, of course that's a given," Katyusha said, her voice carrying a soft, cold smile as the muzzle of her heavy rifle traced idle patterns through the falling snow.

The violent wind caught her words, scattering them across the ice like dying embers. "It's a brutal world out there—kill or be killed. Mutants like us have absolutely nowhere else to go on this map, which means we must stick together exactly like a family."

She let out a small, subtle scoff, her breath curling into a tight white spiral in the dark. "Though, of course, traitors still pop up here and there within the ranks. No system is absolutely perfect, but..."

She sighed heavily, her jaw tightening. "We tried to integrate everyone peacefully, but there are always those foolish individuals who want nothing more than to ruin their own lives and jeopardize the safety of everyone else for a human payday."

Her gaze shifted instantly, turning as sharp and clinical as a surgeon's scalpel as she locked eyes with the old fisherman. "We do not tolerate betrayal under any circumstances within the borders. Remember that. If anyone betrays their own kin to the outside forces, they are permanently stripped of their status. The lightest punishment available is immediate exile into the frozen wastes... the worse alternative is an immediate sentence of death."

The old fisherman met her icy stare steadily, his milky eye remaining completely unblinking despite the freezing wind. After a long, tense moment of mutual understanding, he gave a single, slow nod of his head. "Not much different from the world outside then," he muttered grimly. "Just with a hell of a lot less pretending."

A sudden, violent gust of arctic wind howled between the crags of ice, sharp enough to make several of the exhausted refugees flinch and cover their faces. The mother pulled her daughter closer, her boots slipping slightly on a patch of black ice as they forced their way through the relentless white wall.

"So... does that mean we're finally safe here, Momma?" the young child asked, her third hand clutching tightly at the damp sleeve of her mother's winter jacket.

The blonde captain smiled reassuringly down at the little girl, her thick Russian accent rolling over the group like distant thunder. "Of course you are safe, young one," she said, her voice filled with an unshakable pride. She jerked her chin upward toward the storm-choked sky overhead. "No human air vehicles or surveillance drones will ever breach our airspace without being blasted to pieces."

Her gloved thumb tapped the thick steel of her chestplate with a dull, metallic clink. "The Frozen Front Battalion—while the vast majority are currently deployed defensively along the outer boundaries—still maintains a permanent force of seventy-five thousand heavily armed soldiers patrolling this exact sector."

Her smirk widened, fierce and absolute. "The rest of the division? They are currently away on active defensive combat duties. There is a very specific reason our battalion earned its name."

The old fisherman adjusted his grip on his coat, his segmented spine flexing visibly beneath his layers as he looked around the desolate landscape. "How many total soldiers do you actually have out there in the blizzard?"

Katyusha laughed—a short, sharp, bark of a sound that was instantly swallowed by the roar of the storm. "Numbers mean absolutely nothing when you've got defensive walls that actively bite back at the enemy. And of course, every single one of us serves directly under General Kevin. Around the barracks, we call him 'The Cold Wall.' If any of you ever start to feel unsafe, just remember that he is permanently stationed at the front lines of the arctic, living out in these brutal blizzards just to personally verify that no human spies or scouts manage to breach the vicinity."

Her voice dropped to a lower, almost reverent register. "The General always finds liars—his senses are flawless. I’ve heard rumors that he’s a high-tier mutant possessing at least four distinct abilities simultaneously."

She turned back to the path, her rifle swinging smoothly to point at a massive, monolithic shadow looming directly ahead of them in the thick of the storm. "So stop worrying. You're all perfectly safe with us. In fact... we're almost there."

She came to a sudden halt.

The tusked man stepped out of the line, frowning deeply as thick frost crusted over his lower jaw and upper tusks. "Wait a damn minute—that’s nothing but a solid mountain wall of ice!" his voice boomed, bouncing off the sheer glacial rockface before being swallowed by the blizzard. "You told us this was the way into the city!"

Katyusha chuckled softly, her gloved fingers dancing casually near the rifle’s trigger guard. "I did say it was an entrance elevator, didn't I? A secret one." She raised her left hand and tapped her integrated commlink twice, the electronic tone sounding crisp and clear despite the wind. "Command, this is Captain Katyusha. Open Entrance Elevator B."

For three long heartbeats, nothing happened but the howling of the wind. Then, the entire snowy mountainside suddenly shuddered with a deep, subterranean vibration. A thin, perfectly straight seam appeared where absolutely none had existed a moment prior, cutting vertically through the ice face like a high-precision laser. The mountainous  'rockface' split apart with a colossal, deep-pitched hydraulic hiss, revealing a heavily reinforced titanium blast door wide enough to easily fit an armored combat tank.

Gargantuan sheets of frozen snow and ancient ice cascaded off the edges of the retracting panels as they slid smoothly into the mountain structure, exposing a cavernous, brilliantly lit industrial elevator platform. The sudden, intense wave of dry heat radiating outward from the interior caused the freezing refugees to collectively gasp—real, temperature-controlled warmth hitting their frozen skin after hours of agonizing arctic misery.

The refugees stared in awe, their mouths hanging open in sheer disbelief at the engineering marvel hidden within the glacier. The mother was the first to step forward cautiously, the welcoming warmth beckoning her like a long-lost friend after a lifetime of hiding in cold cellars. She reached out a trembling hand, touching the smooth, warm metal of the doorframe—almost afraid to believe that the structure was real.

The old fisherman let out a low, appreciative grunt, his sharp milky eye sweeping across the vast expanse of the hidden elevator platform with a mixture of profound astonishment and grudging military respect. "Quite the... secret passage you've got here, Captain."

"Step inside, everyone. The platform will take you straight down into the subterranean core of the Empire," Katyusha said, gesturing warmly toward the yawning elevator shaft, its interior glowing with bright, sterile white lighting.

She glanced down at her wrist-mounted tactical display, where thousands of biometric registries and coordination files were blinking in rapid succession. "I can’t join you for the descent—my patrol duty schedule calls me back out to the coast."

Her heavy rifle shifted comfortably against her shoulder as she gave a firm nod to the line of immigrants. "But don’t worry about a thing. Other imperial processing guards are waiting at the bottom station to verify your IDs, handle your medical screenings, and get you properly settled into the residential sectors."

The mother hesitated briefly at the threshold of the platform, her child’s three small hands gripping the fabric of her coat in collective anxiety. Katyusha noticed the hesitation and leaned down slightly, her voice dropping to a low, conspiratorial whisper that bypassed the wind. "A quick note for you: each registered family receives a baseline stipend the moment they pass processing to fully assist with your housing and relocation costs while you find standard employment."

She straightened back up to her full height, tapping the side of her helmet with a smirk. "And there are absolutely no income taxes within our borders either. The Empire’s central coffers stay permanently full thanks to our leadership—especially the General in charge of the Finance Department. That woman is an absolute, terrifying nightmare with a spreadsheet."

Her smirk turned as sharp and biting as the arctic wind outside. "Welcome to your new home."

The refugees exchanged wide-eyed, stunned glances, the old fisherman's heavy eyebrows raising in complete shock. Absolutely no tax grid? The entire proposition sounded almost too miraculous to be true for people who had spent their lives being hunted and exploited by human authorities. But their profound physical exhaustion—and the sudden, powerful surge of hope in their chests—instantly overruled their lingering caution.

The tusked man muttered something under his breath about the deal being "too damn good to be true," but he didn't hesitate to follow the others, stepping firmly onto the warm metal floor of the elevator platform.

The mother lifted her child into her arms, the little girl clinging tightly to her neck as she absorbed the heat. The woman cast one final, deeply cautious look back at Katyusha's faceless visor before disappearing through the massive doorway along with the rest of the forty-thousand-strong line of survivors.

Somewhere on the hostile southern perimeter of the white void, twelve human operators trudged heavily through the knee-deep drifts. The specialized heating coils of their high-end thermal suits hummed with a faint vibration, fighting a losing battle against the biting arctic wind. Their breaths came in short, strictly disciplined bursts—a technique drilled into them during black-ops training to minimize the condensation that would otherwise fog the interior of their tactical goggles. The lead operative, a grizzled reconnaissance veteran with a jagged combat scar running down his weathered cheek, suddenly raised a heavy, gloved fist. The entire squad froze instantly, their weapons raised to cover their respective sectors.

A single, pristine snowflake drifted downward, settling gently onto the center of the leader's protective visor. A millisecond later, the snowflake shattered along with his entire skull.

The high-caliber sniper round tore through his helmet casing with a deafening, metallic crack, the kinetic force obliterating the bone and spraying a graphic mist of brains and dark blood across the pristine white landscape. Before the remaining men could even register the deafening echo of the shot, nine more high-velocity rounds tore through the blizzard in a clinical, hyper-accelerated succession. Each bullet found its mark with terrifying, mathematical precision. Necks were snapped, chest plates were punctured, and torsos erupted as the invisible marksmen methodically dismantled the squad. Bodies dropped into the powder like heavy sacks of stone, painting the snow in a macabre, steaming artwork of crimson.

The final two surviving operatives hit the ice hard, scrambling frantically behind the meager cover of a frozen snowdrift.

"Fuck!" one of them hissed, his voice cracking into a panicked shriek as he pressed his back against the ice shelf. "Where the hell did that come from? Give me a trajectory!"

"I didn't sign up for this shit," the last standing operative growled into his internal commlink, his fingers scrambling for purchase in the blood-slick snow beneath him. His tactical thermal goggles were ruined, the left lens spiderwebbed into a useless matrix of cracked glass from a near-miss ricochet. He cut his eyes to his partner, but any hope of survival vanished. The man was incapacitated, pressing both of his trembling hands against a ragged, gaping hole where his stomach used to be. The freezing wind immediately carried the foul, metallic stench of ruptured intestines into the operative's nostrils, thick and sickeningly warm.

"We need immediate backup! Command, do you copy? We need a heavy extraction unit at our coordinates!"

"Can we even send the signal through the storm?" the dying soldier wheezed, his eyes rolling back.

"I'm trying, but—wait, the signal is jammed! No, the entire sector has a localized dampening field! Fuck, fuck, we're dead out here!"

"Then fire a retreating flare... we have to report the base coordinates to the higher—"

A heavy thump cut his words short. A heavy round punched directly through his throat, severing his vocal cords and sending him collapsing backward into the snowbank.

"Fuck! Guess I'm alone... Shit! Fucking shit!" the lone survivor screamed, pulling his sidearm with a shaking hand.

Up on a distant, jagged ice ridge that remained completely invisible to the naked eye against the swirling blizzard, a mutant sniper clad in seamless white camouflage ejected his spent casing. The hot brass pinged sharply against the ice before sinking into the drift, a small wisp of smoke rising from the snow. The sniper calmly slotted a fresh, armor-piercing magazine into the underbelly of his long-barreled rifle, sliding the bolt forward with a satisfying click.

"Two more humans trying to cross our sovereign borders..." he muttered, his breath curling like thick smoke from between his sharp, prominent fangs. Through the high-powered optics of his scope, the crosshairs settled comfortably over the trembling operative's temple.

"Mh... Make absolutely sure they never see the daylight again," his spotter replied from a few feet away, effortlessly chambering another round into his own rifle without wasting a single motion. His breath crystallized instantly in the sub-zero air, the heavy custom suppressor of his weapon steaming faintly as the howling blizzard swallowed the sound of the mechanical action sliding home.

Through his specialized lens, he watched the final human's thermal signature flicker and pulse, the deep orange hues of his core temperature spiking wildly as panic sent his heartbeat into a frantic, erratic staccato. The sniper exhaled a long, slow, controlled breath, his finger resting feather-light against the cold metal of the trigger.

"It's like shooting wolves trapped in a pen," he murmured coldly, and squeezed.

Alisa ran effortlessly through the deep snow, humming a cheerful, upbeat tune to herself as her cybernetic optics scanned the treacherous surroundings. Despite the thick, insulated pink earmuffs framing her face, her heightened mutant auditory senses picked up the distant, muffled thuds of suppressed gunfire echoing from the valley.

"Was the target area supposed to be this close to the southern border?" she muttered to herself, her fingers tightening around the grip of her compact submachine gun. "Meh, doesn't really matter to me."

She trudged onward, her light frame allowing her to navigate the deepening, powdery drifts with far more agility than any human soldier could manage. The arctic wind howled violently around her, lashing her pink tactical suit with sharp pellets of ice as the storm reached a crescendo. Visibility across the tundra had dropped to near zero, but she pressed forward without an ounce of hesitation.

Then—a sudden, violent atmospheric flicker cut through the whiteout. A monstrous, towering silhouette blurred past her peripheral vision at a supernatural velocity, gone as quickly as it had appeared.

The entity was moving far too fast for a standard biological eye to track, but Alisa recognized the distinct, heavy air-wake instantly. Her teeth clenched into a wide, excited grin as she broke into a full, aggressive sprint, kicking up sprays of loose snow as she chased the terrifying shadow directly through the heart of the storm.

Meanwhile, a secondary human reconnaissance squad was fleeing in terror a few miles away. Five of their elite comrades had already died in the blink of an eye, systematically torn apart by an unseen force. Something monstrous was actively hunting them through the dark.

The last surviving operator of the unit gasped into his commlink, his legs pumping furiously as the biting cold gnawed through the seams.

"This is Group B! We are under active attack! Unknown biological assailant—the whole damn squad is down! The intelligence briefs never mentioned a threat of this caliber! Repeat, we are initiating a full retreat—"

An abrupt, dead silence hung over the line.

"Group A, Group C, Group D—any station, respond to me!" he screamed, his voice cracking with hysteria.

A vast, oppressive shadow suddenly loomed directly behind him, blocking out the faint light of the aurora borealis. Slowly, instinctively, the operator turned his head around—and froze entirely, his lungs seizing in his chest.

An imposing, seven-foot-tall figure hovered effortlessly against the full fury of the blizzard. Colossal black wings spread wide from his shoulders, catching the wind, while a long, reptilian tail lashed the frozen air behind him with the force of a whip. His skin was a deep, glacial blue, contrasted sharply by a mane of wild white hair, and his face was framed by twin black horns tipped in an unnatural, glowing ice-blue hue. In his right hand, he gripped an enormous, eight-foot-long executioner's cleaver, the heavy steel reflecting the dull red glow of the human's tactical gear.

Before the operative could even open his mouth to scream, the winged being dove from the sky. The snow erupted in a violent cascade as the monster seized the operator directly by the face, his clawed fingers crushing the visor before slamming the man's head violently down into the hard, blue glacial ice below. Thick blood immediately began to pool in the white indentation.

"Who sent your units here?" The voice wasn't a shout; it was a low, resonant rumble that carried a temperature colder than the arctic storm itself.

The operator squirmed wildly beneath the immense weight, his boots kicking and his fists punching in an exercise of pure, wild desperation against the creature's armored chest. He tried to scream for mercy, but the being's hand clamped down with a crushing pressure, forcing him to gasp frantically for even a sliver of air.

"Mph—we were just... we were a recon team—" the human managed to choke out, spitting blood and saliva as he struggled against the iron grip of the creature's claws. "We were only sent to investigate the anomalies... nothing more, I swear to God—"

The winged figure tilted his head at a sharp angle, his glacial blue eyes narrowing into thin, predatory slits. His grip tightened fractionally—just enough to make the human's skull creak ominously under the strain. "Investigate what, human?" The words slithered out of his maw, carrying a lethal finality.

The operator’s combat boots scraped uselessly against the sheet ice as he wheezed, "R-rumors! Just—ghhk—reports of unauthorized mutant activity in the sector! Command wanted confirmation—" His words dissolved into a wet, desperate gurgle as the creature’s sharp claws pricked deep into his jugular vein.

A sudden pink shadow darted through the swirling snow, Alisa skidding to a sharp, sprayed halt a few feet away, her submachine gun already leveled out of pure habit. "General Kevin!" she chirped loudly, her bright voice was unfazed by the gore-streaked snow and the dying man on the ground. "I have an official message direct from Her Highness!"

The winged figure didn’t bother to turn his head to look at her. The heavy edge of his large cleaver dripped thick, crimson droplets onto the operator’s twitching corpse. "Speak."

Alisa tapped the side of her pink earmuffs with a grin. "Morse code transmission only, General. High-priority clearance."

Kevin’s reptilian tail lashed against the ice with a sharp crack. With an effortless flick of his wrist, he sent his cleaver flying through the air, the heavy blade embedding itself deep into a solid glacial wall nearby with a resonant thwack. He reached down and seized the human operator’s surviving tactical commlink, flexing his clawed fingers and crushing the high-tech military hardware into a fine gray powder. "Proceed with the playback."

Alisa’s fingers immediately began to dance in an intricate, rapid pattern against the side of her thigh, her gloves creating a series of sharp, rhythmic click-clack sounds that were easily lost to the howling wind to any normal ear. Kevin’s pupils contracted to the size of pinpricks, his advanced senses easily parsing the tactile code within milliseconds.

"Hmph," he rumbled, his chest expanding as he wrenched his blade free from the ice wall with a single tug. "Go back and tell Katyusha to triple the guard rotations along the southern patrol vectors immediately." His black wings flexed, scattering a layer of fresh frost into the air. "And Alisa?"

Alisa tilted her head to the side, her pink earmuffs bobbing with the movement. "Yes, General?"

Kevin's breath crystallized midair into a dense cloud as he leaned his large frame down toward her, his jagged black horns casting long, terrifying shadows across her small face. "Tell the General from the Intelligence Department that I have personally intercepted six separate human spy squads trying to slip through this perimeter within this week alone. Their insertion attempts are escalating at an alarming rate." His clawed fingers flexed tightly around the wrapped hilt of his cleaver. "It seems that there are some factions within the higher tiers of their human governance who are beginning to realize that the myths of our Empire are turning into an undeniable reality..."

"Does that mean the big war is going to happen anytime soon?" Alisa chirped, tilting her head with a curious expression as she watched General Kevin casually wipe the thick human gore from his blade using a handful of fresh, clean snow.

At their feet, the captured human operator let out a low, agonizing groan, his consciousness flickering back into reality after the brutal impact of being slammed into the glacier floor.

"Yes," Kevin rumbled, his breath curling like dark smoke between his exposed fangs. He nudged the operator's limp, broken form with the tip of his clawed combat boot. "It seems their command structure has slowly started planting false operational intelligence among their own lower ranks, deliberately sending these expendable scout units out here under the guise of investigating mere rumors." His glacial eyes gleamed with a malicious light, his slit-like pupils adjusting to the blizzard's bite. "Regardless of their strategic intent—it remains highly beneficial for us. It makes the process of interrogation... significantly easier."

Alisa tilted her head further, her earmuffs bobbing as she studied the groaning, shivering human soldier. "Should I go ahead and bring this one back to the detention center with the others?" she chirped, wasting no time as she grabbed the heavy-set operator by the reinforced collar of his tactical harness. The thick material of his thermal suit crinkled loudly under her surprisingly immense grip, loose snow sloughing off his trembling body as she hoisted him up.

Kevin's long tail lashed the ice once more—a sharp, decisive movement that left a gouge in the glacier. "Lock this human away in the subterranean blocks." With a casual flick of his wrist, he tossed the broken man back into a deep snowbank, where the body landed with a heavy, wet thud. The General turned his back to the scene, his black wings mantling outward against the storm like a living, unyielding fortress of flesh and bone. "We do not bring warmth or comfort to humans who fear us. Get him down to the cells, and ensure our handlers make him speak for as long as he has breath left in his lungs."

Alisa giggled softly, adjusting her grip on the operator's tactical utility belt. "Got it, General!"

She began dragging the heavy human soldier backward through the deep powder without showing an ounce of physical strain, his heavy boots carving twin, deep furrows through the snow as he moaned incoherently into the dark. "Come on, big guy, there we go. Keep those legs moving."

"Continue your designated operations here," General Kevin commanded, his voice a low, vibrating growl that resonated through the foundations of the ice valley. His wings flexed out to their full span, scattering a cloud of frozen crystals into the howling gale. "The High Empress summons me, and I will not keep the council waiting."

Alisa snapped a perfect, crisp military salute mid-drag, her free hand resting sharply against her temple even as the operator's limp body slid another foot through the freezing drifts. "For the glory of the Armadora Empire!" she chirped, her bright voice cutting cleanly through the blizzard's roar.

Kevin didn't waste another single glance at the human or the field of slaughter behind him. His black wings snapped open with a sound like a splitting glacier, and with one powerful, explosive downstroke, he launched himself directly into the teeth of the storm. The snow swirled violently in a vortex around his ascent, the full fury of the arctic blizzard parting cleanly around his rising form as though the weather itself was unwilling to touch him.

Alisa watched his silhouette vanish into the upper white void within seconds, then turned her gaze back down to her bleeding captive with a cheery, melodic hum. She adjusted her grip on his gear, hoisting him a bit higher off the ground as she turned back toward the hidden entrance markers.

"Don't you worry about a thing, mister," she chirped brightly, patting his bloodied, frostbitten cheek with a gloved hand. "You're going to love our interrogation rooms down below. They have such cozy chairs waiting for you."
