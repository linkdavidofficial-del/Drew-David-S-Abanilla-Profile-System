Chapter 12: The Council of Eleven

The heavy, undulating mass of the emerald-scaled titan carved its way through the lower arterial roads of the subterranean metropolis, her presence drawing a collective, breathless hush from the gathered crowds. Civilians clustered along the edges of the multi-tiered walkways, their colorful garments casting flickering reflections against her passing form. Armored perimeter patrols stood frozen at attention, their rifles pressed tightly against their breastplates as the shadow of her upper torso swept over them. Vespera paid them no heed, her focus locked on the structural silhouette dominating the center of the cavern.

She glided toward the primary perimeter, where the sheer scale of the architecture began to dwarf even the high-rise apartment complexes of the outer sectors. Rising from the bedrock was the front metal gate, a monumental barrier forged from thick plates of reinforced tungsten alloy and cross-braced with heavy structural steel. Ten specialized security guards stood stationed in a flawless, linear formation across the approach, their heavy ballistic exosuits gleaming under the high-intensity yellow light grid above.

The moment the general’s towering form neared the perimeter markings, the lead guard raised a gauntleted hand, and all ten soldiers snapped into a synchronized, rigid presentation. "General, ma'am!" their voices echoed in unison through the cavernous approach.

Without a word of command, the soldiers split their formation, executing precise side-steps to line the opposing sides of the threshold. Behind them, the massive gate emitted a deep, mechanical whirr that vibrated through the stone floor, followed by the heavy, clanking of internal hydraulic counterweights lifting the thousands of tons of metal. A thick cloud of pressurized steam hissed from the release valves as the doors parted, breaking the seal to the inner citadel.

Vespera slithered forward, her vast lower half gliding over the smooth threshold with a soft, scraping hiss. As she entered the massive fortress, the chaotic noise of the harbor city died away, replaced by the sterile, echoing emptiness of the high-command sector. The interior hallways were gargantuan, designed specifically to accommodate the movement of the empire’s largest military units. The walls of brushed metal rose hundreds of feet toward vaulted ceilings, lined with vertical strips of pale white luminescent casing that cast a cold, clinical glare over her green-tinted skin. Her movements carried her deeper into the structural labyrinth, past secure communication hubs and silent specialized checkpoints, until she finally reached the entrance of the primary council chamber.

The double doors of the room were vast, constructed from dark, sound-absorbent composite material, currently sitting slightly ajar to reveal a sliver of warm light from within. Vespera reached out with a webbed hand, her long claws lightly catching the rim of the reinforced structure as she pushed it open just wide enough to slip her bulk through the gap.

She stepped into the Council room — built, like everything else in the capital, to accommodate the Empire's largest. Overhead, massive crystal chandeliers hung from structural iron chains, their thousands of low-voltage elements glowing like trapped constellations. At the center of the polished floor sat a large, heavy round table crafted from dark, treated hardwoods and ringed by custom-engineered seating arrangements tailored to the diverse physiology of the high command. Vespera navigated her way toward her designated station, where a large, custom-tufted velvet pillow lay waiting. With practiced, fluid grace, she coiled her vast, emerald-scaled lower serpent half upon the plush fabric, settling her weight into the cushions. She planted the base of her legendary trident firmly into the floor slot beside her, the prongs gleaming under the chandeliers, and scanned the room.

The immediate vicinity was largely vacant, save for the few council members who had already assumed their positions around the curvature of the table.

Seated to her far right was a woman who presented a striking, chaotic contrast to the sterile order of the citadel. Her overall appearance was a visual mess of industrial design and field grime. Long strands of wild brown hair dangled untamed across her shoulders, unbrushed and partially pinned back by mechanical clips. She wore a heavily customized, trimmed leather top crisscrossed with utility belts, tactical harness straps, and a series of specialized holsters containing glass vials filled with dark, chemically reactive fluids that had left soot stains across the fabric. A tight, reinforced leather corset snapped around her narrow waist, further secured by heavy brass buckles, while her lower uniform consisted of tight, mud-flecked brown pants tucked into knee-long boots of a deep mahogany hue, supported by thin, heightened steel heels.

The eccentric figure reached up, using two grease-stained fingers to adjust the circular frame of her thick round glasses as she peered across the table. "Oh, hey! I honestly thought you wouldn't notice me from all the way up there. I suppose the angle required for you to look down at us common folk must be getting less steep by the day."

Vespera squinted her sapphire eyes, her nostrils flaring as she caught the heavy atmospheric currents coming from the right side of the chamber. "I can smell the distinct stench of black powder, liquid sulfur, and chemical explosions radiating off your uniform from across the table."

The woman chuckled, a sharp, manic grin spreading across her face as she leaned back in her high-backed chair. "That isn't a stench, my aquatic friend. That is the literal smell of progress, the aroma of cutting-edge technology being forged in the lower research bays."

"Friends and military colleagues are two very different things, Vill-8," Vespera huffed, her voice a low rumble that caused the crystal elements of the chandelier above her to vibrate slightly.

The manic inventor let out a loud laugh, resting her forearms against the polished surface of the table and clapping her leather-gloved hands together with enthusiastic energy. "Oh, come now, Your Highness, there is no need to be so incredibly serious before the protocols are even initiated. The official meeting hasn't started yet, and several of our counterparts are still tied up with various other administrative things across the sectors."

"Things?" Vespera repeated, her brow furrowing slightly as she adjusted her posture against her pillow. "Be specific."

"Oh, right, I forgot that you've been tucked away in your deep-sea slumber for nearly a month," the scientist said, a muffled chuckle bubbling underneath her breath as she tapped her fingers against the table. "You really should have checked the secure news feeds the moment you broke the surface. The entire domestic OMP-site was destroyed a week ago."

The giant serpent woman went rigid, her eyes locking onto the inventor in a cold, unblinking stare. "What did you say?"

"Oh, yes, it was a grand success," the woman in the glasses explained, leaning forward to emphasize the data. "Antares’s primary lieutenant led the offensive operation personally. They managed to break the outer containment grids, and roughly forty thousand or so new mutant assets were secured and transferred directly into the internal borders of the empire."

"Forty thousand mutants that are bound to be an unorganized mixture of untrained civilians and unpredictable psychotics?" Vespera scoffs, her hand tightening slightly around the shaft of her trident. "Do you have any practical conception of how many of those frontline subjects are already severely brain-damaged from human experimentation? The sheer logistical cost alone to mental health infrastructure and localized structural repairs to integrate them safely—"

"I understand the underlying sentiment, fish," a sharp, distinctively youthful feminine voice cut through the debate from the left side of the table.

Sitting in an elevated chair was a figure with the physical proportions of a small child, yet her demeanor carried the weight of a hardened battlefield commander. Her hair was a vibrant, untamed mane of orange flaming locks that seemed to ripple with internal heat, framing a face set in a stern expression. She wore an ornate ensemble of traditional Chinese-style embroidered silks mixed with heavy, slate-gray tactical armor plates. Her forearms were encased in, oversized mechanical gauntlets that looked capable of crushing structural steel, which she kept crossed firmly over her small chest. The most commanding detail, though, were the immense avian wings protruding from her back—their feathers displaying a magnificent gradient hue transitioning from deep crimson to brilliant orange, the surrounding air shimmering slightly from the ambient temperature of her body heat.

"They are currently being locked in the secure sectors of the Omega Mutant Penitentiary for processing," the winged commander continued, a faint, knowing smirk playing on her lips. "But let us not overlook the fact that the vast majority of them are former urban gang members, or functional civilians who were forcibly taken from their homes by human raiders. Some of them are highly capable former pit fighters. I can assure you they are remarkably useful assets. In fact, they have already proven their utility; a significant portion of them have already been transferred directly into my aerial forces."

Vespera raised a single green brow, her gaze shifting to the feathered operative. "So you have already managed to secure a fresh batch of unseasoned rookies for your ranks."

The brown-haired inventor to the right Chimed back in, adjusting her glasses once more. "She managed to secure a large portion of them the exact moment the transports arrived right after the destruction of the OMP-Site. Right now, she has roughly eleven thousand of them integrated into her aerial flight commands."

"That is an absurdly large number of winged-type mutants to process simultaneously," Vespera muttered.

"Indeed it is," the winged commander replied with an elegant, careless flutter of her massive crimson wings, the movement sending a brief wave of warm air across the table. She reached down with a gauntleted hand, delicately lifting a porcelain cup of hot tea to take a slow, deliberate sip. "They are all raw rookies, of course. Some of them don't even possess a basic understanding of their own kinetic power sets or the biological mechanics required to maintain stable flight at high altitudes. It is truly tragic to watch them drop during drills." She lazily pointed the rim of her tea cup toward the giant mermaid. "But you shouldn't look so smug, fish. You received a significant portion of the spoils yourself. In fact, every single branch within the imperial military—the ground divisions, the navy, and the air forces—all received an equal portion of the new assets."

Vespera’s eyes narrowed as she absorbed the information. "Then how many new naval units were officially assigned to my command?"

"You should probably ask your own regional lieutenants for those specific figures," the inventor stated, leaning back and resting her boots against the lower rim of the table. "We already finalized the primary distribution conversations while you were down in your deep sleep cycle."

The serpent titan’s gaze darkened. "Who exactly authorized the immediate integration of unverified forces into our active military ranks without council consensus?"

"Antares’s executive lieutenant, of course," the brown-haired scientist replied smoothly.

Vespera let out a long, heavy sigh, her expression twisting into a subtle, annoyed roll of her eyes as she leaned her upper body back against the velvet pillow. "That zealous, red-eyed lunatic?"

"Yes, that exact Red Ash," the inventor confirmed, nodding her head with a wry smile. "Seriously, Vespera, you truly need to keep yourself better updated with the current times."

"Hmph," Vespera rumbled, a brief silence settling over the table before she shifted her weight, the scales of her tail shifting with a dry rustle. "When do we officially start this session?"

"You're asking that now, while we are all sitting here actively waiting for the executive administrators?" the orange-haired aerial commander said, raising a brow in clear irritation. "If I hadn't been explicitly summoned to this chamber, I would still be busy conducting combat training at my private dojo, hammering some discipline into those eleven thousand rookies. Unlike a certain giant fish right here who sleeps for almost an entire month at a time."

"Watch your tongue, bird brain," Vespera growled, her sapphire eyes flashing with a dangerous, predatory sharpness as she leveled her gaze at the smaller woman.

The aerial commander’s eyes immediately flared with a bright, internal orange glow, the feathers along her wings rustling as the temperature in her immediate vicinity spiked. "Or what, exactly?"

The inventor let out a low chuckle, raising her hands in a mock gesture of peace. "Enough, enough, you two. Vespera, you must still be incredibly annoyed by whatever crude wake-up call the frontline units used to pull you out of the ice." She then turned her gaze toward the winged commander. "And you certainly don't want both of your bickering to become an active hindrance to the High Empress when she arrives."

The two military powerhouses exchanged a long, tense gaze across the hardwood table, the air between them thick with hostility, before slowly leaning back into their respective seats.

"Apologies," the serpent titan spoke, her voice dropping back into a controlled, formal register.

"We were merely engaging in a small bit of pre-meeting teasing here," the orange-haired commander muttered, adjusting her heavy gauntlets as the glow in her eyes faded.

The heavy sound of the main double doors opening resonated through the chamber, drawing everyone's attention toward the threshold. A woman stepped into the room with an air of absolute poise, her movements were fluid as she adjusted her immaculate gloves. She held a sleek, expensive umbrella in her right hand, humming a sharp, melodic tune that matched the confident click of her heels against the floorboards. Her piercing emerald eyes swept across the council table, taking in the three gathered figures.

"Ah, so the three of you are our early birds for today's session?" she noted, gliding toward her designated seat at the head section of the table. "Or perhaps I should say, we have an early fish, an early bird, and an early woman?"

Vespera simply let out a low huff, turning her face slightly toward the window slots.

"We are just waiting for the rest of the cabinet and having a simple, casual chat," the orange-haired commander explained, crossing her arms once more.

The emerald-eyed administrator paused as she reached her chair, her gaze raking over the surrounding air with a critical expression. "I highly doubt a simple, casual chat includes a thick layer of chemical smoke and the overwhelming smell of raw seaweed."

Behind her, a large, the lizard lieutenant attempted to follow her into the secure perimeter of the room. The administrator didn't even turn around; she simply set her umbrella down against the edge of the table and waved her left hand dismissively over her shoulder. "No, no. You will wait outside the main corridor, lieutenant. Otherwise, you will find yourself in incredible structural trouble with the security detachment."

The reptilian officer stopped instantly, bowing her head in deep respect. "As you wish, ma'am. Then I shall be heading back outside to secure the perimeter." She slowly turned her around, walking with measured, heavy steps back into the hallway as the doors began to slide shut behind her.

The administrator turned back to face the round table, clapping her hands together with a crisp, bright sound. "Now then, since we have a few moments before the Empress enters, how is everyone currently doing?"

"I punched a rookie straight in the jaw this morning," the winged commander announced flatly.

"Good for you," the administrator replied, unbothered as she pulled out a secure digital tablet.

"He explicitly stated I lacked the stature to command armies," the petite woman said, her feathered wings twitching with residual fury.

The administrator barely glanced up from their datapad. "Mmm. Familiar complaint."

"It's always the same—they dismiss me based on appearances alone," she hissed, jaw tight.

"Well, you do look exactly like one, dear," the administrator noted without looking up.

"So I punched him square in his nuts and then forced him to execute one hundred consecutive push-ups in full combat gear on the hot tarmac."

"Hmm, yes, sounds like a typical Tuesday in the aerial corps," the administrator sighed smoothly. She then shifted her emerald eyes toward the massive form coiled on the velvet pillow. "And how are you doing then, Vespera?"

"I slept," the giant serpent woman said simply, her voice flat. "Then someone rudely woke me up with an acoustic beacon."

"Oh my, a truly terrible wake-up call for a titan," the administrator offered with a faint, polite smile.

"There is no need to go any further into the details of the deployment," Vespera muttered, closing her eyes for a brief moment.

The administrator hummed softly, and before the silence could stretch, the brown-haired inventor leaned forward, tapping her grease-stained fingers against the wood. "Did you happen to receive my encrypted data message regarding the resource allocations?"

"I did," the administrator confirmed, her eyes scanning the tablet interface. "It was a lavish request for additional shipments of alloy iron, copper, and tungsten... My primary question is, why do you require such an absurdly large amount of tungsten?"

"Because I absolutely need them for my current projects," the scientist stated simply, as if it were obvious.

"Yes, but why?" the administrator pressed, her tone turning sharp and analytical. "I am certainly not throwing the department's reserve cash around just for more tungsten shipments without a clear operational justification."

"We have more than enough money in the treasury," the inventor argued, adjusting her round glasses.

"But why do you need it?"

"To construct more weapons, obviously."

"Who could possibly need more weapons at this current stage of our domestic security?"

"I am currently in the middle of manufacturing a brand-new tank prototype," the scientist revealed, her manic smile returning. "One designed with six independent firing barrels."

The administrator paused, looking up from her screen with an expression of profound disbelief. "Dear, that is practically a war crime by our current regulations. We already possess the heavy armored models with triple barrels."

"I know, but I already secured official administrative permission for the project," the inventor countered triumphantly.

"From whom?"

"From Antares."

"We already possess more than enough firepower to level the surface continents twice over," the administrator sighed, rubbing her temples.

"Yes, but just imagine the sheer kinetic output!"

"Please stop making unauthorized weapons inside that chaotic lab of yours before you blow up a section of the city," the administrator warned, closing the file.

The orange-haired aerial commander spoke up next, leaning her chin on her gauntleted hand. "I was highly appreciative of the thirty million in cash that arrived in our department accounts this morning, by the way."

"You're very welcome, but I was going to give it to you anyway as part of the department's regularly scheduled quarterly budgets," the administrator replied, tapping the screen to bring up a new logistical map. "In fact, thank you, Ling, for providing that critical intelligence regarding the human scout movements near the coast of Japan. We had heard various unverified rumors, but thankfully your aerial reconnaissance units were able to successfully locate a hidden human base that is currently running advanced technical projects."

"You're welcome," the winged commander, now identified as Ling, replied lazily. "Does that mean I can finally get that fire-proof velvet couch I requested for my quarters?"

"Yes, I already had someone send the delivery order directly to your residential wing this morning," the administrator confirmed.

Vespera’s eyes snapped open at the mention of the eastern coordinates. "There are active human populations left in Japan?"

The administrator turned her head slightly. "Dear, please be specific with your geopolitical queries."

"Is there a new faction of enemies developing on the outer islands?" the giant general demanded, her posture straight.

"If you are specifically talking about the Japanese territory's new technical projects, then yes," the administrator explained, zooming in on a coastal grid display. "In fact, the facility is located right on the shoreline. If you have some spare time after this council meeting concludes, you should go down and check the sector personally. But please exercise extreme caution; we don't know exactly what type of machinery they might be manufacturing out there."

Ling let out a dry chuckle from behind her tea cup. "It's probably giant mechas."

"Ling, please be serious," the administrator chided gently.

"It's Japan," the winged general countered with a dismissive shrug. "If their engineers think it looks impressive, they'll find a way to make it reality—regardless of practicality."

"Fair point, but completely irrelevant to our current tactical discussion," the administrator noted.

Before the argument could develop, the heavy double doors of the chamber slid open once more. A stunning woman stepped into the room with a soft, perfectly practiced smile, her long hair cascading elegantly down her back. Walking immediately beside her was a terrifyingly bulky, mechanized entity clad in a pristine, tailored military uniform. The machine possessed a massive, box-like monitor for a head, its dark glass screen featuring a single, sharp red dot that blinked continuously as its internal sensors scanned the room.

The elegant woman offered a gentle wave to the gathered council members. "Hello~"

"Hi," Ling replied lazily.

"We brought drinks along from the corridor," the woman with the soft smile announced, gesturing toward the entrance.

Vespera blinked her sapphire eyes, looking confusedly at the table layout. "We don't have a designated beverage station anywhere near this secure level of the citadel."

"We actually had a brand-new beverage dispenser installed nearby," the woman explained, smoothing down the front of her dress as she glided toward her chair.

"Since when?" the giant serpent woman demanded.

"Since exactly two days and two weeks ago, while you were still sleeping beneath the glacier," the newcomer clarified, letting out a soft, melodic chuckle as she took her seat. "My, the chamber sounds incredibly lively today, doesn't it?"

The bulky machine in the military uniform stepped forward, its heavy metallic frame leaning slightly over the back of an reinforced iron chair. "Indeed," the machine’s deep, synthesized voice resonated through the room, a cold, calculated tone that carried the weight of an imperial strategist. "The sound of your debate could be heard clearly through the reinforced structure of the outer door."

"We are all just incredibly bored because we are waiting for the exact time for the high command to start the session," Ling complained, reaching into her tactical gear jacket and pulling out a heavy, lacquered wooden box. She slid the lid open to reveal rows of carved tiles. "I brought a set of mahjong."

Vespera let out a heavy, dismissive breath from her chest. "Bird brain, none of us in this room know how to play that surface game."

"Well, I am entirely bored out of my mind, so come on, fish, let's play a round. I will teach you the basic rules as we go," Ling insisted, sliding a row of tiles across the dark wood of the table.

The towering, metallic machine leaned its monitor head closer to the center grid. "I possess full historical data and comprehensive tactical knowledge of the game of mahjong," the tactical machine stated, its internal cooling fans whirring softly. "I shall participate in this match."

"Oh, that is completely unfair, Mavankill," the administrator and the elegant woman giggled in unison, their voices blending together as they watched the machine adjust its digital interface.

The administrator gestured with her pen toward the glowing red dot on the screen. "You are a literal high-level military tactician, Mavankill. You will make the poor Phoenix cry within the first five rounds of play."

"Don't make me come over there and flip that table," Ling warned, lazily pointing a clawed finger toward the machine while remaining firmly melted into her chair. "I am perfectly comfortable right here in my seat, and I don't want to move a single inch before the Empress arrives."

The elegant woman, ignoring the impending game, turned her attention to the head of the table. "By the way, Navia, I noticed you got a brand-new pair of earrings."

The administrator, now addressed as Navia, immediately smiled, tilting her head to flaunt the glittering jewelry as she gestured gracefully with the back of her hand. "I know, right? They are full twenty-four-karat gold, and the surfaces are intricately sprinkled with micro-diamonds along the lower rim."

"Ohh, they are stunning," the elegant woman cooed, leaning closer to admire the craftsmanship. "Do you happen to have anything similar to that style in your private collection?"

Navia reached down into her designer bag, pulling out a velvet display roll containing several shimmering strands and pairs of high-end earrings. "Here, check these out. I managed to secure this specific pair during our last covert operation in London; this one over here features genuine natural pearls sourced directly from Saudi Arabia; this set is from a high-end designer in Korea; and then this final one is from one of the top global brands specializing in matching pearls like this and that."

"Hmm, these are truly magnificent," the woman with the soft smile murmured, her eyes tracing the reflection of the diamonds. "They could be incredibly useful for my next public concert appearance."

"Speaking of which, how did your last major concert tour conclude, Eden?" Navia asked, leaning back and resting her chin in her hand.

The pop idol, now identified as Eden, chuckled softly, her expression remaining serene and unbothered. "It was great, dear. The hospitality staff provided excellent drinks, and the human audiences behaved exactly as they usually do—laughing, cheering, and just having a good time in the arenas." She paused, her eyes glinting with a cold, superior amusement under the chandelier lights. "Though, of course, they don't possess a single clue as to what is truly happening behind the scenes of this world, right?"

"Absolutely," Navia agreed smoothly, her emerald eyes reflecting the cold light of her tablet.Navia’s fingers tapped rhythmically against the glass screen of her digital tablet, her emerald eyes reflecting the shifting rows of strategic data. Across the table, the sharp clicking of lacquered wooden tiles echoed through the silent chamber. Mavankill, devoid of organic emotion, methodically tapped a heavy steel finger against the polished wood before laying down a final, flawless arrangement of pieces.

Ling threw her hands up, letting out a loud, frustrated sigh that caused her crimson and orange feathers to ruffle aggressively. "Baah! This is completely no fun! Can you just stop winning and choose to lose for once in your life?"

The tactician machine's monitor-head remained locked onto the board, the singular red dot on the glass screen blinking flatly. "But doing so would fundamentally defeat the competitive purpose of the game."

Ling clicked her tongue, her fingers drumming impatiently against the table before she swept the tiles into a haphazard mound. "Spare me the algorithmic posturing," she muttered, rebuilding her wall with deliberate, jerking motions. "Round two—and disable whatever predictive subroutines you've got humming in that chrome skull of yours."

Mavankill's optics flickered. "My decision-making protocols do not function in that—"

"I am not losing to a toaster again!" Ling interrupted, leaning over the table. "If you keep winning every single round, I might just forget council decorum and punch that annoying flat face of yours right off your chassis!"

The massive tactician simply stared back through his dark screen, entirely unbothered by the aerial general's fiery temperament. Towering high above the rest of the table, Vespera quietly observed the bickering duo. Her sapphire eyes darted left and right, tracking the movement of the tiny tiles, before she settled back into a silent, imposing stare.

Suddenly, the heavy, reinforced tracking mechanisms of the main tower doors engaged, sliding open with a resonant mechanical hiss. The movement drew the immediate attention of the room. Navia offered a swift side glance over the rim of her tablet. Eden let out a soft hum, turning her head with fluid elegance, while Vespera subtly shifted her massive gaze toward the threshold.

Stepping through the grand frame, General Kevin finally arrived. His pitch-black wings were cloaked tightly around his armored shoulders like a dark shroud, his cold, piercing gaze sweeping across the assembled high command as he marched into the light.

"You," Vespera rumbled, her voice a low, heavy vibration that rattled the porcelain teacups on the table.

Kevin paused his advance, his dark wings shifting slightly. "Hm?"

"Your women," the giant serpent titan stated flatly.

A brief pause hung in the room before Kevin replied, his tone perfectly level. "I assume you are referring to my frontline battalion captains."

"They are insufferably loud," Vespera murmured, her brow knitting in irritation.

"Had they been quiet, you'd still be hibernating beneath the continental shelf," Kevin replied, his tone effortlessly smooth.

"Their methods were... abrasive." Vespera narrowed her sapphire eyes.

Kevin adjusted his stance, voice still honeyed but firm. "The council's schedule takes precedence. The High Empress tolerates neither tardiness nor excuses."

A heavy silence settled over the table as the towering woman's sapphire eyes narrowed to slits. "You soldiers will never wake me like that again."

Kevin strode toward his seat, dragging back the reinforced chair with a metallic scrape. "No other option. You were buried too deep in the trench."

"You could have easily deployed my own forces to deliver the administrative summon," Vespera countered, her emerald scales shifting against the plush velvet pillow. "My... naval units, at the very least."

"Your units would freeze at the thought of closing the gap," Kevin countered, lowering himself into the chair while maintaining an unblinking stare. "The odds were too high—you might've registered them as seismic ghosts and devoured your own troops."

Vespera scoffed, fingers tightening around her trident's shaft. "What are you implying?"

"I did not mean that as an insult," Kevin said, straightening his uniform with deliberate calm.

She drew breath to snap back, but Mavankill's voice sliced through first.

"Technically correct," the machine intoned. In one fluid motion, his metal hand overturned his tiles—another flawless mahjong victory. Ling squawked, outraged. Unfazed, Mavankill's crimson optic locked onto Vespera. "Records show repeated incidents of... digestive mishaps within your territory. And you have hyper-acoustic hearing—you can detect surface vibrations, even from the seabed."

Vespera let out a frustrated breath, rolling her sapphire eyes as she leaned her upper body back against the velvet cushions, entirely done with the conversation.

Ling and Mavankill engaged in one final, rapid-fire round, the lacquered wooden tiles clacking aggressively against the hardwood table. With a swift, calculating slide of his cold, mechanical fingers, the tactician halted his movements entirely.

"It appears I have secured victory once more," his flat voice intoned.

He tilted his tiles forward, revealing a statistically improbable, flawless arrangement: Picking the Moon from the Bottom of the Sea.

Ling’s jaw dropped in disbelief, her orange-tipped wings twitching violently. "That is impossible... You managed to collect every single one of the One of Circles?"

"Mathematically speaking," Mavankill began, raising a rigid metal hand in a precise gesture, "the tile arrangement follows a predictable pattern once you account for the randomization variables. Memorize the subtle flaws in each piece, and winning ceases to be probabilistic—it becomes inevitable. Pure pattern recognition."

Ling’s mouth opened and closed in a silent cycle of mounting fury. Her face flushed a brilliant, angry crimson. She slammed her gauntlets onto the table and surged to her feet. "Oho, that is it, you oversized, over-glorified tin can—"

She wound back her fist, aiming directly for his optical display.

Before her blow could connect, a thunderous, heavy thud reverberated through the structural foundations of the room as the monolithic council doors slammed open. The sudden shockwave caused Navia and Eden to freeze instantly, their casual conversation halting mid-sentence. Kevin shifted his cold gaze sideways, his black wings tightening defensively around his torso. At the center of the table, Mavankill’s glowing red dot shrank by a mere three pixels—a subtle, automated contraction of his optical sensors that barely registered to the organic eye, yet betrayed his internal system alert.

Ling froze entirely mid-swing, her fist suspended inches from the machine’s face. Vespera slowly rotated her massive head, her aquatic senses instantly registering a crushing, suffocating wave of thermal energy. A familiar, unbearable heat began to bleed into the atmosphere, clashing violently with the sub-zero aura radiating off Kevin’s massive, frozen frame.

General Antares marched into the chamber with deliberate, crushing footsteps. The moment his monolithic, obsidian-like frame crossed the threshold, the room's automated climate control systems roared to life at maximum capacity, the vents whistling aggressively as they struggled to counter the immense, radiating heat his body emitted. Dressed in a pristine imperial military uniform beneath a sweeping black combat coat, the volcanic titan let his blazing orange, star-like gaze sweep slowly across the length of the table.

The sudden, absolute silence in the room was deafening. It was more than enough to shatter Ling's aggressive resolve; she rapidly scooped the entire mahjong set off the table, shoving the lacquered box out of sight beneath the edge of the hardwood. Navia smoothly and seamlessly slid her velvet rolls of jewelry back into her secure bag. Eden maintained her soft, serene smile, remaining utterly fabulous and unperturbed by the thermal shift. Mavankill returned to a completely static, unbothered posture, his internal cooling fans compensating for the temperature spike. Vespera and Kevin maintained their stoic expressions, though their postures tightened into a dead-serious military alignment.

Antares offered no spoken greeting, simply staring at the gathered council for several agonizing seconds before moving heavily toward the far end of the round table to assume his seat.

Breaking the heavy tension, another figure stepped gracefully through the open doorway. The familiar pink-haired diplomat offered a cheerful wave of her gloved hand to the room.

"Hello, everyone," Alyxia chimed, her voice carrying a light, melodic lilt as she hummed a soft tune behind her elegant smile. "My, what an impressive turnout already. And here I thought I would be the first one to arrive early."

Vespera’s massive sapphire eyes locked onto the diplomat, her gaze lingering with a profound, unblinking intensity as memories of the gray shoreline and the gentle hand flashed behind her eyelids. Alyxia felt the weight of the titan's attention and turned her head slightly.

"Hm? Oh, good morning, Vespy."

Vespera let out a low, non-committal vibration from her throat. "Hm."

Navia broke the momentary focus, offering a smooth, welcoming smile to the newcomer. "Ah, my, my, Alyxia dear, you are not late at all. You have arrived precisely on time."
