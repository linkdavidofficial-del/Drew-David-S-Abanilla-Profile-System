Chapter 10: A House Divided

The Pentagon's encrypted channels had remained stubbornly silent for four days following the catastrophic events at the OMP-Site. No reports, no updates, no confirmation of anything—just an unnerving void where routine communications should have been flowing. The initial dispatch attempting to convene an emergency meeting had vanished into that same silence, the envoy sent to deliver it simply... gone without a trace.

On the fifth day, President Johnson Strongarm made the decision to call a meeting. On paper, it was routine. A standard briefing to review the state of affairs, nothing that would raise eyebrows or trigger alarm among the watchful eyes that perpetually monitored the White House's pulse.

But the Situation Room at 8:00 AM was anything but routine. The air itself seemed thick with unspoken anxieties as secretaries and aides muttered in hushed tones, their eyes darting toward the door with each passing moment. High-ranking officials who had been in the inner circle for decades sat in their designated chairs, some fidgeting with pens, others staring blankly at the polished mahogany table as if the answers might materialize from its grain. The low murmur of conversation ebbed and flowed like a restless tide, punctuated by the occasional sharp laugh that died almost as quickly as it began.

Then, slowly, the heavy oak door swung open. Two Secret Service agents entered first, their eyes sweeping the room with practiced vigilance before they stepped to either side, flanking the doorway. President Johnson Strongarm strode through the gap they created, his signature blonde hair catching the harsh fluorescent light, his muscular build evident even beneath the perfectly tailored suit. He moved with the calm, deliberate confidence of a man who had weathered countless storms, yet there was something in the set of his jaw that suggested this particular morning weighed heavily on him.

He took his seat at the center of the table, the leather creaking softly as he settled into position. For a long moment, he simply looked at each face in turn, assessing, measuring. Then he cleared his throat—a sharp, commanding sound that instantly silenced the lingering whispers.

"Right then," he began, his voice carrying the gravelly authority that had defined his presidency. He leaned forward slightly, resting his forearms on the polished surface. "Let's begin. How is everything with the state?" His gaze settled on Vice President Harrison, who sat to his immediate right. "Any news on the current situation?"

The Vice President met his eyes, then bowed her head briefly in acknowledgment. "Well, sir, we've received concerning reports from multiple fronts. Mutant-related incidents are spiking globally. Massive gang breakouts in Moscow, a school shooting that devolved into a massacre of humans killing mutants—and one particularly disturbing case where an elderly human woman was shot by a mutant in broad daylight during a heated argument. We have the perpetrator in custody, but the damage to public sentiment is... significant."

"Additionally," the Secretary of State interjected, adjusting his glasses as he consulted a tablet, "there are reports of small groups—three or four individuals—targeting human schools in coordinated attacks. The pattern suggests organization, not random violence."

President Strongarm's jaw tightened almost imperceptibly. "Is there any word on whether these criminals are being transferred to the OMP-Site?"

"Yes, Mr. President," the Secretary of Defense responded, his voice flat and professional. "We received confirmation from the Warden himself that the most recent batch of detainees was processed and secured. Standard protocol, nothing out of the ordinary."

"Good." The President allowed himself a small nod. "Then that means we should be receiving regular reports from them. Has there been any communication since?"

The Secretary of Defense hesitated—a fraction of a second too long. "We did receive a routine communication yesterday, sir. Minimal funding request, citing proper disciplinary actions and unusually cooperative prisoner behavior. Nothing that raised immediate flags."

"That's right, Mr. President," another official chimed in, eager to shift the conversation to more stable ground. "I've also heard that Canada is preparing to open discussions with us next week regarding border policies and oil trade rates. The preliminary signals are positive."

The President nodded along, his expression unreadable as he absorbed the information. He folded his hands on the desk, the gesture both casual and commanding. "That's all good to hear. Truly. But is there anything else?"

"Like what, Mr. President?" the Secretary of State asked, his brow furrowing.

President Strongarm's eyes hardened slightly. "Like the fact that mutant-related incidents are suddenly... decreasing. Dramatically."

A ripple of uncertainty passed through the room. The Vice President leaned forward slightly. "Mr. President, what exactly are you suggesting?"

"You've all seen the news anchors," the President continued, his voice dropping to a more measured tone. "This week's ratings are lower for mutant terrorism coverage. Fewer reports of rogue killings, fewer incidents of humans being targeted. On the surface, that appears to be good news—a sign that our systems are working."

"Sir, that's correct," the Secretary of Defense offered cautiously. "It means our containment strategies are effective."

"Right." The President's voice carried a sharp edge now. "But it's also deeply strange that we've received no reports from the Pentagon or the military chain of command, and the Warden himself has been conspicuously absent from any communications. He should be the one enjoying this most—this is what he's spent his career building."

"Sir, are you suggesting there's something else going on?" The Secretary of Homeland Security's voice was carefully neutral.

The President turned, retrieving a remote from his jacket pocket. With a press of a button, the window curtains slid shut with a soft mechanical hum, plunging the room into dim light. A large projection flickered to life, casting a glowing chart across the far wall. "This is our current logistics report," he said, gesturing at the data streaming across the screen. "Incoming and outgoing communications. Notice the imbalance—the incoming is severely lacking." He turned his gaze back to the Secretary of Defense. "Can you explain the statistics we're seeing?"

The man sighed heavily, rubbing his temples. "With all due respect, Mr. President, there is a problem. A major one."

"Go on."

"Our soldiers are going missing. Politicians too. Three days ago, we lost three high-ranking officials—one in a supposed accident, one by suicide, and one who was stabbed in the back and never got up. But that's not the most troubling part." He paused, collecting himself. "There was a call from the OMP-Site requesting reinforcements from the Pentagon and US armed forces. They were dispatched immediately. But then, suddenly, the Warden called back and reported that the situation had been dealt with."

"Dealt with how exactly?"

"He claimed the mutants had been neutralized, their weapons confiscated, and operations were resuming as normal. The general requested a recon team to verify, but they went missing—true to protocol, since no one outside of the highest clearance is supposed to know the OMP-Site's exact location."

"How bad is it?"

"The problem," the Secretary of Defense said slowly, "is that the Pentagon also sent a formal request for updates, but we've received no reports since that initial message. However, we've been tracking an alarming number of suicide events among personnel stationed there."

"Suicides?"

"Yes, Mr. President. Too many to be a coincidence."

The room fell into a heavy silence. Then, quietly: "Does that mean there's been a sudden shift? A breach?"

"Worst case scenario..." The Secretary of Defense's voice dropped to barely a whisper. "It wasn't the Warden speaking to us."

A chill seemed to pass through the room. The President's face remained impassive, but his knuckles whitened slightly where his hands rested on the table.

"Have you all heard the rumors circulating?" the Vice President finally asked, breaking the silence.

"Why, yes," another official replied. "That's exactly why we've been monitoring this so closely."

"That's right, Mr. President," the Vice President confirmed. "We sent two recon squads two days ago to investigate the sector."

"And what happened to them?"

"We haven't received any news. I'm sure it's just signal interference from the North Pole region, but there are reports of huge amounts of mutant encampments—some say the wanted criminal networks have established bases under the ice."

"Keep me updated on that." The President turned his attention to another corner of the table. "What about the Pentagon?"

"The director sent a message regarding the events, but they can't transmit digitally for security reasons. The envoy they dispatched has not arrived."

"Then they should have been here two days ago. What happened?"

"We have no idea. We're attempting to establish an encrypted channel, but the signal today is noticeably weaker than usual."

"Then tell the electricity and technical teams to focus all available power into that channel. I need them yesterday." The President's voice was steel now. "This could be a systematic attack."

"By mutant terrorists, sir?"

"No." The President shook his head slowly. "This is too strategic. I want our military generals in here immediately for assessment. We need to understand what we're facing."

"All due respect, Mr. President," the CIA Director spoke up, his voice measured and calm, "if we blindly accuse foreign powers, we risk publicly confirming the existence of the OMP-Site. It's one of our few facilities capable of successful mutanology research. If word spreads, there will be sympathizers, riots, and massive public backlash. We need a controlled statement—spin the narrative that mutant activity is decreasing, not escalating."

The Secretary of Defense scoffed. "With all respect to the Director, we can have JSOC choppers on the tarmac in twenty minutes. We can send in armed forces to check the site, and if it's been infiltrated by mutie freaks, we sanitize the area and leave no paper trail."

The White House Chief of Staff slammed his hand on the table. "Are you insane? If we launch an unauthorized military strike inside an allied nation, we destroy NATO and our treaties! The President will be impeached by morning!"

"Then we do nothing and let them parade our officers on international television!"

"We have to contain this before it reaches the press!"

"If we contain it too aggressively, it will definitely reach the press!"

The arguments escalated, voices rising over one another in a cacophony of competing agendas and mounting panic. Papers were shuffled, fingers pointed, accusations hurled across the table. The Situation Room, designed for cool-headed deliberation, had become a pressure cooker of barely contained hysteria.

Then—two sharp claps.

The sound cut through the chaos like a knife, immediate and commanding.

"Ladies, gentlemen," a smooth, feminine voice cut through the noise with effortless authority, "please sit down and take a deep breath. All of you."

The room stilled. Heads turned toward the source of the interruption. The Ambassador-at-Large sat at the far end of the table, her pink hair flowing in a low ponytail, her equally pink eyes fixed on the assembled officials with a serene, almost otherworldly calm. Her posture was impeccable, her hands folded neatly before her, a single pen twirling idly between her fingers.

"If we send black ops choppers into a sovereign ally's airspace," she continued, her voice carrying a honeyed warmth that somehow made her words even more chilling, "we aren't managing a leak anymore—we are starting a war. And if we do nothing, they parade our officers on international television." She paused, letting the weight of her words settle. "Neither option is acceptable."

The room went dead silent.

President Strongarm leaned back in his chair, studying her with a mixture of respect and wariness. "The Ambassador-at-Large is right," he said slowly. "If we rush into this blindly, we don't know if we're walking into a trap." He turned his full attention to her. "Alyxia, what is the best approach for this situation?"

Alyxia smiled—a serene, almost beatific expression that seemed utterly out of place given the gravity of the discussion. "Very well, Mr. President." She clasped her hands on the table, the pen going still between her fingers. "Here is what we are actually going to do. The CIA will freeze all movement at the site immediately. While they do that, someone needs to utilize a back-channel—as has been suggested—to contact the Foreign Minister directly on a secure line within five minutes. We will offer them a joint-intelligence narrative: we'll tell their public that this was a 'coordinated counter-terrorism exercise' they authorized months ago."

She raised a finger, the gesture delicate but commanding. "Then we need a small crew to investigate the site itself. Given the number of disappearances, it's best to keep in contact with a satellite every three minutes. That way, we'll know if someone is deliberately targeting communications. They should maintain a distance of at least four kilometers—far enough to stay out of immediate danger, close enough to observe. Cross-reference with satellite coverage simultaneously — if the view from above is jammed, that confirms a larger operation than we initially suspected.”

She paused, allowing her words to sink in. "If we publicly frame this as a continuation of a problem from months ago, we save their political reputation. We can quietly transfer our assets out under cover of night, and offer them a foreign aid concession next quarter to seal the deal." She turned her gaze to the Secretary of Defense. "Stand your choppers down, but keep them on standby just outside their radar line. Ready to move, but not moving. There's a difference."

President Strongarm nodded slowly, his expression thoughtful. "That's good work. You heard her, everyone—get things in motion." He turned back to Alyxia. "I need you to be the one to call the Foreign Minister. I'll authorize you personally."

Alyxia's smile widened slightly. "My, my, Mr. President. Thank you for giving me that opportunity and trusting me with such a heavy responsibility." She paused, tilting her head as if in consideration. "But it might be better for someone like the Vice President to handle it. My role is advisory, not operational."

"You have an exceptional record of successful diplomacy, especially international discussions with Britain and other key allies. I'm more than certain you will handle the job."

She let out a soft sigh, the sound almost imperceptible. "Very well." She bowed her head gracefully. "Then I'll do my part. Thank you for trusting me, Mr. President."

"Good." He turned to address the rest of the room. "Now, the rest of you—investigate the updates on the OMP-Site. I want eyes on this immediately. No delays, no excuses."

The assembled officials nodded in unison, a wave of grim determination settling over the room.

Alyxia rose from her seat with fluid grace, smoothing the front of her suit as she did. "I'll take my leave to prepare for the call, Mr. President. Time is of the essence."

"Alright, everyone." The President stood, his chair scraping softly against the floor. "This meeting is adjourned. Get to work. We are going to be very busy over the next few months."

The heavy oak door swung open, and the assembled officials began filing out of the Situation Room in a steady stream. Their footsteps echoed against the polished floors, a rhythmic percussion that gradually faded as they dispersed into the corridors. Small clusters formed organically—here a group of military advisors huddled together, their voices low and urgent; there a pair of intelligence officers exchanged hushed words, their expressions grave. The tension that had filled the room moments before now bled out into the hallways, dissipating like smoke through open windows.

Alyxia emerged last, her heels clicking softly against the marble floor as she walked with measured grace. A gentle hum escaped her lips—a light, almost carefree melody that seemed utterly at odds with the weight of the meeting she had just left behind. Her pink hair swayed gently with each step, catching the fluorescent light in soft waves.

"Miss Alyxia!"

She paused mid-stride, her humming ceasing as she turned smoothly on her heel. Secretary of Defense Robert approached her with long, deliberate strides, his imposing figure cutting an unmistakable silhouette against the corridor's harsh lighting. He was a tall man, broad-shouldered and solidly built, but it was his arms that drew the eye—heavy mechanical constructs, sleek and powerful, their servos whirring faintly with each deliberate movement.

"Oh, Sir Robert," Alyxia said, her serene smile settling into place. She clasped her hands before her, tilting her head slightly. "Is there something you need me for?"

The Secretary crossed his mechanical arms, the metal joints clicking softly as they locked into position. He stepped closer, his single optic eye—a glowing red implant set into his left socket—fixing her with an unblinking stare. "I still think it would be a great idea to bring out arms," he said, his voice a low rumble. "Should we also check the seas? Mutant biology is always unpredictable, their DNA conforming to no standard pattern. We can't afford to assume the threat is only on land."

"Oh my!" Alyxia's pink eyes widened slightly, her hand lifting to her chin in a thoughtful gesture. "You're right. Where do you think you should deploy?"

"I'm considering calling in the naval forces," Robert continued, uncrossing his arms to gesture broadly. "Send a scouting fleet to the shores close to Arctic waters. I've heard reports of many muties heading in that direction. If we can intercept them, maybe we can get some intel on what's actually happening up there."

Alyxia hummed softly, tapping her chin with a single finger as she considered his words. Her smile remained fixed, warm and encouraging. "That's actually a great idea, Secretary."

"Is it?" Robert's mechanical eye flickered, the red glow intensifying for a brief moment. His posture seemed to relax almost imperceptibly, as if her approval had eased some hidden tension.

"Of course it is," she replied, her tone carrying a note of genuine enthusiasm. "But maybe don't get too close just yet. We still don't have any information about the current status of our recon forces. If you want to proceed, by all means, go ahead—but I strongly suggest getting permission from the President first. He'll need to sign off on any major naval deployment."

Robert nodded slowly, his mechanical arms uncrossing to rest at his sides. "Right. Of course. I understand." He paused, his organic eye—the one not replaced by machinery—studying her with an unreadable expression. "I just wanted to gain some advice from you, to make sure I'm making the right call. You've always had a head for strategy."

"I appreciate that, truly." Alyxia inclined her head, the gesture graceful and measured.

"Are you going to make the call now?"

"Oh, not immediately," she said, letting out a soft, almost musical laugh. "I have to file some papers first—discussions with the prince in Dubai, and some terms to finalize with Germany regarding our mutual agreements. The bureaucracy never sleeps, I'm afraid."

Robert offered a tight, respectful nod. "Then I won't keep you late." He turned slightly, then paused, glancing back over his shoulder. "Please, come visit from time to time. My daughter is always fond of you. She asks about you constantly."

Alyxia chuckled warmly, pressing a hand to her chest. "Of course, of course. Please tell her I send my regards. I'll try to stop by soon."

The Secretary nodded once more, then strode off down the hall, his heavy footsteps echoing against the marble as he disappeared around a corner. Alyxia watched him go, her serene smile unwavering, then turned and continued on her path.

She walked with purpose now, her humming resuming its soft, lilting melody as she navigated the labyrinthine corridors of the White House. The sounds of distant conversations and ringing phones faded behind her as she descended a flight of stairs, her heels clicking a steady rhythm against the concrete. Eventually, she reached a nondescript door, its surface unmarked save for a small brass nameplate that read "Ambassador-at-Large."

She pushed the door open, stepping into a modest but elegant office. The space was warm and inviting, with tall windows that overlooked a small courtyard, and shelves lined with leather-bound books and tasteful decorations. A young man stood near the desk, his arms full of neatly stacked papers. He turned at the sound of her entrance, his expression brightening.

"Ah, Lady Alyxia," he said, his voice carrying a note of respectful familiarity. "Sorry to bother you, but here are the papers you requested." He crossed the room and extended the stack toward her.

She took them gracefully, her eyes moving rapidly across the top page. "Ah, are these by any chance the documents about the oil exchange rates from last month?" She flipped through a few pages. "Yes, these are perfect. Thank you, Matt."

The young man smiled, a flicker of pride crossing his face. "It's my job as your assistant, ma'am."

She let out a soft giggle, the sound light and genuine. "Yes, you're indeed very good at it." She moved past him, her heels carrying her to the large oak desk that dominated the center of the room. She settled into her chair with fluid grace, the leather creaking softly as she leaned back. Her fingers began to move, selecting a single paper from the stack and lifting it before her. Her pink eyes scanned the text, her expression serene and focused, as though she were reading nothing more consequential than a grocery list.

Matt lingered by the bookshelves, his hands moving automatically as he tidied the space. He paused, noticing a book that had slipped from its place, lying face-down on the floor. He bent down, retrieved it, and was about to slide it back into its place when he hesitated, turning to face her.

"Lady Alyxia," he began, his voice thoughtful, "if I may..."

She looked up from her papers, her pen pausing mid-stroke. "Hm?"

"I've been thinking about today's news," he continued, gesturing vaguely with the book still in his hand. "You know, about everything that's happening."

Alyxia chuckled softly, returning her attention to the document before her. "You mean today's conflict?"

"Yes, it's..." Matt shrugged, his brow furrowing. "I don't know. It's getting confusing. All the reports, the violence, the way people are reacting to each other..."

"Politics is designed to be confusing," Alyxia said, her tone dismissive as she waved her pen absently. "The press cycle feeds on whatever it can reach, turning tragedy into spectacle and ordinary decency into a headline. A man helps a mutant woman cross the street and it runs for days. Someone aids a poor elderly man and it's treated as remarkable. The same story, endlessly repackaged under a different title."

"I know, I know." Matt set the book on a nearby shelf, his hands finding his pockets. "I'm just saying... I don't know. It feels like things are getting worse."

Alyxia sighed softly, setting her pen down and leaning back in her chair. She regarded him with a patient, almost maternal expression. "Well, that's how people tend to be, Matt. That's what humanity is—not good, not benevolent, but not entirely evil either." She paused, her pink eyes flickering with something unreadable. "People will always find ways to hurt each other. Others will find ways to help. And some..." Her voice dropped slightly, taking on a more contemplative tone. "Some just want to see others burn."

Matt shifted uncomfortably, his gaze dropping to the floor.

"They always try to find someone to mark as a villain, as a monster," Alyxia continued, her voice soft and measured, "so that they can call themselves good people—without actually having to show their own good nature." She smiled, serene and untroubled. "But that doesn't mean it applies to everyone."

"Really?"

"Really." She rested her elbows on the desk, lowering her chin to her folded hands. "Humans, mutants... we all fear what is not 'normal' to us. History repeats itself—humanity invented slavery and spent centuries trying to undo it." She tilted her head, her pink eyes glinting softly. "It all takes time, Matt. Just be patient. I'm sure everything will turn out well in the end."

Matt nodded slowly, his expression thoughtful. "Yeah... yeah, that makes sense." He paused, then offered an apologetic smile. "Sorry to take up your time, Lady Alyxia."

"It's no trouble at all."

He turned and moved toward the door, pausing with his hand on the frame. "I'll go clean up the other office now."

As the door clicked shut behind him, Alyxia's serene smile shifted, the warmth fading to something colder, more calculating. She set the papers down with deliberate precision, her fingers smoothing the edges before she rose from her chair. She crossed to the window, her reflection faint against the glass, and stood in silence for a long moment.

Then she turned back into the room and lowered herself into her chair once more—unhurried, deliberate, as though the weight of the meeting had already left her entirely.

A soft beep sounded from her ear. She raised a hand, pressing the hidden commlink nestled within her ear canal. Her expression smoothed back into that familiar, placid serenity.

"This is General Alyxia of the Diplomacy Department," she said, her voice calm and collected. She turned slowly, spinning her chair to face the window fully, her pink eyes gazing out at the Washington skyline. "I'll have to report today's meeting."
