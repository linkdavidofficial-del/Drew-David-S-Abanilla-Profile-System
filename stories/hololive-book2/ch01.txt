Chapter 1: Part One: The Peaceful Beginning

When Idols Fall From the Sky

Part One: The Peaceful Beginning

The private aircraft hummed smoothly through the cloudless sky, its engines purring with the confident reliability of top-tier engineering. Inside the VIP cabin, the entirety of Hololive English had essentially taken over the spacious interior, transforming what should have been a dignified business flight into something resembling a chaotic slumber party at thirty thousand feet.

Cover Corporation had spared no expense for this particular trip—a team-building retreat that promised luxury accommodations, exclusive experiences, and most importantly, a chance for all the EN members to bond outside the usual studio recordings and online collaborations. The plane's interior reflected this investment: plush leather seats arranged around polished mahogany tables, ambient lighting that could be adjusted to any mood, and enough legroom to satisfy even the tallest among them.

Holomyth had claimed the front section, naturally. As the pioneers of Hololive English, they carried themselves with a certain unspoken seniority, though it manifested more as comfortable confidence than any actual hierarchy.

Calliope Mori stretched out in her seat, her usual rapper aesthetic somehow making the expensive surroundings look cooler rather than out of place. She had her phone out, scrolling through beats she'd been working on, occasionally bobbing her head to rhythms only she could hear through her earbuds.

"Calli, you've been staring at that screen for like an hour," Kiara Takanashi chirped from across the table, her phoenix-bright energy undimmed by the confined space. "We're supposed to be bonding! This is a group trip, remember?"

"I am bonding," Calli replied without looking up, though a small smirk played at her lips. "I'm bonding with my creative process. It's a very deep relationship."

"That's the saddest thing I've ever heard, and I once watched Gura try to play a horror game," Kiara shot back, grinning.

"Hey!" Gawr Gura protested from where she was sprawled across two seats, somehow having claimed more space than anyone else despite being the smallest member present. "I'm great at horror games! I only screamed, like, thirty times."

"Per minute," Amelia Watson added dryly, not looking up from her detective novel. The time-traveling investigator had her feet propped up on her small suitcase, completely absorbed in her book. "Thirty times per minute."

"That's an exaggeration and you know it," Gura huffed, though she couldn't quite hide her grin. She rolled over, her shark hoodie somehow still perfectly in place despite her constant fidgeting. "It was only twenty-seven times per minute. I counted."

"The fact that you counted makes it worse, not better," Amelia pointed out, finally glancing up with that characteristic smirk that always meant she was enjoying herself.

Kiara laughed, the sound bright and warm. "See, Calli? This is bonding. Put the phone away and join us in roasting Gura."

"I was here first!" Gura protested. "You can't just gang up on me like this! Ina, help!"

But Ina'nis Ninomae was too absorbed in her own world to respond immediately. The artist sat slightly apart from the others, her tablet propped on the table in front of her, stylus moving in smooth, practiced strokes across the screen. She was working on something—a landscape, from what the others could glimpse—with that serene focus that characterized everything she did.

"Ina's in the zone," Calli observed. "You know she can't hear us when she's drawing."

"Is that why she didn't react when we took off?" Kiara wondered aloud. "I swear she didn't even blink during the whole ascent."

"Ina exists on a higher plane of consciousness," Gura declared sagely. "She's transcended the need for things like 'awareness' and 'acknowledging her friends.'"

"I can hear you," Ina said mildly, still not looking up from her tablet. "I'm just choosing to ignore your nonsense in favor of productive activity."

"See? Transcendent," Gura repeated, nodding as if this proved her point.

Further back in the cabin, Council and IRyS had claimed their territory with equal enthusiasm if slightly less chaos.

IRyS sat with perfect posture, somehow managing to look elegant even in travel clothes. The half-demon, half-angel idol had her hair tied back in a practical ponytail, and she was engaged in what appeared to be a very serious discussion with Fauna.

"I'm just saying," IRyS explained, gesturing expressively, "that pineapple on pizza represents humanity's ability to transcend traditional boundaries and create something new. It's basically a metaphor for innovation."

"It's basically a crime against nature," Fauna Ceres countered, her usually gentle voice taking on an edge of playful steel. The Mother Nature of Hololive was not about to concede this point. "Pineapple is a fruit. Pizza is a savory dish. They belong in separate categories for a reason."

"By that logic, tomatoes shouldn't be on pizza either," IRyS shot back. "They're a fruit too, technically."

"Tomatoes are culinarily a vegetable, which is what matters in cooking," Fauna retorted. "Pineapple is sweet and tropical and has no business being heated to that temperature and placed on cheese."

Kronii Ouro watched this exchange with barely concealed amusement, her arms crossed and a smirk on her face. The Warden of Time looked completely relaxed, her usual slightly chaotic energy mellowed by the comfortable flight.

"You two have been arguing about this for twenty minutes," she observed. "At what point does it stop being a debate and start being foreplay?"

"Kronii!" IRyS sputtered, her cheeks flushing.

Fauna simply laughed. "Someone's projecting."

"I don't project," Kronii said with exaggerated dignity. "I'm perfect. Perfect beings don't project."

"You literally called yourself narcissistic on stream last week," Mumei Nanashi pointed out without looking up from her phone. The Guardian of Civilization was slouched in her seat, thumbs moving rapidly across her screen as she played some mobile game with intense focus.

"That was self-aware narcissism, which is totally different," Kronii insisted. "It's like... meta-narcissism. Advanced narcissism."

"That's not a thing," Sana Tsukumo chimed in cheerfully. The cosmic entity had somehow managed to curl up in her seat despite her height, looking completely comfortable in what should have been an awkward position. "You're just a narcissist, Kronii. But we love you anyway."

"I feel like that should be more comforting than it is," Kronii muttered.

Hakos Baelz had been suspiciously quiet, which usually meant she was up to something. The embodiment of Chaos sat with her legs tucked under her, her dice bag resting on the table in front of her. She'd been rolling dice idly for the past few minutes, each roll producing a satisfying clatter against the polished wood.

"Bae, if you're planning something chaotic, can you at least wait until we land?" Fauna asked, eyeing the dice warily. "I'd rather not deal with your brand of randomness at thirty thousand feet."

"What? Me? Planning something?" Bae's grin was absolutely unrepentant. "I'm just enjoying the flight. Rolling some dice. Very innocent, very normal behavior."

"That's exactly what someone planning something would say," Mumei observed, still not looking up from her game.

"You're all so suspicious," Bae sighed dramatically. "Can't a chaotic entity just vibe anymore?"

"No," came the immediate, unanimous response from everyone else in the Council section.

The Advent section of the cabin was... well, it was exactly what anyone who knew Advent would expect.

Shiori Novella had somehow already accumulated a small library around her seat, books stacked in precarious towers on every available surface. She sat in the middle of this literary fortress, her ever-present quill and journal in hand, scribbling notes about something only she understood.

"And then," Shiori was saying, her eyes gleaming with that particular intensity she got when discussing her favorite topic, "the reader has to choose between the mysterious vampire prince and the werewolf guardian, but plot twist—they're actually the same person cursed to shift between forms based on the moon cycle."

"That's so big-brained," Nerissa Ravencroft breathed, completely enraptured. The songstress was practically vibrating with excitement, her phone temporarily forgotten in her lap. "The angst potential is astronomical. The reader thinking they're betraying one by choosing the other, but really they're just—"

"Choosing the same person twice!" Shiori finished, nodding enthusiastically. "And the identity reveal happens during a full moon when both forms try to protect the reader from danger and they see the transformation—"

"STOP, I can't handle this," Nerissa actually clutched her chest dramatically. "My heart can't take it. That's too good. Why isn't this a real fanfic yet?"

"Because we're on a plane and I don't have my laptop," Shiori said matter-of-factly. "But trust me, the moment we land, I'm writing this. I've already got the title: 'Moonlit Reflections: A Dual Love Story.'"

"I would read that so hard," Nerissa declared. Then her phone buzzed, and her attention immediately shifted. She grabbed it with almost frightening speed, her eyes going wide. "Oh my god. OH MY GOD."

"What?" Shiori leaned over, curious despite herself. "What happened?"

"Kiara just posted a selfie," Nerissa whispered reverently, staring at her phone like it held the secrets of the universe. "Look at her. Look. At. Her. She's so beautiful I could cry."

"Nerissa, Kiara is literally in this plane with us," Shiori pointed out. "She's like, fifty feet away."

"Yeah, but this is a new picture," Nerissa said, as if this explained everything. "I need to like it immediately. And comment something supportive but not too desperate. What says 'I appreciate your existence' without saying 'I'm obsessed with you'?"

"You're literally obsessed with her though."

"She doesn't need to know that from my comment," Nerissa hissed.

Fuwawa and Mococo were having their own animated conversation across the table, their twin energy creating a sort of synchronized chaos that was uniquely theirs.

"I still think we should have brought more snacks," Fuwawa was saying, her fluffy ears twitching with emphasis. "What if the food on the retreat isn't good?"

"We packed three entire suitcases," Mococo pointed out. "How much food do you think we need?"

"More than three suitcases' worth, obviously," Fuwawa replied with perfect logic. "Besides, what if someone else gets hungry? We need to be prepared to feed the entire group."

"We're not running a restaurant, Fuwawa."

"Not with that attitude we're not."

Koseki Bijou had her headphones on, apparently content to exist in her own world. The rock-themed idol had her eyes closed, head bobbing slightly to whatever music she was listening to. Occasionally she'd mumble something that might have been lyrics, but it was impossible to tell if she was actually singing or just making sounds.

It was, all things considered, a perfect snapshot of Advent energy: chaotic, enthusiastic, and somehow perfectly synchronized despite appearing completely random.

At the very back of the cabin, Justice had claimed their territory with an enthusiasm that bordered on territorial.

"LET IT GO, LET IT GO!" Elizabeth Rose Bloodflame belted out, her voice carrying through the entire cabin with operatic intensity. "CAN'T HOLD IT BACK ANYMORE!"

"LET IT GO, LET IT GO!" Cecilia Immergreen joined in, her own vocal prowess creating a harmony that was actually quite beautiful despite the chaotic energy.

"TURN AWAY AND SLAM THE DOOR!" Raora Panthera added, her accent adding a unique flavor to the lyrics.

Gigi Murin conducted them with dramatic flair, standing on her seat and gesturing like she was directing a full orchestra. "More passion! More drama! Pretend you're actually Elsa!"

"I'm already pretending I'm Elsa," Elizabeth protested, not stopping her singing for a moment. "What do you think all these hand gestures are for?"

"Those are jazz hands, not ice magic hands," Gigi corrected. "You need more—" she wiggled her fingers elaborately, "—sparkle in the motion."

"I DON'T CARE WHAT THEY'RE GOING TO SAY!" all four of them sang together, loud enough that several members from other groups looked back with expressions ranging from amused to mildly concerned.

"Should we tell them to quiet down?" Kiara asked from the Myth section.

"Nah," Calli replied, a grin on her face. "Let them have their moment."

"THE COLD NEVER BOTHERED ME ANYWAY!"

The finale was delivered with such enthusiastic volume that even the pilot probably heard it.

"Beautiful," Gigi declared, wiping away an imaginary tear. "Truly we are the most talented group on this plane."

"We're definitely the loudest," Raora agreed cheerfully.

"Same thing," Gigi insisted.

The flight continued in this vein for another hour—Myth trading friendly banter, Council debating increasingly absurd topics (they'd moved from pineapple on pizza to whether cereal was a soup), Advent deep in their fanfiction discussions with occasional breaks for Nerissa to simp over other idols on social media, and Justice running through their Disney song repertoire with undiminished energy.

It was perfect. It was chaotic. It was exactly what a Hololive EN group trip should be.

And then everything went wrong.
