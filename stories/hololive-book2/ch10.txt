Chapter 10: The Long Road Home — Part Three: Paradise Found (Advent's Island Life)

The Long Road Home
Part Three: Paradise Found (Advent's Island Life)

Seven hours.

It had been exactly seven hours since Advent had crash-landed on their tropical island, and somehow, impossibly, they had transformed a survival situation into what could only be described as a vacation resort.

Shiori stood at the edge of their settlement, clipboard in hand (she'd made the clipboard herself from salvaged materials and sheer determination), surveying their work with the critical eye of a project manager who had somehow found herself running an island development company.

"Status report!" she called out.

"Hut construction complete!" Fuwawa called back from where she and Mococo were putting finishing touches on the last of five individual shelters. "All structures are waterproof, ventilated, and have proper drainage!"

"How do you even know how to build proper drainage?" Shiori asked.

"Instinct!" Mococo replied cheerfully. "And we may have found a construction manual in one of the abandoned buildings!"

"That tracks."

Each member of Advent now had their own small hut, arranged in a circle around a central communal area. The structures were surprisingly well-made—wooden frames from island bamboo, roofs thatched with large leaves, walls woven from smaller branches and vines. They weren't fancy, but they were functional and actually quite comfortable.

But the huts were just the beginning.

"Hot spring status?" Shiori continued down her mental checklist.

"Operational and amazing!" Nerissa's voice drifted over from the forest. "You have to feel this, Shiori! It's perfect!"

Shiori walked over to where Nerissa was lounging in what could only be described as a natural hot spring that definitely hadn't been there when they first explored the island. The water steamed gently, the temperature perfect, the rocks around it arranged in a way that was just a bit too convenient to be natural.

"Nerissa," Shiori said slowly. "Did you make this?"

"Maybe?" Nerissa grinned, not opening her eyes. "I found a natural warm spring and then used a teensy bit of magic to enhance it. It's totally safe! Probably!"

"Probably?"

"Definitely probably!"

Shiori sighed but couldn't argue with results. The hot spring was genuinely nice, and after everything they'd been through, a bit of magical enhancement seemed reasonable.

"Livestock status?" she called out to the camp in general.

"All tamed!" Bijou appeared from around a hut, somehow covered in mud but looking pleased with herself. "We've got three pigs and two cows fully domesticated. They're penned up near the forest edge, and I've already started a feeding schedule."

"How did you tame wild pigs in seven hours?"

Bijou shrugged. "I gave them food and spoke nicely to them. Turns out island pigs are pretty chill if you're not threatening."

"The cows were harder," Fuwawa added, walking over. "But Mococo did this thing where she just sat with them for like two hours and now they follow her around."

"They think she's their mom," Mococo said, slightly embarrassed. "I didn't mean for that to happen."

"We have livestock that think Mococo is their mother," Shiori noted on her mental list. "Sure. Why not. Farming status?"

"Started!" Nerissa had apparently gotten out of the hot spring and was now standing there dripping. "We found some wild vegetable patches and I've been using magic to accelerate their growth. We should have fresh produce by tomorrow."

"You can do that?"

"I'm a songstress with magic powers. I can do lots of things." Nerissa paused. "Most of them are probably not recommended by any governing magical authority, but they work!"

Shiori decided not to question it further. Seven hours on this island had taught her that sometimes you just had to accept the chaos and move forward.

"Ship construction?" she asked, moving to the big question.

"Almost done!" Fuwamoco called in unison from the beach.

Shiori walked down to the shore and stopped, staring.

Somehow—and she really couldn't explain the somehow—Fuwamoco had constructed what looked like a genuinely seaworthy vessel. It wasn't large, maybe fifteen feet long, but it had a proper hull, a mast, rigging for a sail, and what appeared to be a functional rudder system.

"How," Shiori said flatly. "How did you build this in seven hours."

"We work fast!" Fuwawa said proudly.

"And we found some really good wood!" Mococo added.

"And Nerissa helped with magic!" they said together.

"I made the joints seal themselves," Nerissa explained, walking up behind Shiori. "And accelerated the drying process so the wood wouldn't warp. Basic enhancement magic, really."

"There is nothing basic about any of this," Shiori muttered, but she was impressed despite herself.

She pulled out her book and quill—her special book, the one that was never quite normal—and opened it to a page covered in calculations and notes. With Nerissa's magical assistance, they'd been able to use the book's properties to triangulate their position.

"Okay," Shiori said, studying the notes. "Based on the stars, the sun's position, and some educated guessing, we're about two hundred miles from the nearest major shipping lane. If we sail northeast for approximately—" she did some quick math, "—sixteen hours, we should intercept commercial traffic."

"Or," Nerissa suggested, "we could use my magic to enhance the ship's speed and my navigation sense to guide us directly home. Cut the travel time to maybe eight hours."

Shiori looked at her. "You can do that?"

"Probably! I mean, I've never done it before, but how hard can it be?"

"That's not reassuring!"

"It's the best we've got!"

Bijou joined them, now cleaned of mud. "So what's the plan? We sail out and hope for the best?"

"Essentially," Shiori confirmed. "We load up supplies, set sail at dawn tomorrow, and either make it to civilization or have an exciting maritime adventure that we'll never speak of again."

"I'm voting for civilization," Fuwawa said.

"Seconded," Mococo agreed.

"All in favor?" Shiori asked.

Everyone raised their hands.

"Unanimous. Great. Let's finish preparations."

The rest of the day passed in a flurry of activity. They gathered supplies—dried fish they'd caught and preserved using Nerissa's magic (which Shiori was beginning to suspect was her solution to everything), fruits from the island, fresh water stored in containers they'd fashioned from bamboo and sealed with sap.

The tamed animals were given extra food and released back to the wild—they'd survive fine without human care, and taking them on a boat seemed cruel.

As the sun set, they gathered around a fire in their central communal area, eating a meal of grilled fish and roasted vegetables that was honestly better than it had any right to be.

"You know," Bijou said, looking around at their little settlement, "if the circumstances were different, this would actually be a nice vacation spot."

"It really would," Nerissa agreed. "Hot spring, fresh food, beautiful beach, no responsibilities..."

"We could start a resort," Fuwawa joked. "Hololive Island Getaway."

"Absolutely not," Shiori said firmly. "I am never coming back here. Ever. This island is dead to me."

"You literally organized the best survival camp ever," Mococo pointed out.

"That's beside the point!"

They laughed, the sound carrying across the peaceful island. Despite everything—the plane crash, the initial panic, the uncertainty—they'd not only survived but thrived. They'd worked together, used their individual skills, and created something genuinely impressive.

"Tomorrow we sail," Shiori said, raising her bamboo cup of fresh water. "To going home."

"To going home!" everyone echoed, cups clinking.

They slept well that night, each in their own hut, comfortable and safe.

At dawn, they loaded the ship with their supplies, took one last look at the island that had been their temporary home, and set sail.

Nerissa stood at the front of the vessel, her magic flowing around the ship like invisible currents, guiding them northeast. The wind filled their sail—perhaps a bit more enthusiastically than natural wind would—and they began to move at a pace that was definitely enhanced.

"We're flying!" Bijou laughed, holding onto the mast. "This thing is moving way faster than it should!"

"Magic!" Nerissa called back. "Don't question it!"

Shiori, manning the rudder with Fuwamoco's assistance, allowed herself a small smile. They were going home. It had been an adventure—terrifying, exhausting, but also strangely bonding.

Eight hours later, exactly as Nerissa had predicted, they spotted a cargo ship on the horizon.

They waved, shouted, used Nerissa's magic to create a pillar of light that couldn't be ignored. The cargo ship altered course, approaching with obvious confusion at finding five young women in a handmade boat in the middle of the ocean.

After some explaining—heavily edited to remove the more unbelievable parts—the captain agreed to take them to the nearest port, which happened to be exactly where they needed to go.

Advent watched their little ship be tied to the cargo vessel, a strange fondness for the craft that had carried them home.

"Think we'll ever see that island again?" Mococo asked.

"Not if I have anything to say about it," Shiori replied.

But she was smiling as she said it.
