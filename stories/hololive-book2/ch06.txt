Part Six: Snow and Yetis (Justice's Mountain Madness)

Elizabeth Rose Bloodflame woke up to the sensation of cold.

Not just cold. Cold. The kind of cold that immediately informed every nerve ending that this was a terrible place to be and maybe consciousness was overrated.

She sat up slowly, her breath misting in front of her face, and took in her surroundings with mounting disbelief.

Snow. Everywhere snow.

Mountains. Everywhere mountains.

"You have got to be fucking kidding me," she said aloud, her voice echoing slightly in the crystalline air.

They'd crashed in the mountains. The snowy, frozen, absolutely inhospitable mountains.

"Elizabeth?" A groggy voice came from nearby. Cecilia Immergreen was stirring, pushing herself up from a snowdrift. "What... where... why is it so cold?"

"We're in the mountains," Elizabeth supplied, already standing and looking around for the others. "Snow mountains. Because apparently the universe looked at our plane crash and thought 'you know what would make this better? Hypothermia.'"

"I hate this," Cecilia declared, hugging herself against the cold. "I hate this so much. Where are the others?"

"Raora! Gigi!" Elizabeth called out, her voice carrying across the snow. "Where are you?!"

"Over here!" Raora's accented voice came from behind a large snowbank. "We're okay! Sort of! Mostly!"

Elizabeth and Cecilia trudged through the snow—which was surprisingly deep—to find Raora and Gigi Murin both conscious and already going through their scattered luggage.

"Status?" Elizabeth asked, falling into leader mode automatically.

"Cold," Gigi said immediately. "Very cold. Extremely cold. Did I mention cold?"

"Noted. What about injuries?"

"Bruised, but nothing broken," Raora reported, checking herself over. "Our luggage is mostly here too. Your greatsword is over there—" she pointed to where Thorn was half-buried in snow, "—my claws are on my belt, Cecilia's lance is stuck in that ice formation, and Gigi's gauntlets are... Gigi, where are your gauntlets?"

"On my hands," Gigi held them up. "I never took them off. Which in hindsight was probably dangerous during a plane crash, but it worked out!"

"Small miracles," Elizabeth muttered. She retrieved her greatsword, brushing snow off the blade. The weapon's natural fire affinity made it slightly warm to the touch even in this cold, which was at least comforting.

Cecilia was working on extracting her white lance from the ice formation, which had apparently decided to claim it. "Why is everything stuck in something? Why can't weapons just land on the ground like normal items?"

"Physics hates us," Gigi theorized. "It's the only explanation."

They spent several minutes gathering their belongings, which were scattered across the snowy landscape but mostly intact. The section of plane they'd been in was visible about fifty yards away, half-buried in a snowbank like a modern art installation titled "Transportation Failure."

"Okay," Elizabeth said once they'd gathered everything essential. "We need to figure out where we are and how to get somewhere less frozen. Any ideas?"

"We could try following the mountains down?" Cecilia suggested. "Eventually we have to hit a lower elevation with less snow."

"Or we could look for caves," Raora added. "Shelter from the wind at least."

"Or," Gigi said slowly, "we could figure out what that is."

She was pointing at something in the middle distance. A formation that Elizabeth had initially dismissed as just an unusual rock shape.

It moved.

"Please tell me that's not what I think it is," Cecilia whispered.

It moved again, standing up to its full height, which was considerable. Easily eight feet tall, covered in white fur, with a face that was simultaneously ape-like and distinctly not any known primate.

"That's a yeti," Elizabeth said flatly. "We crashed in a mountain range inhabited by yetis."

"Maybe it's friendly?" Raora offered weakly.

The yeti noticed them. It stared for a long moment.

Then it roared—a sound that echoed off the mountains and probably could be heard for miles—and started charging toward them.

"NOT FRIENDLY!" Gigi shrieked. "DEFINITELY NOT FRIENDLY!"

"Why did you have to jinx it?!" Cecilia accused Raora.

"I was being optimistic!"

More roars answered the first one. From behind rocks, from around snow drifts, from what had looked like innocent white mounds, more yetis appeared. Lots of yetis. At least a dozen, all large, all angry-looking, all now very interested in the strange colorful creatures that had appeared in their territory.

"Run now, recriminations later!" Elizabeth ordered, and they ran.

Four idols, carrying their weapons and whatever luggage they could grab, sprinting through knee-deep snow while being chased by an entire pack of territorial yetis.

"This is your fault, Gigi!" Elizabeth yelled as they ran.

"How is this my fault?!" Gigi yelled back.

"You punched a rock earlier! I saw you! You punched a rock 'to see what we'd get' like this was Minecraft!"

"That was a completely reasonable thing to do!"

"IN WHAT WORLD?!"

"IN A WORLD WHERE WE FELL OUT OF THE SKY! NORMAL LOGIC DOESN'T APPLY ANYMORE!"

The yetis were gaining. They were built for this terrain, evolved for deep snow and thin air, while the Justice members were idols who spent most of their time indoors streaming.

"We need to fight!" Raora called out. "We can't outrun them!"

"Finally, something I'm good at!" Elizabeth skidded to a halt, spinning to face their pursuers. Her greatsword Thorn began to glow with internal fire, flames licking along the blade. "Come on then, you oversized snowmen! Let's dance!"

The nearest yeti reached her first, massive fists swinging. Elizabeth met it head-on, her flaming sword clashing against its claws with a sound like a forge hammer striking an anvil.

"For the record," she grunted, pushing back against the creature's strength, "I still think this is a terrible idea!"

"Noted!" Cecilia had taken up position beside her, her white lance gleaming. She thrust forward with precision, keeping another yeti at bay with quick, controlled strikes. "But we're doing it anyway!"

Raora's pink claws extended to their full length, energy crackling around them as she engaged two yetis at once. Her fighting style was more acrobatic, dodging and weaving between attacks while landing quick strikes. "There's too many of them!"

"I noticed!" Gigi had her oversized gauntlets up, blocking a yeti's charge with pure defensive stance. The impact sent her sliding backward through the snow, but she held her ground. "Anyone have a plan beyond 'don't die'?!"

"Working on it!" Elizabeth's sword flared brighter, flames intensifying. She swung in a wide arc, forcing three yetis to back off from the heat. "Spread them out! Don't let them surround us!"

What followed was perhaps the most surreal fight any of them had ever experienced. Four idols, using weapons that definitely shouldn't exist in the normal world, fighting a dozen territorial yetis in the middle of a snowy mountain range.

Elizabeth fought with controlled fury, her greatsword leaving trails of fire in the air with each swing. The flames didn't just illuminate—they drove the yetis back, the creatures clearly uncomfortable with the heat even as they continued their assault.

Cecilia was grace and precision, her lance finding weak points with each thrust, never overextending, always maintaining perfect form even in the chaos.

Raora was speed and ferocity, her claws raking across fur and forcing yetis to defend rather than attack, her smaller size letting her slip past guards that her companions couldn't.

And Gigi was pure stubborn defense, her gauntlets absorbing blows that should have shattered bone, her stance unshakeable even when the impacts sent snow flying.

"LEFT!" Elizabeth called out.

"Got it!" Raora was already moving, intercepting a yeti trying to flank them.

"Three more coming from the right!" Cecilia warned.

"I see them!" Gigi shifted position, putting herself between the new threats and her teammates.

They fell into a rhythm, covering each other's blind spots, calling out threats, working together with the kind of synchronization that came from hundreds of hours of collaboration—even if that collaboration had usually been singing streams rather than monster fights.

But there were still too many yetis, and the cold was sapping their strength with every passing minute.

"We need to retreat!" Cecilia gasped out between strikes. "Find defensible ground!"

"Or offensive ground!" Elizabeth countered, flames flaring along her sword as she drove back another attacker. "I'm open to either!"

"THERE!" Raora pointed with one claw while blocking with the other. "That rock formation! If we can get there, they can't surround us!"

The formation she'd indicated was about thirty yards away—a cluster of large boulders that formed a natural barrier on three sides.

"On three!" Elizabeth ordered. "One!"

She swung her sword in a massive arc, creating a wall of flame between them and the yetis.

"Two!"

The others prepared to move, weapons ready to defend during the sprint.

"THREE!"

They ran. Again. Because apparently today was just an endless series of running from things that wanted to kill them.

The yetis roared in frustration, charging after them through Elizabeth's dissipating flames.

Gigi reached the rocks first, sliding into the protected space and immediately turning to provide cover. "Come on, come on, come on!"

Raora was next, her superior speed carrying her through. Then Cecilia, lance held behind her to discourage pursuit.

Elizabeth came last, her sword still blazing, backing into the defensive position while keeping the yetis at bay with sweeping strikes.

"Everyone in?!" she called.

"We're in!" came the unanimous response.

"Good, because I'm getting tired of playing with these things!"

From their defensive position, the fight became more manageable. The yetis could only approach from one direction now, and that direction was covered by four very motivated idols with very sharp weapons.

Elizabeth's flames created a literal wall of fire across the opening. Cecilia's lance thrust forward to catch any creature that tried to push through. Raora and Gigi provided secondary defense, ready to intercept anything that got past the first line.

The yetis, faced with organized resistance instead of fleeing prey, began to reconsider their assault. Several of them had burns from Elizabeth's flames. Others bore cuts from Cecilia's precise strikes. They roared and postured, but they weren't advancing anymore.

"I think they're backing off," Raora observed cautiously.

"Don't jinx it again," Gigi hissed.

The standoff continued for another tense minute. Then, seemingly by some unspoken agreement among themselves, the yetis began to retreat, lumbering back into the snowy landscape.

Within minutes, they'd disappeared completely, leaving only tracks in the snow to prove they'd been there at all.

"Are they gone?" Cecilia asked, not lowering her lance.

"I think so," Elizabeth replied, finally letting her flames die down. "They decided we weren't worth the effort."

"Or they're regrouping for another attack," Gigi suggested darkly.

"Why are you like this?"

"Trauma from the last hour of my life!"

Now that the immediate danger had passed, the reality of their situation set in. They were in the middle of a snowy mountain range, they had no idea where they were, their luggage was scattered somewhere back where they'd first landed, and they'd just been attacked by cryptozoological creatures.

"Okay," Elizabeth said, her breath still misting in the cold air. "New plan. We need to find shelter, we need to find our luggage, and we need to figure out where the hell we are and how to get somewhere that isn't trying to kill us."

"And we need to do it before nightfall," Cecilia added, looking at the sun's position. "Because I do not want to be out here in the dark."

"Agreed on all counts," Raora said. "So... which direction?"

They looked around at the snowy mountains, which all looked essentially identical—white, cold, and full of potential yeti ambushes.

"I hate this," Gigi declared. "I hate everything about this situation."

"Get in line," Elizabeth muttered.

They spent a few minutes catching their breath and organizing what little they had. Their weapons, thankfully, were all accounted for. Some smaller items of luggage had made it with them during the fight. But most of their belongings were still back at the crash site, which was now somewhere behind them in yeti territory.

"We have to go back for our stuff," Raora said reluctantly. "We need supplies if we're going to survive out here."

"I was afraid you'd say that," Cecilia sighed.

"Maybe the yetis are gone for good?" Gigi offered.

Three sets of eyes turned to stare at her.

"What? I'm trying to be optimistic!"

"Stop trying," Elizabeth advised. "Every time someone's optimistic, something worse happens."

"That's not how probability works."

"It's how our luck works."

Despite the bickering—or perhaps because of it, as a way to maintain morale—they prepared to head back toward the crash site. Elizabeth kept her sword ready, flames flickering at low burn to provide both light and deterrent. Cecilia maintained her vigilant watch. Raora's claws stayed extended. Gigi's gauntlets were up and ready.

They moved carefully through the snow, retracing their frantic flight path. Every shadow could be a yeti. Every distant sound might be another attack brewing.

But luck, for once, was with them. The yetis seemed to have truly retreated, at least for now. They found the crash site unmolested, their luggage still scattered where they'd dropped it.

"Quick gathering time," Elizabeth ordered. "Five minutes maximum. Get what you can carry and we leave."

They worked fast, grabbing clothes, supplies, any gear that had survived the crash. Elizabeth found a jacket in her luggage—thank god—and immediately put it on. The others followed suit, layering against the cold.

Cecilia discovered a waterproof bag containing energy bars and bottled water. "Food and hydration secured!"

"Good, because I was starting to contemplate snow-eating," Gigi said.

"Please don't eat the snow," Raora begged. "That's how you get hypothermia faster."

"Everything about this place gives you hypothermia faster!"

"Valid point."

With their luggage recovered—or at least the essential bits—they faced the next question: where to go?

"We need to head downslope," Elizabeth decided. "Lower elevation means warmer temperatures and hopefully some sign of civilization."

"And if there's no civilization?" Cecilia asked.

"Then we keep walking until there is. We didn't survive a plane crash and a yeti attack just to freeze to death on a mountain."

"That's the spirit," Raora said, trying for encouragement. "Aggressive determination in the face of certain doom."

"I wouldn't say certain," Elizabeth corrected.

"Probable doom?"

"...Acceptable."

They began their trek, following what looked like a natural path down the mountainside. The snow was still deep, the cold was still brutal, and there was still no guarantee they'd find anything helpful.

But they were together, they were armed, they'd survived their first major threat, and they had a direction to move in.

It wasn't much, but it was something.

"So," Gigi said after they'd been walking for about ten minutes. "Anyone want to start a conversation to pass the time?"

"Like what?" Cecilia asked.

"I don't know. Normal stuff. How about... favorite foods? What's everyone's favorite food?"

"Right now? Anything hot," Raora replied immediately. "Hot soup. Hot tea. Hot anything."

"Same," Elizabeth agreed. "I would commit crimes for a bowl of hot ramen right now."

"I'd settle for just being somewhere that my fingers don't hurt from cold," Cecilia added, flexing her free hand.

"We're all just discovering we're very food and temperature motivated," Gigi observed.

"Survival tends to clarify priorities," Elizabeth said dryly.

The conversation continued as they walked, topics ranging from the mundane to the absurd—anything to keep their minds off the cold, the danger, and the uncertainty of their situation.

They talked about their favorite streams, their worst technical difficulties, that one time Gigi accidentally muted herself for fifteen minutes and didn't notice. They debated whether a hot dog was a sandwich (Elizabeth: no, Cecilia: yes, Raora: "why are we having this argument?", Gigi: "because we're losing our minds").

As the sun began to set, painting the snow in shades of pink and orange, they crested a ridge and stopped.

Below them, maybe two miles down the mountainside, they could see lights.

Actual lights. As in artificial lighting. As in civilization.

"Is that..." Raora started.

"A village," Cecilia finished, disbelief in her voice. "That's a village."

"We found people!" Gigi actually jumped, despite her exhaustion. "We found actual people! We're saved!"

"Let's not celebrate until we get there," Elizabeth cautioned, but even she couldn't hide the relief in her voice. "It's still a couple miles and it'll be dark soon. But yeah. We found civilization."

They picked up their pace, exhaustion temporarily forgotten in the face of hope. The lights below beckoned like a beacon, promising warmth, shelter, and an end to their immediate survival crisis.

The path down was treacherous in the fading light, but they helped each other—calling out hazards, offering hands over difficult sections, keeping each other upright when someone slipped.

By the time they reached the outskirts of the village—which appeared to be some kind of mountain resort town based on the architecture—full darkness had fallen and they were running on pure determination.

The first building they reached was some kind of inn or lodge. Warm light spilled from its windows. Through the glass they could see people inside, normal people, going about normal activities like they hadn't just lived through a apocalyptic aircraft disaster.

Elizabeth pushed open the door, and four disheveled, snow-covered idols practically fell into the warmth of the building's interior.

Everyone inside stopped and stared at them.

"Hi," Elizabeth managed, her sword still somehow in her hand, her hair full of snow, her jacket torn from yeti claws. "We had a really bad day. Do you have rooms available?"

The desk clerk, to his credit, recovered quickly from the shock of four armed, frozen, clearly traumatized young women appearing in his lobby.

"I... yes?" he managed. "We have rooms. Do you need... medical attention? The police? Should I call someone?"

"Just rooms for now," Cecilia said, stepping forward with as much dignity as someone covered in snow could muster. "And maybe hot food. Lots of hot food."

"And hot tea," Raora added.

"Hot anything," Gigi contributed. "We're very cold."

The clerk nodded slowly, clearly deciding that asking questions could wait until these strange guests weren't at risk of hypothermia. "I'll... get you set up immediately. Please, come in properly. Sit by the fire."

There was a fireplace. An actual fireplace with actual fire. Elizabeth made a sound that might have been a sob of relief.

They collapsed into chairs near the hearth, weapons finally set aside, the warmth slowly seeping back into frozen limbs.

"We made it," Raora said softly. "We actually made it."

"We fought yetis," Gigi added, staring at the fire. "We fought actual yetis and survived."

"And won," Cecilia corrected. "We didn't just survive. We won."

“We didn't win yet,” Cecilia added “we still have to find a city with an airport,”

“Right, we rest first, and go down more of the mountain,” Roara said.

“Let's up their aren't anymore creatures.”

“don't jinx it.”

Elizabeth leaned back in her chair, finally letting herself relax. They were safe. They were warm. They were alive.

The others—Myth, Council, Advent—were still out there somewhere, dealing with their own survival situations. They'd have to figure out how to reunite, how to get home, how to explain all of this to their managers.

But for right now, in this moment, with the fire crackling and warmth returning and hot food on the way, they could just be.

Four idols who'd survived a plane crash, a yeti attack, and a mountain trek.

Four friends who'd made it through the impossible together.

"Next team building event," Elizabeth declared, her eyes closed, "we're doing bowling."

"Agreed," came the unanimous, exhausted response.

The fire crackled. The warmth continued to seep in. And somewhere in the distance, the mountains stood silent, holding their secrets and their yetis, while inside the lodge, Justice finally, finally rested.
