Chapter 8: The Long Road Home — Part One: Desert Pilgrimage (Myth's Transformation)

The Long Road Home
Part One: Desert Pilgrimage (Myth's Transformation)

The sand flew in every direction as five figures sprinted across the desert, their footprints disappearing almost as quickly as they were made. The sandstorm behind them roared like a living thing, consuming the landscape with hungry fury.

"INA!" Amelia's voice cut through the wind as they finally caught up to the purple-haired idol who had so wisely decided to flee ahead of them. "YOU TRAITOR! WHY ARE YOU RUNNING?!"

Ina didn't even slow down, her tentacles flowing behind her as she maintained her lead. "DO YOU THINK I WOULD WAIT?!" she called back, not a trace of guilt in her voice.

"WELL IF YOU THINK ABOUT IT—" Kiara started.

"EXACTLY!" Ina interrupted. "Plus! I'm sure we could probably survive!"

"PROBABLY?!" Calli wheezed, her scythe bouncing on her back as she ran. "PROBABLY ISN'T REASSURING, INA!"

"It's better than definitely not!" Ina shot back, still not slowing.

"That's the worst logic I've ever heard!" Amelia argued, adjusting Gura on her shoulder—they'd been taking turns carrying the increasingly delirious shark.

"Says the person who thought punching a time machine was a good troubleshooting method!"

"That was ONE TIME and it WORKED!"

"It exploded!"

"It worked THEN exploded! There's a difference!"

Kiara, running alongside them, couldn't help but laugh despite their dire situation. "Are we seriously having this argument while running from a sandstorm?!"

"Multi-tasking!" Ina replied cheerfully.

"I hate all of you," Calli gasped out, though there was no real venom in it.

Gura, still draped over Amelia's shoulder like a very dehydrated sack of potatoes, mumbled something incoherent about water and the ocean and why everything was so dry.

They continued their desperate flight, bickering the entire way because if they were going to die in a desert, they were at least going to die being themselves.

"Ina, I'm putting you in charge of watching horror movies alone as punishment!" Kiara declared.

"You wouldn't dare!"

"Try me!"

"That's psychological warfare!"

"You left us to die in a sandstorm!"

"I invited you to follow me! That's basically the same as waiting!"

"IT'S LITERALLY THE OPPOSITE!"

The argument might have continued indefinitely if Calli hadn't suddenly skidded to a halt, her eyes going wide.

"Uh, guys?" she said, her voice taking on a note of panic that made everyone else stop and look.

Ahead of them, rising from the desert like a wall of granular doom, was another sandstorm.

A second sandstorm.

Coming directly toward them.

From the opposite direction.

"You have GOT to be kidding me," Amelia said flatly.

They were trapped. Sandstorm behind them, sandstorm in front of them, and nowhere to go.

"OKAY, PANIC TIME!" Kiara announced, and panic they did.

"We're gonna die!"

"This is it!"

"I should have stayed in the Underworld!"

"Why is the desert so hostile?!"

"EVERYONE SHUT UP AND THINK!" Amelia's detective voice cut through the chaos. "We need a plan! Now!"

"Dig down!" Ame suggested immediately, her mind racing. "We dig into the sand, create an air pocket, wait it out!"

"Fly up!" Kiara countered, her phoenix wings already starting to manifest. "Get above the storm!"

"Slash through it!" Calli hefted her scythe. "We cut a path straight through!"

"Those are all terrible plans!" Ina protested.

"DO YOU HAVE A BETTER ONE?!" they all shouted at her.

"...No!"

The storms were closing in. They could feel the wind now from both directions, sand beginning to pelt them from two sides simultaneously.

"WE'RE RUNNING OUT OF OPTIONS!" Kiara screamed.

"Ame, Ina, get on!" Calli commanded, lowering her stance. "Kiara, you take point! We fly!"

"Flying it is!" Ame leaped onto Kiara's back while Ina grabbed Calli. "GO GO GO!"

Kiara's wings burst into full Phoenix glory, flames licking along the feathers as she launched upward. Ina's eldritch powers manifested as floating tentacles that lifted her and Calli. They began to ascend, trying to get above the converging storms.

But they weren't fast enough.

The two sandstorms met directly where they'd been standing, colliding with massive force. The interaction of two opposing storm systems created something worse—a vortex, a whirling tornado of sand that reached up toward them like a hungry hand.

"OH COME ON!" Ame yelled as they were caught in the updraft.

The sand tornado pulled them in, spinning them around its circumference like some kind of horrifying desert carousel. Up became down, left became right, and all of it was sand and wind and screaming.

"I'M GONNA BE SICK!" Kiara shrieked, her wings useless against the tornado's force.

"DON'T YOU DARE!" Ame clung to her back desperately.

"THIS IS THE WORST DAY EVER!" Calli's scythe was torn from her grip, spinning away into the storm.

"I REGRET EVERYTHING!" Ina's tentacles flailed uselessly.

Gura, still somehow on someone's back—whose, it was impossible to tell in the chaos—was making sounds that might have been words but were mostly just distressed shark noises.

They spun and spun, caught in the desert's fury, completely at the mercy of natural forces that seemed personally offended by their existence.

And then something changed.

"WAIT!" Calli shouted, or tried to—the wind stole most of her words. "LOOK AT GURA!"

Through the swirling sand, visible even in the chaos, Gura was beginning to glow.

Not the normal kind of glow. This was something else, something primal, a golden light that seemed to emanate from her very being.

"WHAT'S HAPPENING TO HER?!" Kiara managed to yell.

"I DON'T KNOW BUT IT'S GETTING BRIGHTER!" Ame yelled back.

The light intensified, growing from a soft glow to a brilliant radiance that cut through the sand like a beacon. Gura's form, still limp and dehydrated moments before, suddenly straightened. Her eyes, previously glazed with dehydration, snapped open.

But they weren't Gura's eyes anymore.

The light reached critical intensity, and everyone instinctively shut their eyes against the brilliance.

There was a sensation of stillness, of peace, of something fundamental shifting in the desert itself.

Then silence.

Actual silence.

No wind. No roaring. No sand pelting their skin.

Cautiously, they opened their eyes.

And gasped in unison.

Gura—or the being that had been Gura—was hovering in the center of where the tornado had been, perfectly calm, perfectly still. The storms had stopped. The sand hung suspended in the air around her, as if waiting for permission to fall.

But it wasn't Gura. Not anymore. Not exactly.

Her skin had transformed, taking on a sun-kissed tan that seemed to glow with internal warmth. Her eyes were rimmed with thick, dramatic eyelashes in the ancient Egyptian style, lined with kohl that made them look impossibly large and striking. Her shark tail had changed completely—no longer the blue-grey of an ocean predator, but a sandy brown marked with distinctive black spots, exactly like a sand shark.

Her usual hoodie had vanished, replaced by an elaborate chest wrap in the Egyptian style, white linen crossed and bound with golden thread. She wore loose black pants that billowed slightly, tucked into sandals that looked far too elegant for the desert. Her left ear bore a golden earring shaped like an ankh, gleaming in the sunlight.

And in her hand—called to her as if by thought alone—her trident had transformed as well. No longer a simple three-pronged spear, it had become an elaborate staff topped with a long, curved blade like a ceremonial khopesh, the ancient Egyptian sword.

She smiled—a confident, almost smug expression that Gura would never have worn—and with a casual gesture, commanded the suspended sand to simply... stop.

The sand fell harmlessly to the ground. The desert became peaceful. The storms dissipated as if they had never been.

Then she descended, landing with one knee down and one fist to the ground in a perfect heroic pose, her transformed weapon held dramatically to the side.

The rest of Myth, who had been dumped unceremoniously to the ground when the tornado stopped, could only stare.

"Uh," Amelia said eloquently. "What."

The transformed Gura stood, flipping her hair (which was longer now, flowing down her back in waves) and struck a dramatic pose, one hand on her hip.

"Call me Sura!" she announced, her voice carrying an accent that hadn't been there before—something ancient, regal, and completely at odds with Gura's usual gremlin energy. "I am the Shark of the Desert! The Princess of Ancient Egypt! The Goddess of Storms, Foreign Lands, and the Endless Sands! I rule all that you see before you, the dunes bow to my command, the winds obey my whims, and the very desert itself knows my name!"

She continued posing, seemingly expecting some kind of response.

Myth just stared.

Ame leaned toward the others, whispering, "Is this what happens when you place Gura in the desert?"

"Apparently she evolves," Calli whispered back, looking between the unconscious Gura-shape in her memory and this confident desert goddess before them.

"Like a Pokémon," Kiara added, fascinated despite herself.

Ina, her eyes narrowed in analysis, spoke up. "Look at her tail. That's definitely a sand shark pattern. And her whole aesthetic... it's like she adapted to the environment completely. Fascinating."

"I CAN HEAR YOU," Sura called out, still in that dramatic pose. "And yes, I have adapted. I am the perfect form for this domain. The desert recognizes its mistress!"

"Okay but are you still Gura?" Amelia asked, standing up and brushing sand off herself. "Like, in there somewhere?"

Sura's confident expression flickered slightly. "I... am Gura. But also not. I am what Gura becomes in the desert. I am the adaptation, the evolution, the—"

"So you're a desert alt costume," Kiara summarized.

"That is a gross oversimplification of a divine transformation!"

"But accurate?"

"...Technically."

Ina stepped forward, studying Sura with clinical interest. "You're a completely different persona, aren't you? Different memories, different personality, different capabilities."

Sura nodded regally. "Indeed. When the ocean meets the desert, transformation occurs. I am who Gura must become to survive here."

"Can you get us out of here?" Calli asked bluntly. "Because that's really what matters right now."

Sura's smug smile returned. "But of course! I own this desert! Every grain of sand answers to me!"

"You own it?" Amelia asked skeptically.

"OBSERVE!"

With a dramatic flourish, Sura dove into the sand as if it were water, disappearing beneath the surface. A moment later, a shark fin—sandy brown with black spots—emerged, cutting through the sand like it was an ocean.

"FOLLOW ME!" her muffled voice called from beneath the dunes.

The members of Myth looked at each other.

"Well," Kiara said. "This is our life now, apparently."

"Following a desert-adapted Gura through sand," Ame agreed.

"I'm adding this to the list of 'Things I Never Expected to Do,'" Calli muttered.

"That list must be getting pretty long," Ina observed.

"You have no idea."

They followed the fin, which moved through the sand with surprising speed, leading them across dunes and around rock formations. The journey, which would have been brutal under the sun, was somehow made bearable by Sura's presence—wherever she passed, the temperature seemed to cool slightly, the harshness of the desert mellowing.

After about thirty minutes of following their desert-adapted friend through the wasteland, they crested a dune and stopped.

Below them, nestled in a valley between sand formations, was a village.

An abandoned village, from the looks of it—buildings weathered by sand and time, structures that might have once been homes now half-buried by dunes. But it was shelter. It was civilization, even if long-departed.

"There!" Sura emerged from the sand dramatically, pointing at the village. "We can rest there!"

They descended into the abandoned settlement, finding it surprisingly intact despite its obvious age. Some buildings were fully furnished, protected by closed doors from the worst of the sand. There was even a well in the center of the village, though whether it still had water was anyone's guess.

They claimed one of the larger buildings as a temporary base, setting down their salvaged supplies and finally, finally allowing themselves to collapse.

"Okay," Amelia said, sprawling across what might have once been a merchant's counter. "Status check. Everyone alive?"

"Unfortunately," Calli replied from where she was sitting against a wall.

"Alive and confused," Kiara added, eyeing Sura, who was examining the building with interest.

"I'm processing," Ina said simply.

Sura, meanwhile, was pointing at the well outside. "That will have water. Desert wells go deep. We can refresh ourselves."

"Finally, some good news," Amelia groaned, pulling herself up.

They gathered at the well, and sure enough, the bucket system still worked. The water was cool and clear when they pulled it up—desert water, mineral-rich and clean.

Sura looked at the water with an expression that was hard to read. "Adequate," she declared, though she made no move to drink any herself.

Amelia, watching this, got an idea.

"Hey Sura," she said casually, filling the bucket. "Catch."

She threw the water at the desert goddess.

"AAAH! WATER!" Sura hissed, recoiling as the water hit her.

And then, in a flash of light, she transformed back.

Gura stood there, dripping wet, looking confused and disoriented. "Wha... what happened?" she blinked at them. "Why am I wet? Where are we? I thought we got hit by the sandstorm?"

The others stared.

"Oh," Ina said slowly. "Oh, that's interesting."

"What's interesting?" Gura wrung out her hoodie—wait, she was wearing her normal hoodie again—looking around in confusion. "Why are you all looking at me like that?"

"Ina," Amelia said, her detective mind already working. "Dump sand on her."

"What? Why?" Gura backed up. "What's going on?"

"We need Sura," Ina explained, already scooping up sand.

"Who's Sura?!"

"You'll see," Kiara said, trying not to laugh.

Ina tossed the sand at Gura.

"Ah!" Gura sputtered as sand covered her.

The transformation happened again—golden light, shifting features, and suddenly Sura stood there instead, looking supremely confident.

"What happened?!" Sura demanded, looking around. "Why am I back? What—" She noticed the wet ground. "Did someone throw water on me?!"

"It's nothing," Calli said quickly, exchanging glances with the others.

"Don't lie to your goddess!" Sura declared dramatically.

"We're not lying, we're just... omitting details," Amelia said.

"That's the same thing!"

"No it's not," Ina countered. "Lying is active deception. Omission is passive. There's a philosophical difference."

"I don't care about your philosophy! What did you do?!"

"We discovered you have a very convenient weakness," Kiara explained. "Water turns you back into regular Gura. Sand turns Gura into you."

Sura's confident expression cracked. "You... you can just turn me back and forth?"

"Yep!" Amelia grinned. "Instant transformation. It's like having a desert switch."

"This is undignified! I am a goddess!"

"A goddess with a weakness to water," Calli pointed out. "Kind of ironic for a shark."

"I AM A DESERT SHARK! OF COURSE I HAVE AN AVERSION TO—" Sura stopped, recognizing she was arguing their point. "This conversation is over. We're resting here for three hours, then I'm leading you to civilization."

"Can we keep you as Sura?" Ina asked seriously. "You're much more capable than regular Gura in this environment."

"HEY!" Gura's voice somehow came from Sura. "I can hear you in here! What does that mean?! I'm plenty capable!"

"Do you trust that face?" Ina asked the others, pointing at where regular Gura would be.

They all exchanged glances.

"Fair point," Kiara admitted. "Sorry Gura, but desert you is way more functional."

"I'M RIGHT HERE!"

"We know," Amelia patted Sura's shoulder. "And we appreciate you. But Sura has the survival skills we need right now."

"This is bullying," Gura-via-Sura complained.

"This is pragmatism," Ina corrected.

They settled into the building, using the time to properly organize their supplies, clean their weapons, and recover from their ordeal. Sura, despite her complaints, provided valuable information about the desert—where they were approximately, which direction led to civilization, how to identify dangerous areas.

"The desert ends to the east," she explained, pointing through a window. "Beyond that is a city—a real one, modern, with people and technology and everything you need. We can reach it by tomorrow if we travel through the night."

"Through the night?" Calli asked. "Is that safe?"

"Safer than traveling during the day," Sura replied. "The desert cools at night. And with me guiding you, we'll avoid the dangers."

"What dangers?" Kiara asked nervously.

"Better you don't know. Ignorance is bliss."

"That's not comforting!"

"I'm not here to comfort you. I'm here to guide you."

After their rest period, they prepared to depart. They were gathering their supplies when Amelia noticed something that made her laugh.

"Guys, look what I found in one of the buildings."

She held up several pieces of cloth—old, weathered robes in the desert style, perfect for protecting against sun and sand.

"No," Calli said immediately.

"Yes," Amelia countered.

"We are NOT dressing up like some kind of desert movie."

"We absolutely are," Kiara said, already grabbing a robe. "When in Rome—or in this case, when in the desert..."

Ten minutes later, they set out across the sands, each member of Myth wearing flowing desert robes that made them look like they'd stepped out of an adventure film. They walked in a line, Sura leading the way, the sun beginning to set behind them painting everything in gold and red.

And then Ina pulled out a flute.

"Where did you get that?" Amelia asked.

"Found it in the village," Ina replied. "Thought it might be useful."

"For what?"

Ina brought it to her lips and began to play. The melody that emerged was instantly recognizable—"Arabian Nights," slow and atmospheric.

"Oh my god," Calli said. "Are you serious right now?"

"We're having a cinematic moment whether you like it or not," Ina replied, continuing to play.

And so they walked across the desert in slow motion—or it felt like slow motion—with dramatic music, flowing robes, and a confident desert goddess leading the way. The sun set behind them, the stars began to emerge, and the scene was so ridiculously cinematic that they couldn't help but embrace it.

"This is so extra," Kiara said, trying not to laugh.

"This is perfect," Amelia countered.

"I feel like we should be in a trailer," Calli added.

"Holomyth: Desert Adventure," Ina narrated between flute phrases. "Coming never to a theater near you."

Sura, walking ahead of them, called back without turning. "Are you all finished being ridiculous?"

"Never!" they chorused.

She sighed, but there was amusement in it. "Mortals. Even the desert goddess cannot change their nature."

"You're one of us!" Gura's voice protested from within Sura.

"I am elevated beyond such—"

"You're literally me!"

"SILENCE, LESSER SELF!"

The others dissolved into laughter, the sound carrying across the peaceful evening desert as they walked toward civilization, toward home, toward whatever came next.
