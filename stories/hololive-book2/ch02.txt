Part Two: When Pigeons Attack

The first sign of trouble was a sound.

It wasn't the normal, comfortable hum of the engines that had provided background ambiance for the past hour. This was different—a sort of wet thunk that echoed through the cabin with disturbing clarity.

Ina looked up from her tablet, head tilted slightly. "Did anyone else hear—"

THUNK.

Another impact, harder this time.

THUNK. THUNK. THUNK.

"What the hell is that?" Calli was on her feet now, looking toward the windows.

"Oh my god," Kiara pressed her face against the glass, her eyes going wide. "Are those—"

"PIGEONS!" Gura shrieked, also plastered against a window. "There are PIGEONS flying at like 30,000 feet! How is that even possible?!"

And indeed, through the windows, the most absurd and horrifying sight became visible: dozens—no, hundreds—of pigeons, flying at impossible altitudes, slamming themselves directly into the plane's engines with what could only be described as suicidal determination.

"That's not normal pigeon behavior," Amelia observed, her detective instincts kicking in even as her voice rose with concern. "Pigeons don't fly this high. They physically can't—"

"Well THESE ones apparently didn't get the memo!" Gura yelled.

The impacts were coming faster now. Thunk thunk thunk thunk thunk—a rapid-fire assault of kamikaze birds meeting metal at high velocity.

"Why are they doing this?!" Fauna had stood up, her connection to nature providing exactly zero helpful information about why birds were committing mass suicide via airplane. "This doesn't make sense!"

"When has anything we do ever made sense?" Bae pointed out, but her usual cheer was strained.

The plane shuddered. It wasn't a gentle turbulence bump—this was a violent, whole-body jerk that sent unsecured items flying.

"Attention passengers," the pilot's voice came over the intercom, admirably calm despite the circumstances. "We're experiencing some... uh... bird strike issues. Please return to your seats and fasten your seatbelts. We may be making an emergency landing."

"'Some bird strike issues'?" Kronii repeated incredulously. "SOME? There are like a thousand birds out there!"

"1,247," Mumei said absently, her eyes tracking the swarm. "I counted."

"HOW IS THAT HELPFUL RIGHT NOW, MUMEI?"

Another massive shudder ran through the plane, accompanied by a sound that no one wanted to hear at altitude: the grinding, sputtering death rattle of an engine failing.

"Okay, that's not good," IRyS said, her voice tight. "That's really not good."

"Everyone stay calm!" Elizabeth called out from the Justice section, trying to project confidence. "I'm sure the pilots have this under control—"

The plane dropped.

Not slowly. Not gradually. Just dropped, like a stone, like the hand of gravity had reached up and yanked them downward with personal malice.

Screaming. There was a lot of screaming.

"I TAKE IT BACK!" Elizabeth shrieked. "THEY DON'T HAVE IT UNDER CONTROL!"

"WE'RE GONNA DIE!" Gura wailed, clinging to her seat with both hands.

"We're not gonna die," Amelia tried to say, but her knuckles were white on her armrests and her voice was barely audible over the chaos.

More pigeons. The windows were practically covered in feathered bodies now, each impact adding to the growing list of mechanical failures rippling through the aircraft.

"I just want to point out," Bae said, somehow maintaining her composure even as she gripped her dice bag like a lifeline, "that this is literally the most chaotic thing that's ever happened to us."

"NOW IS NOT THE TIME FOR YOUR CHAOS PRIDE!" Kronii shouted back.

The plane was falling faster now, spinning slightly, and through the windows they could see they were losing altitude rapidly. The ground—or rather, a variety of different types of ground—was coming up way too fast.

"Is it just me," Shiori called out, her voice remarkably steady, "or are we breaking apart?"

She was right. Those strange sounds—groaning metal, snapping bolts, hissing hydraulics—weren't just damage. They were separation.

"BREAKING APART?!" Nerissa grabbed her seat. "PLANES AREN'T SUPPOSED TO BREAK APART!"

"Wait," Sana said, her eyes widening. "Wait, I remember reading about this model. It's got a—oh no."

"WHAT? OH NO WHAT?" multiple voices demanded.

"It's got an experimental safety feature," Sana explained, her voice rising. "Detachable sections! If the plane is going down, different sections can separate and deploy independent parachutes to—"

CRACK.

The cabin split. Literally split, clean breaks appearing between sections with mechanical precision that would have been impressive if it wasn't absolutely terrifying.

"EXPERIMENTAL?!" Fauna screamed. "WHO THOUGHT THIS WAS A GOOD IDEA?!"

The sections were separating now, rapidly, each group's area becoming its own individual unit. Through the gaps they could see each other being pulled apart, the airplane essentially coming apart like expensive, terrifying Lego.

"WELL THIS HAS BEEN FUN!" Calli shouted to the other groups as Myth's section drifted away. "LET'S NEVER DO IT AGAIN!"

"AGREED!" multiple voices chorused back.

Bae, in the Council section, calmly pulled out a bag of popcorn from seemingly nowhere and started eating, her expression one of resigned acceptance. "Well, if we're gonna die, might as well enjoy the show."

"WHERE DID YOU GET POPCORN?!" Kronii demanded.

"I'm always prepared!" Bae replied, offering the bag. "Want some?"

The sections were falling now, truly falling, each group's piece of the aircraft descending toward completely different parts of the planet below. Parachutes deployed from each section—at least that part of the safety feature worked—but they were still falling fast, still spinning, and definitely not heading toward the same location.

"I HATE FLYING!" Gigi screamed from the Justice section as they spiraled away from the others.

"I LOVE YOU ALL!" Fuwawa called out emotionally to the other groups, tears in her eyes.

"WE'RE NOT DYING!" Mococo insisted, though she didn't sound convinced.

The ground was very close now. Each section was heading toward drastically different terrain—desert, ocean, forest, snow—and there was nothing any of them could do but hold on and hope.

The last thing any of them saw before impact was the scattered pieces of their airplane, separated by miles of sky, each containing a group of terrified idols.

Then everything went dark.
