Chapter 11: The Long Road Home — Part Four: Mountain Gauntlet (Justice's Boss Rush)

The Long Road Home
Part Four: Mountain Gauntlet (Justice's Boss Rush)

After resting in the village, they decided to move on to find a city downhill.

And it wasn't a safe journey as they hope for.

Elizabeth's greatsword clashed against stone with a sound like a church bell breaking. The golem—because of course it was a golem, because their lives had become a fantasy RPG—swung its massive fist in retaliation.

"LEFT!" Cecilia called out, and Elizabeth dodged left, the fist passing close enough to feel the wind of its passage.

"How many of these things are there?!" Raora yelled, her claws raking across another golem's leg, pink energy trailing behind each strike.

"I stopped counting after twelve!" Gigi shouted back, her oversized gauntlets blocking a blow that would have shattered a normal person's arms.

They were surrounded by golems—massive stone constructs that had emerged from the mountainside like the world's worst surprise party. Each one stood easily fifteen feet tall, their bodies carved from living rock, their eyes glowing with some kind of internal light.

And they were angry.

Elizabeth's blade blazed with blue flames as she struck, the fire causing the stone to crack and split. "We need to break through! There's a pass ahead!"

"I SEE IT!" Cecilia thrust her white lance forward, the weapon glowing as she channeled energy through it. "Everyone to me! I'll provide cover!"

They clustered together, backs to each other, a small circle of defiance in a sea of stone enemies. Cecilia's lance began to transform, shifting and flowing until it became a violin. She brought it to her chin and began to play.

The music that emerged was impossible—powerful, resonant, filled with magic that none of them fully understood but all of them could feel. Buffs manifested around each member of Justice: Elizabeth felt her strength increase, her flames burn hotter; Raora's speed doubled, her reflexes sharpening to supernatural levels; Gigi's defense solidified, her gauntlets becoming virtually indestructible.

"CHARGE!" Elizabeth roared, and they charged.

The battle that followed was brutal and beautiful in equal measure. Elizabeth led the assault, her greatsword carving through stone like it was butter, each strike leaving trails of blue fire that refused to extinguish. She was a whirlwind of destruction, never stopping, never slowing, pure offensive fury.

Cecilia alternated between her violin and lance, providing buffs while also striking with precision when opportunities arose. She was the glue holding their formation together, the support that kept everyone fighting at peak efficiency.

Raora was speed and assassination, her smaller size letting her slip past golem guards to strike weak points—joints, eyes, anywhere that would cause maximum damage with minimal exposure. Her pink claws found every vulnerability, exploited every opening.

And Gigi was the immovable wall, positioning herself to intercept the heaviest blows, her gauntlets ringing like bells with each impact but never breaking, never failing. She was the anchor that let the others take risks.

They fought like they'd been training together for years instead of just doing idol streams. Every callout was answered, every opening exploited, every danger covered.

"BEHIND YOU!" Cecilia called, and Elizabeth spun, bisecting a golem that had tried to flank her.

"Thanks! Gigi, incoming heavy!"

"Got it!" Gigi braced, catching a massive overhead blow that cratered the ground around her but left her standing firm.

"Raora, that one on the left!"

"Already on it!" Raora was a blur, striking five times in two seconds before backflipping away.

Slowly, impossibly, they fought their way through. Golems fell to fire, precision, speed, and sheer stubborn defense. The pass ahead grew closer.

With one final coordinated assault—Elizabeth's flames, Cecilia's magic, Raora's speed, Gigi's power—they shattered the last golem between them and freedom.

They ran through the pass, breathing hard, and stopped to catch their breath on the other side.

"That," Cecilia gasped, "was insane."

"That was awesome," Gigi corrected, grinning despite her exhaustion.

"That was terrifying," Raora added.

"That was necessary," Elizabeth finished. "And it's not over. Look."

She pointed ahead. Through the snow, slinking between rocks, were shapes. Large, feline shapes with spotted white fur and ice-blue eyes.

"Snow leopards," Elizabeth identified. "Mountain variety. Territorial, aggressive, and there's at least six of them."

"Of course there are," Cecilia sighed. "Why would we get a break?"

The snow leopards attacked without warning, moving as a coordinated pack. Justice responded instantly, their RPG party formation solidifying even further through necessity.

Elizabeth took point, her flames creating a barrier the leopards hesitated to cross. Cecilia provided buffs and healing, her music flowing constantly now. Raora matched the leopards' speed, engaging them on their own terms. Gigi held the defensive line, ensuring nothing got past to their squishier members.

The fight was faster than the golems but no less intense. Snow leopards were smart, coordinated, and knew their terrain. They used the rocks for cover, attacked from multiple angles, tried to isolate individuals from the group.

But Justice had learned. They didn't fall for feints, didn't overextend, maintained their formation with practiced efficiency.

"They're pulling back!" Raora called out after ten minutes of intense combat.

Indeed, the surviving leopards were retreating, recognizing that these particular prey items were more trouble than they were worth.

"Everyone okay?" Elizabeth asked, scanning her team.

"Scratched but functional," Cecilia reported.

"I'm good!" Gigi said, though her gauntlets now bore several deep claw marks.

"Alive," Raora confirmed. "But I really, really want to be somewhere warm and not full of predators."

"Agreed. Let's keep—" Elizabeth stopped, staring ahead. "Is that a castle?"

It was. Rising from the mountain like something out of a dark fairy tale, a massive castle of black stone and ice, its towers piercing the sky, its walls covered in frost and ancient power.

"That's definitely abandoned," Gigi said. "Right? That has to be abandoned."

"Only one way to find out," Elizabeth said grimly.

They approached cautiously, weapons ready. The castle's gates stood open—never a good sign—leading into a courtyard filled with snow and silence.

The throne room was easy to find. They followed the main path, through corridors lined with frozen tapestries and ice sculptures that might have once been guards. Everything was covered in frost, preserved by the cold, empty.

Until they reached the throne.

On it sat a figure in black armor, a massive greatsword—even larger than Elizabeth's—resting across its lap. The figure was motionless, covered in ice and frost, clearly long dead.

"Okay," Raora whispered. "That's creepy but not dangerous. We should just—"

The figure's eyes opened.

They glowed red in the darkness. The ice around it cracked and fell away. The massive sword lifted, pointing directly at them.

The dead king rose.

"RUN!" Elizabeth screamed.

They didn't run. They couldn't—the throne room doors slammed shut behind them with a boom that echoed like a death knell.

The dead king stood to its full height—easily nine feet tall in its armor—and charged.

What followed was the hardest fight of their lives.

The dead king was powerful, skilled, and seemingly tireless. Its massive greatsword swung with devastating force, each blow powerful enough to shatter stone. It moved with the grace of a master swordsman despite its size, anticipating their attacks, countering with brutal efficiency.

Elizabeth met it head-on, blue flames blazing as her greatsword clashed with its dark blade. The impact sent shockwaves through the throne room, cracking the floor.

"Why are we fighting like it's Fire Emblem?!" Gigi yelled, positioning herself to guard Cecilia while the bard provided support.

"Who cares!" Elizabeth roared back, blocking another massive blow. "Just survive!"

Cecilia's violin sang with desperate intensity, pouring buffs into her teammates while also trying to heal the damage they were taking. Elizabeth was already bleeding from a dozen cuts, Raora had a nasty gash on her arm, Gigi's gauntlets were cracking.

Raora darted in and out, striking the king's armor joints, looking for any weakness. "It's not slowing down!"

"Keep hitting it!" Elizabeth commanded, her flames intensifying. "Everything falls eventually!"

The battle raged, minutes stretching into what felt like hours. They swore they could see an HP bar above the king's head, though it was probably just exhaustion-induced hallucination. The bar slowly, painfully depleted as they poured everything they had into the fight.

And then, when it reached zero, the king staggered.

"IT'S WORKING!" Gigi shouted.

The king's armor began to crack, red light pouring from the fissures. It raised its sword one final time—

And transformed.

The armor shattered, revealing a form of pure dark energy underneath. The greatsword grew even larger, wreathed in shadows. The red eyes blazed brighter.

"Phase two," Elizabeth said flatly. "Of course there's a phase two."

"I think we're hallucinating," Raora gasped. "This can't be real."

"Hallucination or not, it's trying to kill us!" Cecilia's music reached a crescendo, desperate healing flowing to her teammates. "Keep fighting!"

They did. They had no choice.

The second phase was worse. Faster, stronger, more aggressive. The shadow king didn't just swing its sword—it commanded darkness, sent waves of energy, created zones of corrupted ice that drained their strength.

But Justice adapted. Elizabeth's flames burned brighter, her fire the only thing that could fully dispel the shadows. Cecilia's buffs stacked higher, pushing everyone beyond their normal limits. Raora found patterns in the chaos, striking in the half-seconds between attacks. Gigi became truly immovable, her defense absolute.

Slowly, incredibly, they began to win.

The shadow king's movements grew more erratic, its attacks more desperate. The darkness surrounding it flickered and faded.

With one final, coordinated assault—all four of them striking simultaneously, all their power focused on one point—they pierced its core.

The shadow king froze. The darkness dissipated. The massive form crumbled to ash.

The throne room doors opened.

They collapsed, breathing hard, barely able to move.

"Did we just..." Gigi started.

"Fight a Dark Souls boss?" Raora finished. "Yes. Yes we did."

"And won," Cecilia added weakly.

"And hallucinated HP bars," Elizabeth muttered. "Or maybe we didn't hallucinate them. At this point, who knows."

They lay there for several minutes, too exhausted to move.

"We should go," Elizabeth finally said. "Before something else tries to kill us."

They dragged themselves up and out of the castle, back into the snow.

Ahead, impossibly, they saw a volcano. An active volcano, in the middle of a snow-covered mountain range, because their lives made no sense anymore.

"No," Raora said. "Absolutely not. We're not fighting a volcano."

They fought entities near the volcano. Fire spirits that emerged from lava flows, creatures of pure heat that clashed against the cold. Elizabeth was in her element here, her flames matching theirs, her power reaching new heights.

They kept walking, kept fighting. Snow fairies that tried to freeze them solid—fought and defeated. Skeleton dwarves in a cave that ambushed them—fought and defeated. Everything the mountain threw at them, they overcame.

And then, on the edge of a cliff with the wind howling around them, they faced their final challenge.

An ice dragon.

Massive, ancient, powerful beyond anything they'd fought so far. Its scales were glacial blue, its eyes contained the cold of a thousand winters, its roar shook the mountain itself.

"You have got to be kidding me," Gigi said, staring up at the creature.

"We've come this far," Elizabeth said, raising her greatsword one more time despite her exhaustion. "One more fight."

"One more boss," Cecilia agreed, her violin appearing in her hands.

"Let's finish this," Raora said, her claws extending.

"For home," Gigi added, her gauntlets glowing.

The dragon fight was epic. They dodged ice breath, avoided tail swipes, climbed the creature's body to strike at weak points. Elizabeth's flames provided their only real damage, burning through the dragon's natural ice armor. Cecilia kept everyone alive through sheer willpower and healing magic. Raora distracted the dragon, drawing its attention while others struck. Gigi tanked hits that should have killed her, standing firm through attacks that would have obliterated buildings.

Four hours. The fight lasted four hours, through exhaustion and pain and desperation.

But they won.

The ice dragon fell, its massive form crashing to the mountainside with enough force to trigger a small avalanche.

Justice stood victorious, barely, more dead than alive but standing.

"Is it over?" Raora asked weakly.

"Please let it be over," Cecilia whispered.

Elizabeth looked ahead and saw, through the snow, lights. City lights.

"It's over," she confirmed. "We made it."

They stumbled down the mountain, supporting each other, their weapons dragging behind them. The city grew closer, revealing itself to be a modern mountain town, civilization, safety.

They found the airport, walked in looking like they'd fought their way through every fantasy RPG ever made—because they had.

Elizabeth pulled out her phone. The screen was cracked, spiderwebbed with damage, but it still worked. She navigated to an airline website with shaking fingers, selected tickets to their home city, and paid with digital currency.

"Four tickets," she said. "Leaving in two hours."

"I could kiss you," Gigi said.

"Please don't. I'm too tired to respond appropriately."

They went through security, which was awkward given their weapons and general appearance of having been through a war zone. Several explanations later—heavily edited—they were allowed through, their weapons checked as specialized sporting equipment.

They found their gate, collapsed into the seats, and simply existed for a while.

When their flight was called, they boarded like zombies, found their seats, and immediately slumped into them.

The plane began to taxi. Someone asked if they needed anything. They declined, already half-asleep.

As the plane took off, carrying them home, the four members of Justice finally, finally allowed themselves to relax.

They'd survived. They'd fought through an entire RPG's worth of enemies. They'd defeated bosses, completed quests, and emerged victorious.

And now, they were going home.

"Next vacation," Elizabeth mumbled, her eyes already closed, "we're going to a beach."

"A beach with no monsters," Cecilia added.

"And no bosses," Raora said.

"And no fighting," Gigi finished.

"Agreed," they all said together.

The plane flew on, carrying four exhausted warriors home, their adventure finally, blessedly, over.
