Part Three: Desert Survival (Myth Minus One)

Five hours.

That's how long it took for consciousness to return to the members of Holomyth, scattered across burning sands under a merciless sun.

Amelia was the first to wake, her detective instincts pulling her from unconsciousness with the insistent warning that something was Very Wrong. She groaned, pushing herself up from the sand, every muscle protesting the movement.

"Ow," she muttered, taking stock of herself. Nothing broken, miraculously. Bruised, definitely. Sandy in places she really didn't want to think about? Absolutely. But alive.

She looked around, her vision swimming slightly before focusing on the absolute nightmare of their situation.

Desert. They'd crashed in a fucking desert.

The section of the plane they'd been in was scattered across the sand in pieces, like some kind of modern art installation dedicated to aviation disasters. Their luggage was everywhere, contents spilled and scattered, already being invaded by sand.

"Guys?" Amelia called out, her voice raspy. "Anyone conscious?"

A groan answered her. Calli was stirring about twenty feet away, one arm thrown over her eyes to block out the brutal sun.

"Am I dead?" Calli asked. "This feels like being dead."

"If you were dead, it probably wouldn't hurt this much," Amelia pointed out, stumbling over to help her up.

"Valid point." Calli accepted the hand, pulling herself to her feet with a wince. "Okay, status check. I'm alive, you're alive, where's everyone else?"

"Kiara!" Amelia spotted the phoenix about thirty yards away, already stirring. "Gura! Wake up!"

Kiara sat up slowly, sand cascading from her hair and clothes. "I'm awake, I'm awake. Holy shit, what happened? Where are we?"

"Desert," Amelia supplied unhelpfully. "We're in a desert."

"I can see that, detective." Kiara stood, brushing sand off with limited success. "I meant more like, which desert? How far did we fall? Are the others okay?"

"Don't know, don't know, and probably not," Amelia ticked off on her fingers. "Where's Gura?"

They found her collapsed on a dune, face-down in the sand.

"Gura!" Kiara rushed over, rolling her over carefully. The shark girl's face was flushed, her breathing rapid and shallow. "Oh no. Oh no no no."

"She's dehydrated," Calli said, kneeling beside her. "Sharks need water constantly. Being in a desert is basically the worst possible scenario for her."

"Gura, come on, wake up," Kiara tried, gently shaking her shoulder.

Gura's eyes fluttered open, unfocused. "Thirsty," she croaked. "So thirsty. Why is everything dry? Make it stop being dry."

"We're working on it," Amelia promised, though she had no idea how they were going to accomplish that. She looked around frantically, assessing their supplies. "Okay, we need to find our stuff. See what survived."

The search was depressing. Amelia's luggage had exploded across the sand, her carefully organized equipment scattered to the winds. Her revolver—her trusty six-shooter that had been through countless adventures—was half-buried in sand, the grains already working their way into the mechanism.

"Great," she muttered, picking it up and trying to brush it clean. "Just great. This is going to take hours to fix."

Calli's situation wasn't much better. Her luggage was in a similar state of disarray, and her scythe—her beautiful, deadly scythe—was embedded in what might have been the only dead tree in this entire desert.

"How?" Calli demanded, staring at it. "How did it end up in the one tree? We crashed in open desert! Where did the tree even come from?!"

"Physics doesn't apply to us anymore," Amelia said tiredly. "Just accept it."

Kiara's discovery was somehow worse. "My sword and shield are over there," she pointed to where her orange blade and blue shield lay on the sand, easily thirty feet away. "And my luggage—" she paused, staring in disbelief. "Is that a lizard?"

It was indeed a lizard. A fairly large desert lizard, and it appeared to be eating Kiara's suitcase. Actively chewing on it, the fabric and contents disappearing into its mouth with enthusiastic gulps.

"HEY!" Kiara started toward it, arms waving. "That's mine! Stop eating my stuff!"

The lizard looked at her, continued chewing, and leisurely waddled away with what remained of the suitcase still in its mouth.

"I'm being robbed by a lizard," Kiara said flatly. "A literal lizard is robbing me."

"Could be worse," Calli offered.

"How?!"

"Could be two lizards."

"Not. Helping. Calli."

Meanwhile, Gura was attempting to stand, her legs shaking. "Guys, I feel like death. Actually, I feel like death adjacent. Death's next-door neighbor."

"Your trident is over there," Amelia pointed to where the weapon was embedded in a large boulder, because of course it was. "Very heroically stuck in a rock."

"Can't," Gura wheezed. "Too dehydrated. Everything is spinning. There's two of you now, Ame."

"That's not good," Amelia muttered.

"Wait," Calli said suddenly, her eyes going wide. "Where's Ina?"

They all froze.

"INA!" Kiara called out, spinning around. "INA, WHERE ARE YOU?"

Silence. Just wind and sand.

"Oh god," Amelia felt panic rising. "We need to find her. She could be hurt, she could be—"

"Look!" Calli pointed to the horizon. "Is that—"

It was. Far in the distance, perhaps a mile away, a small purple-haired figure was visible.

"INA!" they all screamed in unison.

But then they noticed what else was in that direction.

"Is that a sandstorm?" Kiara asked slowly.

It was. A massive wall of sand and wind, rolling toward them like an angry god, and it was definitely going to reach them in a matter of minutes.

"Fuck," Amelia said succinctly.

"Grab everything important!" Calli ordered, already running to retrieve her scythe from the tree. "Weapons, supplies, anything we can carry!"

"And Gura!" Kiara added, rushing to the dehydrated shark. "We need to grab Gura!"

What followed was perhaps the most chaotic two minutes of their lives—and considering they'd just survived a plane crash caused by suicidal pigeons, that was saying something.

Amelia stuffed her sand-covered revolver in her belt and grabbed what intact luggage she could find. Calli yanked her scythe free from the tree with enough force to nearly topple herself. Kiara retrieved her sword and shield, throwing one last angry look at the lizard that had stolen her suitcase.

"Gura, we're moving!" Amelia announced.

"Don't wanna," Gura mumbled. "Sand comfortable. Gonna die here."

"You're not dying here," Kiara declared, and simply picked up the smaller girl, throwing her over her shoulder in a fireman's carry. "Calli, get her trident!"

Calli ran to the boulder, gripping the trident and pulling. It didn't budge.

"Are you kidding me?!" she yelled at the weapon. "We don't have time for this!"

With a desperate heave, she wrenched it free, nearly falling backward from the momentum.

The sandstorm was closer now, the roar of wind audible even from this distance.

"RUN!" Amelia screamed, and they ran.

Three idols, carrying an increasingly delirious fourth idol and whatever supplies they'd managed to grab, sprinting across burning sand while a sandstorm bore down on them like nature's personal vendetta.

Gura, bouncing on Kiara's shoulder, started shrieking—a high-pitched, gremlin sound that somehow cut through even the howling wind.

"AAAAAAAAHHHHHHH!" Gura contributed helpfully.

"NOT! HELPING!" Kiara gasped out between breaths.

"RUNNING! RUNNING SO FAST!" Gura continued, still shrieking. "WE'RE ALL GONNA DIE!"

"POSITIVE THOUGHTS!" Calli yelled.

"WE'RE GONNA DIE POSITIVELY!"

Far ahead of them, Ina slowly came to consciousness.

She sat up, brushed sand from her hair with the kind of calm that suggested she either hadn't fully processed their situation or had immediately achieved some kind of enlightenment about it.

Her luggage was scattered around her, but unlike the others, Ina had a distinct advantage: her tome, her connection to the Ancient Ones, gave her access to something extremely useful.

Pocket dimensions.

With practiced efficiency, she simply opened small tears in reality and deposited her scattered belongings into the void, where they would remain safely stored until needed.

"Much better," she said to herself, dusting off her hands.

Then she looked up and saw, in the distance, her fellow Myth members.

Specifically, she saw three people running at full speed, one of them carrying Gura (who appeared to be screaming), all of them loaded down with supplies and weapons, fleeing from an approaching sandstorm.

Ina stared.

She blinked.

She processed this image slowly, carefully.

"What the..." she started to say.

The screaming got louder. She could now make out individual voices:

"WHY IS THERE A SANDSTORM?!"

"BECAUSE THE UNIVERSE HATES US!"

"AAAAAAAAHHHHHHH!"

Ina watched them for exactly three more seconds.

Then, with the kind of calm decision-making that defined her entire personality, she smiled serenely, closed her eyes, turned exactly 180 degrees, and started running in the opposite direction at full speed.

"Nope," she said pleasantly to herself as she ran. "Absolutely not. I did not survive a plane crash to die in a sandstorm. Not today, Ancient Ones. Not today."

Behind her, the screaming continued to grow closer.

"IS THAT INA?!" she heard Amelia yell.

"INA, WAIT!" Kiara called.

"INA'S ABANDONING US!" Calli accused.

"TACTICAL RETREAT!" Ina called back, not slowing down. "IT'S CALLED SURVIVAL INSTINCT!"

And so, in the middle of a vast desert, five members of Holomyth ran for their lives from a sandstorm, one of them being carried, all of them screaming, and one of them having decided that discretion was definitely the better part of valor.

The sandstorm, indifferent to their drama, continued its approach.

This was going to be a long day.
