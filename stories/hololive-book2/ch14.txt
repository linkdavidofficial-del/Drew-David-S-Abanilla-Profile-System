Chapter 14: Homecoming — Part Three: Processing and Chaos

Homecoming
Part Three: Processing and Chaos

For a long moment after all the stories had been told, everyone just sat in silence, processing the sheer scope of what they'd all been through.

Then Kronii said, "We need to never speak of this to management."

"Agreed," came the immediate chorus.

"How would we even explain this?" Fauna asked. "Our plane got attacked by suicidal pigeons, detached into sections with experimental safety features, and scattered us across multiple biomes where we had supernatural adventures?"

"They'd think we were making it up," IRyS agreed.

"Or worse, they'd believe us and then investigate," Mumei added darkly. "Do you want government agencies asking questions about desert goddesses and ice dragons?"

"Hard pass," Gura said immediately.

"So we agree," Ame summarized. "Official story: the plane had mechanical issues, we had to make emergency landings in different locations, we found our way to civilization and came home. No specifics."

"What about our condition?" Raora gestured at Justice's battered state. "We look like we fought a war."

"Hiking accident," Shiori suggested. "You got lost in the mountains, had some falls, finally found a village."

"And us?" Kronii gestured at Council's wet state.

"Boat accident. Fell in the ocean, had to swim to safety."

"Us?" Kiara asked, gesturing at Myth's sand-covered state.

"Got lost in a desert, found your way out. Simple."

"And your group?" Elizabeth looked at Advent.

Advent exchanged looks. "We had a normal, boring survival experience on an island and would like to not be murdered in our sleep?"

Despite everything, that got a laugh.

"Fair," Calli allowed. "You did build a functional ship in seven hours. That's legitimately impressive even if we're jealous."

"Thank you?" Fuwawa said cautiously.

The tension that had been building since Advent's reveal finally broke, replaced with exhausted camaraderie.

"We survived," Fauna said, looking around at all of them. "All of us. Despite everything going wrong, despite being scattered across the world, despite—" she gestured vaguely, "—everything. We all made it home."

"We did," Ina agreed softly. "That's... actually pretty amazing when you think about it."

"We're amazing," Bae declared. "Chaos-tested, disaster-approved."

"I'm putting that on a t-shirt," Nerissa decided.

"Please don't," Elizabeth begged.

"Too late, already designing it in my head."

The conversation began to fragment into smaller discussions—Myth comparing their desert experience with Justice's mountain trek, Council and Advent talking about supernatural power applications, everyone sharing minor details they'd forgotten in the main storytelling.

And then someone—it might have been Mococo—said something that made everyone pause.

"Wait. Where's our luggage?"

They all looked around.

Myth's luggage was scattered across the desert, presumably still being eaten by lizards or buried in sand.

Council's luggage had been deposited in pocket dimensions by Ina—wait, no, they were Council. Their luggage had been scattered across Sana and mostly salvaged.

Advent's luggage was neatly packed in their bags because of course it was.

Justice's luggage was... actually, where was Justice's luggage?

"We left it in the mountains," Elizabeth realized. "We grabbed essentials and ran, and then we just kept fighting and never went back."

"So we have no luggage," Cecilia said.

"We have weapons?" Raora offered.

"I'm not sure how to explain to facilities why we checked weapons but not suitcases."

"Add it to the list of things we're not explaining."

Gura stood up suddenly, stretching. "Okay, I'm claiming first shower. I have approximately seven pounds of sand on me and it needs to go."

"No!" Kronii shot up. "We're soaking wet! Water-based mess takes priority over sand-based mess!"

"Sand gets everywhere!" Gura argued. "It's harder to clean!"

"Water makes everything damp! That's worse!"

"Is it though?"

"Yes!"

"This is ridiculous," Ina observed. "There are multiple bathrooms in this building."

"Oh," Gura and Kronii said together. "Right."

"We've been through too much," Fauna said. "Our brains have stopped working properly."

"Agreed," came the chorus.

They began to move, slowly, heading toward the elevators and the showers beyond. The conversations continued, flowing and changing as they walked.

"Do you think Sura is still in there?" Bijou asked Gura curiously.

"Probably? Maybe? I don't know how it works!" Gura replied. "I'm not testing it though. No more deserts, no more transformations."

"But if you needed—"

"No."

"Even if—"

"Absolutely not."

"What if—"

"BIJOU."

Behind them, Elizabeth was explaining wrestling moves to Sana, which was surreal enough that several people stopped to watch.

"So the suplex is about leverage," Elizabeth was saying seriously. "You need to—"

"I already know how to suplex," Sana interrupted. "I suplexed a demon orca."

"Yes, but did you do it with proper form?"

"Does it matter if the form is proper if the orca goes flying?"

"Technique always matters!"

"This conversation is amazing and I never want it to end," Bae whispered to Mumei.

"I'm recording it mentally for later analysis," Mumei replied.

The elevators arrived, and they piled in—too many for one car, so they split into two groups. Myth, Council, and Advent in one; Justice in the other.

As the doors closed on the first elevator, someone—probably Kiara—started humming. It was picked up by others, turning into an impromptu harmony that was probably supposed to be comforting but was mostly just exhausted.

The second elevator had Justice leaning against the walls, too tired to even maintain conversation.

"Never again," Raora said.

"Definitely never again," Cecilia agreed.

"We're cursed," Gigi decided. "We must be cursed. Normal people don't fight dragons."

"We're not normal people," Elizabeth pointed out. "We're idols with supernatural weapons who stream to thousands of people. Normal was never an option."

"Fair point."

They reached the residential floors—yes, the building had residential floors because of course it did, the talents basically lived here half the time anyway—and scattered to their respective rooms.

The showers that followed were legendary. Myth spent nearly an hour each getting sand out of every possible crevice. Council had to actually scrub to get the seaweed smell out. Advent felt slightly guilty about how quickly they cleaned up. Justice took turns because some of them could barely stand and needed someone nearby in case they passed out.

An hour later, clean and dressed in comfortable clothes, they reconvened in the common area on the residential floor. Someone—probably Fuwawa—had raided the kitchen and prepared actual food, which they ate with the desperation of people who had survived on minimal rations.

"This is the best rice I've ever had," Kronii declared around a mouthful.

"It's just rice," Mococo said.

"It's AMAZING rice."

"You're just very hungry."

"BEST. RICE. EVER."

They ate, they talked, they processed. The adrenaline was fully gone now, replaced with bone-deep exhaustion and the surreal feeling of being home after such impossible experiences.

"We should sleep," Fauna said eventually. "We've been through... a lot."

"Understatement of the century," Calli muttered.

"Are we streaming tomorrow?" IRyS asked suddenly.

Everyone stopped.

"Oh my god," Nerissa said. "We have streams scheduled."

"We're canceling them," Shiori decided immediately. "All of us. We need recovery time."

"Management will ask questions," Ina pointed out.

"We'll say we're sick," Amelia suggested. "All of us caught the same bug on the trip. Food poisoning from the plane food."

"That actually works," Mumei agreed. "It's believable and doesn't require details."

"Done," Elizabeth said. "Everyone cancel streams for the next two days minimum, cite illness, do not elaborate."

"Two days isn't enough," Raora protested.

"It's what we can get away with without raising suspicions," Elizabeth replied. "After that, we ease back into normal schedules and pretend none of this happened."

"I'm never pretending this didn't happen," Bae declared. "This is the craziest thing that's ever happened to me and that's saying something."

"We can talk about it among ourselves," Ina clarified. "We just don't talk about it publicly or to management."

"Secret disaster club," Gigi said.

"I'm making a group chat," Bijou was already on her phone. "We need a group chat for this."

"What are you calling it?" Kiara asked.

Bijou thought for a moment. "'Definitely Normal Vacation Club'?"

"Perfect," came the unanimous agreement.

Phones buzzed as everyone was added to the new group chat. The first message appeared immediately.

Bijou: Welcome to the trauma club

Gura: why did you make this

Bijou: so we can share memes about our suffering

Nerissa: I support this

Kronii: Of course you do

They talked for another hour, slowly winding down as exhaustion finally won out over the need to process. One by one, they headed to their rooms, calling out goodnights, making plans to meet up the next day.

Myth was the first group to fully retreat, Gura practically sleepwalking by the end.

Council followed shortly after, Fauna still finding stray pieces of seaweed somehow.

Advent went next, still looking far too well-rested but now properly guilty about it.

Justice lasted the longest, too wired from their combat high to sleep immediately, but eventually even they had to admit defeat.

The common area fell silent, empty except for the remains of their meal and the general air of "something impossible happened here."

In their individual rooms, the members of Hololive EN finally, truly rested. Some had nightmares about their experiences. Others slept like the dead. A few stayed up texting in the group chat, unable to let go of the shared experience quite yet.

But they were home. They were safe. They were together, even if in separate rooms.

And in the morning, they would wake up and figure out how to move forward from the most bizarre team-building exercise in history.

But that was tomorrow's problem.

Tonight, they were just glad to be alive.

But they had each other, they had their memories, and they had a group chat called "Definitely Normal Vacation Club."

And somehow, that was enough.
