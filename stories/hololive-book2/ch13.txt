Chapter 13: Homecoming — Part Two: The Exchange

Homecoming
Part Two: The Exchange

The initial shock began to wear off, replaced by curiosity and a desperate need to understand what everyone else had gone through.

"Okay," Amelia said, pulling herself together with visible effort, her detective instincts overriding her exhaustion. "We need to debrief. Everyone needs to explain where they ended up and what happened."

"Can we do it after showers?" Fauna asked plaintively. "I'm still dripping."

"And I'm still covered in sand," Gura added. "There's sand in places I didn't know I had places."

"No," Kronii decided. "We do this now. Because if we separate, we're never going to get the full story. I need to know why Advent looks like they went on vacation while the rest of us fought for our lives."

"We didn't go on vacation!" Nerissa protested. "We crashed on an island!"

"A paradise island with food and hot springs!"

"It could have been dangerous!"

"But was it?"

"...No," Nerissa admitted. "Not really."

"That's what I thought."

Ina, ever the voice of reason, spoke up. "Chronological order by landing? We can go through each group's experience systematically."

"Fine," Elizabeth said, wincing as she adjusted her position. "But someone needs to get drinks. If I'm reliving the mountain gauntlet, I need hydration."

"I'll go," Bijou volunteered, standing up. "We brought supplies! Fresh fruit juice from the island!"

"Of course you did," Mumei muttered.

Bijou returned quickly with drinks—actual fresh fruit juice that was honestly better than it had any right to be—and distributed them. The act of doing something normal, of sharing drinks like they always did, helped ground everyone.

"Alright," Amelia said, taking charge. "Let's start with the basics. Where did everyone crash?"

"Desert," Myth answered together.

"Ocean. Specifically the Bermuda Triangle," Council replied.

"Tropical island," Advent said, slightly embarrassed.

"Mountains," Justice stated grimly.

"Okay, so completely different biomes," Ina noted. "That's... actually fascinating from a probability standpoint. The plane sections separated in midair and somehow distributed us across entirely different climate zones."

"Can we focus on the how we survived part rather than the mathematical improbability?" Calli asked.

"Right. Sorry." Ina pulled out a notebook—where had she been keeping that?—and prepared to take notes. "Myth, you want to go first?"

Amelia nodded, then gestured to Gura. "Actually, Gura should explain. Or rather, Sura should."

"Who's Sura?" several voices asked.

Gura grimaced. "About that..."

Myth's Tale

The explanation of Sura took several minutes because everyone kept interrupting with questions.

"So you turned into a desert goddess?" Kronii asked, fascinated despite herself.

"Not voluntarily!" Gura defended. "The desert just... changed me! I adapted!"

"Like a Pokémon evolution," Bae said, grinning.

"That's what we said!"

"Can we see?" Nerissa asked eagerly. "Can you transform right now?"

"No!" Gura said firmly. "It requires sand, and I am never touching sand again. Ever. Sand is dead to me."

"We literally threw sand and water at her to switch between forms," Kiara explained, trying not to laugh at the memory. "It was like having a desert switch."

"That's actually really useful," Mumei observed. "Having an alternate form adapted to environmental conditions—"

"Mumei, you're doing the analysis thing again," Fauna interrupted gently.

"Sorry."

Calli took over the narrative, explaining the sandstorms, the chase, the transformation. "And then Sura guided us to an abandoned village where we could rest. She was smug about it. So smug."

"I was a goddess!" Gura protested. "Goddesses are supposed to be smug!"

"You were insufferable," Ina corrected with a small smile.

"That too."

Amelia described the journey to civilization, the dramatic slow-motion walk across the desert with Ina playing Arabian Nights on a flute she'd found.

"You had a cinematic moment?" Elizabeth asked, slightly jealous. "We just fought constantly. There was no time for cinematic moments."

"Make time for drama," Kiara advised. "It's important for morale."

"Noted for next time we crash in hostile terrain."

"Hopefully there won't be a next time," Cecilia said.

"With our luck? There'll definitely be a next time," Raora muttered.

Council's Tale

Council's story came next, and it was significantly more harrowing.

"So we were floating in the middle of the Bermuda Triangle on Sana," Fauna began, still picking at the seaweed in her hair. "Which was terrifying because I'm Mother Nature and there was literally no nature. Just water. So much water."

"Our powers were weakened," Kronii added. "The Triangle messes with supernatural abilities. Everything was diminished—my time manipulation, Bae's chaos, IRyS's divine energy."

"I couldn't do anything," Fauna emphasized. "Everything I do requires plants or earth. The ocean was basically my kryptonite."

"So you became emotional support," Mumei said with a slight smile.

"I was very good emotional support!" Fauna insisted.

"You were," IRyS confirmed. "Your cheering genuinely helped."

Bae explained the red orcas, and everyone leaned forward despite their exhaustion.

"Four times bigger than normal orcas?" Gigi asked, her eyes wide.

"At least," Bae confirmed. "They were massive. And hungry. And there were fifty of them."

"How did you survive?" Shiori asked, taking notes.

"Mumei's tax beams and IRyS's divine bullets," Kronii explained. "They held them off while I accelerated Sana's time to make her swim faster and Bae threw chaos dice at anything that got too close."

"And then we got out of the Triangle," Sana said, a fierce grin crossing her face. "And everything changed."

The description of their counterattack had everyone engaged. Mumei's tax beams at full power, IRyS's devastating divine energy, Kronii's time manipulation going from barely functional to complete control, Bae's chaos effects running wild, Fauna's seaweed chains, and Sana performing wrestling moves on demon orcas.

"You pile-drived a sea monster," Nerissa repeated, star-struck. "That's the coolest thing I've ever heard."

"It hurt my back," Sana said, but she was smiling. "Totally worth it though."

"We gave them a lesson they won't forget," IRyS said with satisfaction. "No one chases Council and gets away with it."

"Very heroic," Ame noted approvingly. "Solid comeback story."

Advent's Tale

When it was Advent's turn, they exchanged slightly guilty looks.

"Our experience was... different," Shiori began carefully.

"We crashed on an island," Fuwawa continued.

"A tropical island with abundant resources," Mococo added.

"And no immediate threats," Nerissa finished.

The other groups stared at them.

"Go on," Calli said, her voice dangerous.

Shiori, wisely deciding that honesty was the best policy, explained everything. The discovery of the island's resources, the decision to build proper shelter, the construction of individual huts, the taming of livestock, the farming, the hot spring.

"You built a HOT SPRING," Fauna repeated, her eye twitching slightly.

"Nerissa enhanced a natural warm spring!" Shiori defended. "We didn't build it from scratch!"

"Still counts," Kronii said flatly.

"And then we built a ship," Mococo said, trying to move past the hot spring topic.

That derailed into explanations of ship construction, Nerissa's magic enhancement, and Shiori's navigation calculations. By the end, even the exhausted groups had to admit it was impressive work.

"You turned a survival situation into a vacation resort in seven hours," Ina summarized.

"When you say it like that, it sounds bad," Bijou said weakly.

"Because it is bad!" Gura gestured at herself. "I turned into a desert goddess because of dehydration! You were taking hot spring baths!"

"To be fair, we didn't know you were suffering," Fuwawa pointed out.

"That doesn't make it better!"

"Actually, it kind of does," Raora said. "If you'd known and still done it, that would be cruel. Not knowing means you were just lucky."

"Very, very lucky," Elizabeth emphasized, looking at her torn clothes and then at Advent's pristine outfits.

"We can share the fruit juice?" Nerissa offered weakly.

"We're already drinking it," Mumei pointed out.

"Then we're even!"

"We're absolutely not even," Bae said, but she was smiling now. The initial shock and jealousy was fading into something more like bemused acceptance.

Justice's Tale

Finally, it was Justice's turn, and everyone settled in because they looked like they had a story.

"Mountains," Elizabeth began. "We crashed in the mountains. Snow-covered, freezing, hostile mountains."

"With yetis," Cecilia added.

"Wait, back up," Ame interrupted. "Yetis? Like actual yetis?"

"About eight feet tall, covered in white fur, very territorial, and very angry that we crashed in their territory," Raora confirmed.

"We fought them off," Gigi said. "Barely. Then we ran."

Elizabeth described their trek down the mountain, the discovery of the village, the rest, and then the decision to find civilization.

"And that's when things got weird," Cecilia said.

"Weirder than yetis?" Kronii asked.

"So much weirder."

They explained the golems, and everyone listened with rapt attention. The description of fighting fifteen-foot stone constructs had even the exhausted groups sitting up straighter.

"You fought like an RPG party," Ina observed. "Elizabeth as the main fighter, Cecilia as support and bard, Raora as the rogue, Gigi as the tank."

"We didn't plan it!" Elizabeth insisted. "It just happened! We fell into those roles naturally!"

"Very naturally," Cecilia agreed. "To the point where I was literally playing a violin mid-battle to buff everyone."

"Your lance turns into a violin?" Fauna asked, fascinated.

"Apparently? I didn't know it could do that until I needed it to."

The story continued—snow leopards, the abandoned castle, the throne room.

"A dead king," Shiori repeated, writing furiously. "An actual undead monarch with a massive sword."

"That came back to life when we entered," Raora confirmed. "And we couldn't leave. The doors sealed."

"We thought we were seeing HP bars," Gigi said. "Like actual video game health bars above its head."

"You probably were," Mumei said seriously. "At that point, with that level of supernatural activity, you'd essentially entered a domain of concentrated magical energy. Visual manifestations of metaphysical concepts would be expected."

Everyone stared at her.

"There were HP bars," Mumei simplified.

"Oh. Okay."

Elizabeth described the fight in detail—the first phase, the desperation, and then the transformation into the second phase.

"Phase two," multiple voices repeated in awe.

"Full dark energy form, enhanced abilities, area denial attacks," Cecilia listed. "It was like fighting a raid boss."

"How did you win?" Bae asked, genuinely curious.

"Stubbornness," Elizabeth said simply. "We refused to lose. Just kept fighting until it fell."

The rest of the story came out—the volcano, the fire spirits, the snow fairies, the skeleton dwarves in the cave.

"And then the dragon," Raora said.

"The ice dragon," Gigi emphasized.

They described the final fight, and by the end, even Advent looked impressed despite their guilt over having an easy time.

"Four hours," Kiara said softly. "You fought a dragon for four hours."

"Give or take," Elizabeth confirmed. "We weren't exactly checking our watches."

"And you won," Ame said, respect clear in her voice.

"We won," Justice confirmed together.
