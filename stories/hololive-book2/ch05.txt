Part Five: Island Paradise (Advent's Lucky Break)

Shiori Novella regained consciousness with sand in her mouth and murder in her heart.

Not murder directed at anyone in particular—just a general sense that violence should be inflicted upon something, anything, to compensate for the indignity of plane crashes and near-death experiences.

She sat up, spitting out sand, and took stock of her situation.

Beach. She was on a beach. A nice beach, actually, with soft white sand and gently lapping waves. Their section of the plane rested half-submerged in the shallow water nearby, looking significantly less crashed and more gently beached.

"Huh," Shiori said aloud. "We lucked out."

A muffled sound came from nearby. Shiori turned to see two legs sticking out of the sand, kicking frantically.

"Nerissa?" Shiori guessed.

The legs kicked harder.

"Yeah, that's definitely Nerissa." Shiori stood, brushed herself off, and walked over to the struggling figure. She grabbed the legs and pulled.

Nerissa emerged from the sand face-first, gasping and coughing. "I thought I was going to die," she wheezed. "Death by sand. What a way to go."

"You were barely a foot under," Shiori pointed out. "You would've gotten out eventually."

"Let me have my dramatic moment," Nerissa complained, still coughing up sand.

Rustling sounds came from the tree line. Fuwawa and Mococo emerged, both carrying armfuls of luggage.

"Oh good, you're awake!" Fuwawa said cheerfully. "We've been gathering everyone's stuff. Most of it made it to shore intact!"

"How are you both so energetic?" Shiori asked. "We literally just survived a plane crash."

"Dog energy," Mococo explained simply. "Also we've already explored the island a bit. It's not that big, but it's got resources!"

"Resources?" Nerissa was on her feet now, helping Shiori dust off. "Like what?"

"Come see!" Fuwawa grabbed their hands and started pulling them toward the forest.

The island was, as Mococo had said, not particularly large. From what Shiori could estimate, maybe twice the size of Singapore—so still substantial, but definitely finite. And more importantly, it seemed to have everything they needed for survival.

"Look!" Fuwawa pointed. "Pigs! Wild pigs!"

Indeed, several wild boars were rooting around in the undergrowth, paying no attention to the humans nearby.

"And cows!" Mococo gestured to a small clearing where several bovines grazed peacefully. "Actual cows! On an island! How does that work?"

"I'm not questioning it," Shiori decided. "We have food sources. That's good."

They continued walking, discovering more impossible wildlife. Chickens. Lizards—though thankfully not the luggage-eating kind. Coconut trees heavy with fruit. Some kind of wild berries that looked edible.

"This is weird, right?" Nerissa said slowly. "This is too perfect. Islands don't just naturally have this mix of animals."

"Maybe someone was living here before?" Fuwawa suggested. "Like, someone brought livestock and then abandoned it?"

"Or," Shiori said darkly, "this is some kind of weird supernatural island and we're all going to die horribly."

"Why would you say that?" Mococo demanded.

"Just keeping our expectations realistic."

"Your expectations are terrible," Nerissa informed her.

A strange sound from above made them all look up. Perched in a tree, looking extremely confused about her situation, was Koseki Bijou.

"Uh," Bijou said. "Little help?"

"How did you get up there?!" Fuwawa called up.

"I don't know! I woke up in a tree! That's not normal, right? People don't usually wake up in trees?"

"Definitely not normal," Mococo confirmed. "Hold on, we'll get you down!"

Between the four of them, they managed to help Bijou descend from her arboreal prison, though not without significant complaining from all parties involved.

"I hate trees now," Bijou declared once safely on the ground. "Trees are my nemesis."

"Noted," Shiori said. "Okay, we need a plan. We're stranded on an island with apparently abundant resources but no obvious way off. First priority: shelter."

"I saw our luggage on the beach," Nerissa added. "Some of it's damaged but my spear only has a small dent. And I grabbed Bijou's katana and Shiori's book and quill from the wreckage."

"Practical," Shiori approved. "Okay, new plan. We gather all our stuff, find a good location for a base camp, and build shelter. Then we worry about long-term survival and rescue."

"That sounds suspiciously organized," Bijou observed.

"I read a lot of survival isekai," Shiori explained. "I'm prepared for this exact scenario."

"This isn't an isekai," Mococo pointed out.

"Close enough."

They spent the next hour gathering their scattered belongings from the beach. Shiori's book and quill were waterlogged but salvageable. Nerissa's black spear had indeed gotten dented during the crash, but was still functional. Bijou's katana was mostly fine, just needed cleaning. The twins' equipment was scattered but largely intact.

"We have weapons, we have tools, we have food sources," Shiori ticked off on her fingers. "This is actually pretty good considering we fell out of the sky."

"I'm choosing to focus on the positive," Fuwawa agreed. "We're alive, we're together, and we're not being actively hunted by anything."

"Yet," Nerissa added ominously.

"Why are you both like this?" Mococo sighed.

They selected a spot in the forest—not too deep, close enough to the beach to see if any rescue came, but sheltered enough to provide protection from weather. Shiori, channeling her extensive knowledge of manga and light novels, quickly sketched out a design for a basic shelter.

"Nothing fancy," she explained, drawing in the sand with a stick. "Just four corner posts, a slanted roof for rain drainage, walls made from whatever we can find. Simplicity is key in survival situations."

"You're surprisingly good at this," Bijou observed.

"I contain multitudes," Shiori replied. "Now let's build this thing before it gets dark."

Construction turned out to be easier than expected, largely because they had multiple people and abundant materials. The island provided bamboo-like plants that worked perfectly for the frame, large leaves for roofing, and vines for binding everything together.

Fuwawa and Mococo handled the heavy lifting with their enhanced energy. Nerissa used her spear to cut through thicker vegetation. Bijou's precise katana work helped shape materials to size. And Shiori directed everything with the confidence of someone who had read way too many survival scenarios.

"Post goes here," she'd direct. "No, three inches to the left. Perfect. Now tie it off with that vine. Make sure it's tight—we don't want this collapsing in the middle of the night."

"When did you become a construction manager?" Nerissa asked, following instructions while simultaneously complaining about them.

"When I became the only one with a plan," Shiori shot back. "Unless you want to sleep on the beach?"

"The hut is great, Shiori. I love the hut. Huts are wonderful."

By the time the sun started setting—which gave them their first real confirmation that they were indeed in a tropical zone with normal day-night cycles—they had a functional shelter. It wasn't pretty, but it was sturdy, relatively spacious, and most importantly, would keep the rain off if it rained.

"Not bad," Bijou assessed, hands on her hips as she surveyed their work. "We made a house. We're basically pioneers."

"Pioneers with idol training and supernatural weapons," Mococo added. "Very specific pioneers."

"The best kind," Fuwawa agreed.

They gathered their salvaged supplies inside the shelter, organizing them as best they could. Weapons leaned against one wall. Damaged luggage was sorted through, with salvageable items kept and ruined things discarded. Someone—probably Fuwawa—had even gathered some of the island's fruits, creating a small emergency food supply.

As darkness fell, they sat together in their improvised home, exhausted but alive.

"So," Nerissa said into the quiet. "How long do you think we're stuck here?"

"Could be days," Shiori said. "Could be weeks. Could be months."

"That's not comforting."

"I'm not here to be comforting. I'm here to be realistic."

"The others are probably fine," Fuwawa said hopefully. "They all had their own sections of the plane. They probably landed somewhere safe too."

"Or they landed somewhere terrible and are having a much worse time than us," Shiori countered.

"OPTIMISM, SHIORI," Mococo insisted. "Try it sometime."

"I'm optimistic that we won't die immediately," Shiori offered. "That's as good as you're getting."

Despite the bickering, there was an underlying warmth to it—the comfort of familiar voices, of friends who'd survived something terrible together. They'd figure this out. They had to.

"Tomorrow we explore more thoroughly," Shiori decided. "Map out the whole island, find water sources, check for any signs of previous inhabitants. Tonight, we rest."

"I'm exhausted anyway," Bijou yawned. "Today has been approximately fourteen years long."

"Agreed," Nerissa murmured, already half-asleep.

As the sounds of the island night surrounded them—waves lapping, wind rustling leaves, occasional animal calls—the members of Advent settled in for their first night as castaways.

They'd survived a plane crash caused by suicidal pigeons, a detaching experimental aircraft, and a fall from the sky. They'd built shelter with their bare hands. They'd secured resources and made a plan.

Compared to what some of the other groups might be dealing with, they'd gotten incredibly, impossibly lucky.

Tomorrow would bring new challenges, new discoveries, and probably new disasters knowing their luck.

But tonight, they were safe, they were together, and they were alive.

That would have to be enough.
