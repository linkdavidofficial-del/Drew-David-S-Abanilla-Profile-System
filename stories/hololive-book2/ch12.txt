Chapter 12: Homecoming — Part One: The Returns

Homecoming
Part One: The Returns

The Hololive EN Branch Building stood quiet in the late afternoon sun, its glass and steel exterior reflecting the city around it. The building was eerily empty—this was supposed to be their week off, after all, the staff had been given time away, and the building was left in the care of the talents themselves who often treated it less like an office and more like a massive shared apartment.

The lobby was pristine, silent, the reception desk unmanned. The elevator hummed quietly in standby mode. Everything was peaceful.

That peace was about to be shattered.

Council + IRyS: 4:23 PM

The front doors slid open with their usual smooth whisper, and six figures stumbled through.

They were soaking wet.

Not just damp—soaking. Water dripped from every surface, creating puddles with each step. Their clothes clung to them uncomfortably, their hair plastered to their heads. They smelled distinctly of salt and seaweed.

Kronii entered first, her usually perfect appearance completely destroyed. Her hair, normally immaculate, hung in wet ropes around her face. Her clothes were torn in several places, bearing what looked suspiciously like bite marks. She walked with the gait of someone who had been through something traumatic and was running purely on spite and determination.

"I'm never going near water again," she announced to the empty lobby, her voice echoing in the space. "Never. I'm showering in sand from now on."

"That's not how showering works," Fauna said weakly, stumbling in behind her. She was covered in bits of seaweed, which seemed to be clinging to her affectionately. Every few steps, she'd pull another strand off with a grimace. "Why is there so much seaweed? I controlled the seaweed. Why is it still on me?"

"Because the ocean hates you specifically," Bae supplied, rolling her literally soaked dice bag in her hands. Water poured out of it with each rotation. "The ocean looked at you and said 'you know what? Extra seaweed for this one.'"

"I don't appreciate your tone," Fauna muttered.

Sana entered at her normal size—thankfully—but she was limping slightly, one hand pressed to her side. "Note to self: WWE moves on demon orcas are satisfying but terrible for your back."

"You pile-drived a sea monster," IRyS said, her wings drooping and dripping water. "What did you think would happen?"

"That it would be cool and have no consequences?" Sana offered weakly.

"That's not how physics works!"

"Physics stopped applying to us somewhere around 'plane detached into pieces,'" Sana pointed out.

Mumei was the last to enter, still clutching her phone despite its cracked screen. She was muttering something about never trusting the Bermuda Triangle again and also something about filing a complaint with... geography? It was hard to tell.

They made it to the lobby's central seating area—a collection of couches and chairs arranged around a low table—and collapsed. Water immediately began to soak into the furniture.

"Someone's going to have to explain this to facilities," Kronii observed.

"Not it," everyone else said immediately.

"We'll blame it on a plumbing leak," Bae decided.

"We're on the first floor," IRyS pointed out. "There's no plumbing above us."

"Then we'll blame it on a... water-themed performance art piece," Bae tried again.

"That's not going to work."

"Do you have a better idea?"

"...No."

They sat in silence for a moment, water dripping, exhaustion settling into their bones.

"I'm hungry," Fauna said suddenly.

"There's a cafeteria on the third floor," Kronii replied without moving. "You're welcome to climb the stairs."

"I can't feel my legs."

"Then you're not that hungry."

Myth: 4:47 PM

The doors opened again, and Myth entered.

They looked like they'd crawled out of a disaster movie set in the Sahara.

All five of them wore tattered desert robes over their regular clothes, the fabric bleached by sun and abraded by sand. Every exposed surface—hair, skin, clothes—had a fine coating of golden-brown sand that puffed into small clouds with each movement. Their faces were sunburned, their lips chapped, and they all moved with the careful deliberation of people who had spent far too long in extreme heat.

Amelia entered first, her detective coat now more tan than its original color, her hair sticking up at odd angles from a combination of sand and static. She still had her revolver, though it was visibly full of sand and would probably need hours of maintenance before it functioned again.

"Air conditioning," she croaked, stopping just inside the doors to feel the cool air. "Oh my god, air conditioning. I'm never leaving air conditioning again."

"Seconded," Calli gasped, her scythe dragging behind her and leaving a trail of sand. "How is sand in places where sand should not physically be able to reach?"

"Desert magic," Kiara supplied, pulling her robes off to reveal her normal outfit underneath, which was also completely sand-covered. "Evil, terrible, horrific desert magic."

Ina entered calmly, somehow looking more composed than the others despite being equally sand-covered. "I tried to warn you about the second sandstorm."

"You ran away!" Calli accused.

"Tactically retreated," Ina corrected. "There's a difference."

"There's really not!"

And then Gura—normal Gura, not Sura—stumbled in last, moving like every step was an effort. She was the most sunburned of all of them, her nose bright red, and she was drinking from a water bottle like it was the last water on earth.

"Never again," she mumbled between gulps. "Never. Deserts are canceled. I'm canceling deserts."

"You can't cancel geographical features," Ina pointed out.

"Watch me."

They spotted Council on the couches and stopped, taking in the waterlogged group.

Council looked back at them, taking in the sand-covered group.

For a long moment, nobody spoke.

Then Kronii said, very slowly, "You look terrible."

"You're soaking wet," Amelia shot back.

"Ocean," Kronii explained simply.

"Desert," Amelia replied with the same tone.

Another pause.

"That explains so much and nothing at the same time," Fauna observed.

Myth collapsed onto the couches opposite Council, sending up clouds of sand that mixed with the puddles of water in a combination that immediately became mud.

"This is going to be impossible to clean," IRyS noted.

"Not our problem right now," Calli decided. "Right now, we sit."

"Agreed," came the unanimous response from both groups.

Advent: 5:15 PM

The doors opened a third time, and Advent entered looking... remarkably good, actually.

They were clean. Their clothes were intact. They looked rested, refreshed, almost glowing with health. Each of them carried bags that appeared to be full of supplies, and they were chatting cheerfully among themselves.

"—and then we could market the hot spring as a premium feature," Shiori was saying, taking notes in her journal as she walked. "Maybe offer package deals with the livestock petting experience—"

"We're not going back to start a resort!" Nerissa laughed.

"I'm just saying, we left behind a perfectly good infrastructure—"

"That we built in seven hours!"

"Quality craftsmanship knows no time limits!"

They made it three steps into the lobby before stopping, taking in the scene before them.

Council, soaking wet, covered in seaweed, slumped on couches like they'd fought a war.

Myth, completely sand-covered, sunburned, sitting in postures of absolute exhaustion.

And all of them staring at Advent with expressions ranging from disbelief to barely-restrained violence.

"Uh," Fuwawa said carefully. "Hi?"

"Why," Kronii said, her voice dangerously calm, "do you all look like you just came back from a spa vacation?"

"Because we kind of did?" Mococo offered weakly.

"What."

"We landed on an island!" Bijou explained cheerfully, apparently not reading the room. "It was great! We had food, shelter, we built a hot spring—"

"You built a WHAT," Fauna's voice was slightly strangled.

"A hot spring! Nerissa used magic to enhance this natural warm spring and it was amazing—"

"I fought demon orcas for four hours," IRyS said flatly.

"I was chased by a sandstorm through a desert," Gura added.

"We had fresh fruit and tamed pigs," Fuwawa continued, still not catching on.

"I'm going to throw this sand at you," Amelia warned.

"We built a ship!" Shiori said proudly. "It was a really good ship too, proper hull design and everything—"

"I literally cannot see straight from sun exposure," Calli interrupted.

Advent finally processed that maybe their experience had been significantly different from everyone else's.

"Oh," Nerissa said, looking around at the disaster that was the other two groups. "You guys... didn't have a good time?"

"GET OUT," Kronii and Amelia said in perfect unison.

"We're just going to—" Mococo gestured vaguely toward the other couches, "—sit over here. Very quietly."

Advent crept to a third seating area, setting down their well-organized bags carefully and sitting with the posture of people who knew they were in danger but weren't sure from where.

"An island," Bae muttered. "They got an island. We got the Bermuda Triangle and they got a paradise island."

"With a hot spring," Fauna added bitterly, picking seaweed out of her hair.

"And tamed livestock," Kiara said, sand still falling from her clothes.

"I'm not bitter," Ina lied. "I'm totally not bitter about this at all."

"You're definitely bitter," Calli observed.

"Extremely bitter," Ina confirmed.

Justice: 5:43 PM

The final group entered, and everyone's attention immediately shifted.

Justice looked like they'd fought through every video game ever made.

Elizabeth's clothes were shredded, bearing burn marks, claw marks, and what looked suspiciously like sword cuts. Her greatsword was strapped to her back, but the blade was notched and scarred, clearly having seen heavy use. She had bandages wrapped around one arm and her face was bruised.

Cecilia's outfit was in similar condition, though she carried herself with slightly more composure. Her white lance was intact but splattered with what might have been blood—or possibly oil? It was hard to tell. She had a black eye that was already starting to purple.

Raora's claws were extended even though she wasn't actively fighting, like her body had forgotten how to retract them. Her clothes were torn across the back, and she was limping slightly, favoring her left leg.

Gigi's gauntlets were cracked in several places, deep gouges visible in the metal. She had what looked like frostbite on her fingers and a cut across her forehead that had been hastily bandaged with strips of cloth.

They entered the lobby, spotted the other groups, and stopped.

Everyone stared at them.

"What the hell happened to you?" Kronii asked, sitting up despite her exhaustion.

Elizabeth opened her mouth to answer, paused, seemed to run through the entire story in her head, and then just said, "Mountains."

"That doesn't explain—" Ame started.

"Mountains," Elizabeth repeated, her voice taking on a slightly manic edge. "Mountains with golems. And snow leopards. And a castle with a dead king. And a dragon. Especially a dragon."

"Did you say dragon?" Sana asked carefully.

"Ice dragon," Cecilia clarified, collapsing onto the nearest available couch—which happened to be next to Advent. "We fought an ice dragon for four hours."

"You WHAT," multiple voices chorused.

"Also skeleton dwarves," Raora added, sitting down with a wince. "And snow fairies. And fire spirits near a volcano. It was a whole thing."

"A volcano?" Fauna's eyes were wide. "In the mountains?"

"Nothing made sense!" Gigi threw her hands up, immediately regretting it as her cracked gauntlets creaked ominously. "We just kept walking and fighting! Walking and fighting! For hours! Days, maybe! Time stopped meaning anything!"

Justice collapsed into their seats, and for a long moment, the lobby was silent except for the sound of water dripping from Council, sand falling from Myth, and the general air of exhaustion radiating from Justice.

Advent, sitting in their clean clothes with their organized bags and rested appearances, slowly tried to sink into the couch cushions and disappear.

"So," Shiori said carefully into the silence. "Everyone had... different experiences?"

"You think?" Calli's voice was dry enough to match the desert she'd just left.
