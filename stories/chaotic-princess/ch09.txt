Chapter 9: TRIPLET WARFARE

The barracks smelled like sweat, oiled steel, and the particular brand of exhausted optimism that only came from men who had been up since dawn getting hit with wooden swords by a ten-year-old.

Elliana strolled through the training yard with her hands laced behind her head, whistling something that no one in this kingdom would ever recognize as a tune, because it had been written roughly three hundred years before this kingdom existed, in a country that didn't exist here at all.

"There she is." Caius spotted her first, breaking off from a bout with one of the junior knights and waving her over with his wooden practice sword. At fifteen, he'd finally shot up in height, though he still had the same easy, mischievous grin he'd worn since he was six years old and cackling at a crocodile stampede. "Took you long enough. I've been holding down the fort."

"Define 'holding down the fort.'"

"I beat Renner in a duel."

"Renner's twelve and still learning which end of the sword is sharp."

"Details." Caius grinned, and held out his hand.

Elliana slapped it, and what followed was a sequence of motions so practiced, so absurdly elaborate, that any outside observer would have assumed it had been passed down through royal tradition for centuries. It had not. It had been invented by the two of them over the course of roughly four years of relentless repetition, a full nine-step handshake involving a fist bump, a hip bump, a spin, a finger snap, and, for reasons neither of them could fully explain anymore, a brief moment where they both pointed at Sparky (who was not present, but the gesture had become traditional regardless) before ending in a double thumbs-up.

"Still got it," Caius said, satisfied.

"Never lost it." Elliana glanced past him, toward the sparring ring, where Lysander—seventeen now, tall, composed, entirely too dignified for his own good—was running drills with two of the senior instructors, his form crisp and precise in that maddeningly correct way of his.

Elliana's grin sharpened.

"Speaking of people who've still got it," she said, sauntering over, "how's the love life, big brother?"

Lysander didn't even look up from his stance. "I'm training, Elliana."

"That's not a no."

"It's also not a yes."

"So how's Celestine?"

That got him. His form wavered for exactly half a second—just long enough for one of the instructors to nearly land a hit he should have blocked easily—before he recovered, stepped back, and gave his sister a flat, long-suffering look.

"She's well," he said, with the careful precision of a man choosing every single word like it might be used against him in court. Which, with Elliana, it absolutely would.

"Just well?"

"Very well."

"Extremely well?"

"Elliana."

"You know," Elliana said, examining her fingernails with exaggerated casualness, "I did personally engineer your entire relationship. Threw myself on the sword-fighting grenade, so to speak. I feel like that entitles me to updates. Regular updates. Possibly a monthly report."

"You tripped me into her," Lysander said. "That is not the same as engineering a relationship."

"It absolutely is the same. I am the architect of your happiness. I should get a statue. Small one. Modest. Maybe holding a wooden sword, in the garden, right where the historic tripping occurred."

"There will be no statue."

"We'll revisit that." Elliana waved a hand, already losing interest in the argument now that she'd successfully gotten under his skin, which had been the entire point. "Anyway. She's with Rosaria right now, isn't she? Tea and magic nonsense in the gardens?"

"As far as I know."

"Excellent." Elliana was already backing away, grinning. "I have places to be. People to tackle. You know how it is."

"Please don't tackle anyone important."

"No promises!"

She left Lysander to his drills and Caius to his victory lap over poor Renner, and made her way across the training yard toward the far end, where a familiar figure was working through a series of sword forms with more enthusiasm than technique.

Lilia—ten years old, same as her sisters, mud-streaked in the way that had become something of a permanent personal brand at this point—was mid-swing when Elliana crashed into her from the side like a small, gleeful avalanche.

"OOF—"

They went down together in a tangle of limbs, and Lilia, with the particular bad luck that seemed to follow her specifically whenever Elliana was involved, landed face-first in the exact same patch of mud that had claimed her, by this point, dozens of times.

"ELLIANA!" Lilia surged back up, spitting mud, hair plastered to her face, absolutely incandescent with rage. "THAT'S IT. I'M DONE. I AM SO DONE—"

She grabbed her practice sword off the ground and swung it in a wide, genuinely well-executed arc—her form really had improved dramatically over the past few months, Elliana noted with the small, proud flicker of an older sister watching a younger one grow into her strength, right before she skipped backward out of range entirely.

"Ooh, nice form!" Elliana called, dancing just out of reach as Lilia's next swing whistled through empty air where her head had been a half-second earlier. "You've been practicing!"

"I'm going to PRACTICE on YOUR FACE—"

From the sidelines, Caius had already abandoned Renner entirely in favor of watching this unfold, and had progressed rapidly from mild amusement to the specific, wheezing, doubled-over laughter that only his sisters' chaos could produce.

"GET HER, LILIA!" he managed, between gasps. "AVENGE THE MUD!"

"I'M TRYING—"

Lilia swung again, faster this time, genuinely trying to connect, and for a moment it looked like she might actually manage it—her blade cutting a clean line toward Elliana's shoulder with real speed behind it.

Elliana let her get close. Let the moment stretch out just long enough for Lilia's eyes to widen with the first flush of triumph.

Then she smirked, sidestepped with lazy, almost insulting ease, hooked one foot behind Lilia's ankle, and sent her tumbling right back into the mud with an undignified splash.

"WHY," Lilia said, into the dirt, "am I always the one in the mud."

"Bad luck," Elliana said cheerfully, crouching down to pat her sister on the back with the exaggerated gentleness of someone comforting a wounded soldier. "Tragic, really. You can do it next time, little sis."

Lilia lifted her head just enough to glare through a curtain of muddy hair. "We're TRIPLETS. You are not older than me."

"Technically I came out first."

"By THIRTY SECONDS."

"Thirty formative, character-defining seconds." Elliana stood, brushing imaginary dust off her hands with great ceremony. "Besides, age isn't really the metric here, is it? Because if we're being honest, you girls couldn't even learn to crawl fast enough to catch me when we were babies. I was already running circles around the nursery. Literally. There are still scuff marks."

"THAT'S NOT A FAIR COMPARISON, WE WERE INFANTS—"

"And yet," Elliana said, already turning to walk away, hands laced behind her head once more, "here we are, a decade later, and you're still in the mud."

Caius had, by this point, fully collapsed onto the training yard's dirt, wheezing, tears streaming down his face, utterly unable to form words.

"I HATE ALL OF YOU," Lilia announced to the general vicinity of the sky, and let her head fall back into the mud with a resigned splort.

Elliana strolled off through the barracks with the satisfied air of a woman who had accomplished exactly what she'd set out to accomplish, waving cheerfully to every guard and soldier she passed along the way. And they, without exception, waved back, or saluted, or offered some small, respectful nod that most of them reserved for people considerably higher-ranked than a ten-year-old chaos gremlin.

That was the strange part, if she ever stopped to actually think about it. She'd spent a decade in this body being an absolute menace—hunting boars, wrestling crocodiles, terrorizing gardeners, kidnapping herself out of a kidnapping—and somewhere along the way, without her particularly trying, the entire palace staff had shifted from *tolerating* her to something that looked disturbingly like *reverence.*

Servants bowed lower to her than they did to Lysander or Caius. Lower than they bowed to Lilia or Rosaria, even. Not quite as low as they bowed to the King and Queen, but close. Uncomfortably close.

She wasn't entirely sure what to do with that information yet, so she filed it away, the way she filed away most things that were interesting but not immediately useful, and kept walking toward the gardens.

The gardens, this time of day, were exactly where she expected them to be occupied: Rosaria and Celestine, seated at a small wrought-iron table beneath the shade of an old willow, a porcelain tea set between them, both of them utterly absorbed in something glowing faintly blue above Rosaria's outstretched palm.

"—and the resonance pattern shifts depending on proximity," Rosaria was saying, as Elliana approached quietly from behind a hedge, "which means, theoretically, I can tune the spell to track a specific bloom rather than a general species. Watch."

The glowing lattice above her hand condensed, brightened, and shot outward in a thin, barely visible thread that arced across the garden and settled, with unerring precision, on a single rose blossom nearly forty feet away.

"That's remarkable," Celestine said, already scribbling notes in a leather journal balanced on her knee, her honey-blonde hair falling forward as she wrote. Five years married into the family and still every bit as sharp with a quill as she was gentle with everyone around her. "The individuation alone is—Rosaria, this is well beyond rank four theory. Have you shown Archmage Yolanda?"

"Not yet. I wanted to confirm the stability first." Rosaria adjusted her glasses, looking, for a girl of ten, remarkably like a scholar three times her age. "I'd hate to present an unfinished—"

She never finished the sentence, because that was precisely the moment an entire bucket of cold water hit both of them from behind.

The reaction was immediate and total. Celestine gasped, nearly dropping her journal. Rosaria went rigid, water dripping from her glasses, and the nearby gardeners—three of them, mid-hedge-trim—simply froze in place, clippers still raised, wisely deciding that whatever was about to happen was not something they wanted to be caught watching too closely.

"ELLIANA."

"Hiiiii," Elliana said, already backpedaling, empty bucket dangling from one hand, grinning the exact grin that every single person in this palace had learned, over the years, to associate with imminent disaster.

Rosaria stood up so fast her chair toppled backward into the grass. Ice was already forming at her fingertips—not the gentle blue glow of a tracking spell, but sharp, crystalline spears, three of them, hovering and humming with barely restrained fury.

"You have exactly three seconds to run," Rosaria said, in a voice that had gone very calm and very cold, which was, if anything, more alarming than if she'd been shouting.

"I don't think that's how counting works, you didn't actually start the—"

The first ice spear whistled past Elliana's ear close enough to rearrange a few strands of hair.

"Okay, NOW we're counting!" Elliana laughed, already moving, weaving between the second and third spears with the loose, easy grace of someone who had spent a decade dodging things considerably more dangerous than her sister's magic.

Celestine, for her part, had simply picked her teacup back up, taken a small, resigned sip of what was now considerably more diluted tea than she'd started with, and settled back to watch the chaos unfold with the weary fondness of a woman who had, over five years, fully accepted her place in this family.

Rosaria fired a fourth spear, missed, and let out a frustrated noise that was decidedly less composed than her usual register. "HOLD STILL—"

"That would be counterproductive to my continued survival!"

Elliana closed the distance instead of retreating further—which caught Rosaria entirely off guard, because Elliana closing distance during a fight had, historically, meant something was about to go badly for whoever she was closing distance *with.*

She got a hand around Rosaria's waist, another braced against her shoulder, and in one smooth, practiced motion—the kind of motion that suggested she had done this exact maneuver before, possibly on unsuspecting boars—flipped her clean off her feet, spun her once through the air like a dancer with a very startled partner, and launched her sideways into the nearest hedge.

"ELLIANA I WILL END—" The rest of the sentence dissolved into an undignified shriek as Rosaria vanished into the greenery with a rustling crash, emerging a half-second later stuck fast, upside-down, both legs sticking straight up out of the bush like some kind of extremely irritated shrub decoration.

Elliana skipped over, giggling, gave her sister's exposed backside one cheerful, entirely unnecessary smack, and took off running before the resulting yelp had even fully left Rosaria's throat.

"I WILL TURN YOU INTO A NEWT—"

"You don't know that spell yet!"

"I'LL LEARN IT JUST FOR YOU—"

Elliana was still laughing, sprinting through the hedges with the wind in her hair, when Captain Helena rounded the garden path at a brisk, purposeful walk, took one look at the scene—an upended tea table, a bucket rolling in the grass, three gardeners standing very still with their clippers still raised, and Princess Rosaria stuck headfirst in a hedge, both legs kicking furiously—and stopped.

"What," Helena said, to no one in particular, "happened here."

Celestine took another sip of her diluted tea, entirely unbothered.

"Elliana," she said.

Helena closed her eyes for a long moment, the particular long moment of a woman who had, over the past decade, developed an entire second career built around cleaning up after one specific ten-year-old.

"Understood." She drew her sword with the crisp, resigned efficiency of someone who no longer needed further explanation, turned on her heel, and began walking briskly in the direction Elliana had fled. "Have a nice day."

By the time Elliana had made her way, laughing, through three separate hallways and past a truly staggering number of respectfully bowing servants, she'd looped back around to the palace's western courtyard, where a modest stone enclosure had been built years ago specifically to house one very large, very electric crocodile.

"Sparky!" She vaulted the low fence with practiced ease and dropped down beside the enclosure's central pool, where six feet of scaled, crackling menace immediately surfaced, rumbling low and pleased in the back of its throat.

Sparky had grown considerably over the years—no longer quite the size of a large dog, now closer to a small boat, with electricity that arced more visibly across his hide than it had a decade ago. But the moment Elliana's hand touched his snout, he went soft and pliant as a housecat, nudging into her palm with a low, contented rumble.

"Good boy," she murmured, scratching under his jaw. "Did you miss me? I've been busy. Terrorizing family members. The usual."

A sharp, familiar shriek cut through the air above her, and she looked up just in time to see two enormous shapes descend from the palace rooftops—the Thunderbirds, Zeus and Hera, wings crackling faintly with static as they landed, one on each of her shoulders, with a weight that would have flattened most grown men.

Elliana didn't so much as stagger.

"Hey, you two." She reached up with both hands, scratching each bird under its beak in turn, entirely unbothered by the combined weight of two apex predators perched on her small frame. "Did you behave? Did you eat the guard's lunch again? Hera, I can see it in your eyes, you absolutely ate someone's lunch."

Hera made a low, unrepentant chirping sound that Elliana chose to interpret as agreement.

"Your Highness!"

She looked up to find a young servant hurrying across the courtyard, slightly out of breath, bowing low even mid-jog. "Your Highness, the King and Queen request your presence. Immediately, if possible."

Elliana raised an eyebrow, gently coaxing Zeus and Hera off her shoulders and back onto their perches near Sparky's enclosure. "Immediately-immediately, or immediately-eventually?"

"Immediately-immediately, Your Highness."

"Fair enough." She gave Sparky one last scratch under the chin, straightened up, and dusted off her clothes with mild concern for how presentable she currently looked, given that she'd recently been drenched in bucket-water splashback and general garden chaos. "Lead the way, then. Let's see what fresh nonsense awaits."

The throne room, when she arrived, held an unfamiliar figure standing beside her parents.

He was old—properly old, the kind of old that had settled deep into the lines of his face and the white of his beard, which fell nearly to his chest in a thick, well-kept braid. He wore no armor, no royal finery, nothing that marked him as anything other than a traveler: sturdy boots, a worn leather coat, simple trousers, and a long sword strapped across his back in a scabbard that had clearly seen decades of use.

But there was something in the way he stood—loose, relaxed, weight settled evenly, hands resting easy at his sides—that made the hair on the back of Elliana's neck prickle in a way she hadn't felt since she'd first laid eyes on a wild Electro Crocodile as a three-week-old infant.

Queen Marianne spotted her the moment she entered and beckoned her forward with visible relief. "Elliana. There you are. Come, come."

Elliana approached, glancing between her mother, her father, and the old man, who was watching her with sharp, assessing eyes that felt entirely at odds with his relaxed, almost lazy posture.

"Elliana," Marianne said, "this is Grayer. He'll be your new instructor in swordsmanship."

The old man chuckled—a low, rumbling sound, not unlike Sparky's when he was pleased. "Retired, technically," he said, before the Queen could continue. "Been sitting on a nice quiet farm for the better part of a decade, minding my own business, growing turnips of all things. But word travels, even out to farms. Heard the royal family was looking for someone to train a very particular princess." His sharp eyes crinkled with amusement. "Heard rumors, too. About a certain 'Boar Slayer Princess' running around these parts, riding a crocodile, hunting a hundred boars a season for sport and profit."

Elliana blinked, looking to her mother. "People actually call me that?"

Marianne pinched the bridge of her nose in a gesture so familiar by now it barely registered as embarrassment anymore. "Unfortunately, yes."

Elliana considered this for exactly half a second before her chest puffed out with unmistakable, unrepentant pride. "Boar Slayer Princess," she repeated, savoring the words. "That's a GREAT title. I'm keeping that. Someone should put it on a banner."

"There will be no banner," Marianne said, at the exact same moment King Aldric muttered, "I'll have someone draft one," and received a sharp look for his trouble.

Grayer laughed outright at that, a full, genuine sound that seemed to shake something loose in his chest. "I like her already." He looked Elliana over, slow and assessing, the way a blacksmith might look over a promising but unrefined piece of ore. "Old man Grayer, they used to call me, back when I still had adventures worth telling. Gold rank, if the title still means anything out here—one of the few left, these days. Rank seven in the sword, though I've slowed down some since my glory years."

"Rank seven," Elliana echoed, and even she, chaos gremlin though she was, understood enough of what Lysander had drilled into her about the ranking system to feel a flicker of genuine excitement. Rank four was where real skill began. Rank five, sword aura. Rank seven was the stuff of legends—old veterans who'd survived campaigns and monster hunts that killed entire companies of lesser men.

"Rank seven," Grayer confirmed, with the easy modesty of a man who no longer needed to prove anything to anyone. "Before I agree to waste my retirement teaching royalty, though, I'd like to test your constitution. See what I'm actually working with." His eyes glinted. "That alright with you, Boar Slayer?"

Elliana grinned, already rolling up her sleeves. "Bring it on, old man."

He did—reaching out without warning, gripping her forearm in one weathered hand, and squeezing with slow, deliberate pressure. Elliana didn't flinch, didn't so much as wince, just stared back at him with the same unbothered grin she wore into literally everything.

Grayer's eyebrows rose, just slightly, and he let go, flexing his own hand as though testing it against an invisible weight.

"Strong," he said, sounding faintly, genuinely surprised. "Stronger than most boys twice her size. Makes sense, I suppose, for a girl who wrestles crocodiles and drags home a hundred boars for sport." He nodded slowly, something settling into place behind his sharp eyes. "This might actually be fun."

"Oh, it will be," Elliana said, grin widening into something distinctly predatory. "I promise you that."

Queen Marianne, watching the exchange, took one long, steadying breath, and reached for the small glass of wine that had, somewhat conspicuously, already been poured and waiting at her elbow.

"Gods help us all," she murmured, and took a slow sip.
