Chapter 5: MATCHMAKING AND GOLDEN CRIBS

The royal gardens were in full bloom, which meant it was the perfect setting for another awkward meeting between Prince Lysander and his betrothed.

Lady Celestine Aldwick, daughter of Duke Aldwick of the Northern Territories, sat on a marble bench looking like she wanted the earth to swallow her whole. Her honey-blonde hair was arranged in an elaborate style that had probably taken her maids an hour to perfect, and her pale blue dress was immaculate.

She was also blushing so hard she looked like she might spontaneously combust.

"I—I apologize for last time," she stammered, not quite meeting Lysander's eyes. "When I spilled tea on your coat, I didn't mean to—"

"No, no, it was my fault entirely," Lysander interrupted, equally flustered. "I moved too suddenly. Please, you have nothing to apologize for."

"But I—"

"Really, Lady Celestine, I insist—"

"No, Your Highness, I should have been more careful—"

They were both standing now, bowing apologetically to each other in an endless loop of politeness that was painful to watch.

This had been going on for four months.

Every meeting was the same: pleasantries, apologies, more apologies, awkward silence, and then they'd part ways having barely had an actual conversation.

From behind a nearby hedge, two pairs of eyes watched with growing impatience.

"This is painful," nineteen-month-old Elliana whispered to her seven-year-old brother.

Prince Caius nodded solemnly, gripping his wooden practice sword. "Big brother is hopeless at this."

"Completely hopeless," Elliana agreed. "It's been four months and they've had, what, three actual conversations?"

"Two. The third one doesn't count because they just apologized for three minutes straight."

"Operation Cupid is go?"

"Operation Cupid is definitely go."

They emerged from behind the hedge with the confidence of tiny generals entering a battlefield.

"BROTHER LYSANDER!" Caius called out cheerfully, wooden sword held high. "DEFEND YOURSELF!"

"What—Caius, not now—"

But Caius was already "attacking," swinging his wooden sword in exaggerated arcs.

And then Elliana joined in, her own tiny wooden sword wobbling as she charged forward. "FACE THE WRATH OF THE SMALLEST WARRIOR!"

"Elliana! Both of you, this isn't the time—" Lysander tried to sidestep, but Caius was surprisingly persistent.

Lady Celestine watched with wide eyes as the two children "battled" the prince, their wooden swords clacking against his attempts to block.

"You fight with the strength of a NOODLE, brother!" Caius declared dramatically.

"I'm not actually fighting you!" Lysander protested.

"Then you shall FALL!" Elliana added, then deliberately tripped over her own feet and tumbled into Lysander's legs.

He stumbled backward—right toward Lady Celestine.

She gasped, reaching out instinctively to steady him.

Their hands touched.

Both froze.

"I—I—" Celestine's blush deepened to crimson.

"I'm so sorry, I didn't mean to—" Lysander started.

"OOOOOH!" Caius and Elliana chorused in perfect unison. "HOLDING HANDS!"

"We're not—" Lysander jerked back.

"Brother Lysander and Lady Celestine, sitting in a tree—" Caius began in a sing-song voice.

"K-I-S-S-I-N-G!" Elliana finished, cackling.

"CHILDREN!" Lysander's face was now matching Celestine's in redness.

But something interesting happened.

Lady Celestine... giggled.

It was a small sound, quickly covered by her hand, but it was there.

Lysander turned to her, surprised.

"I'm sorry," she said, still smiling behind her hand. "Your siblings are... quite spirited."

"That's one way to put it," Lysander muttered, glaring at the two troublemakers.

Caius grinned unrepentantly. "We're just saying what everyone's thinking!"

"You'll make beautiful babies!" Elliana added helpfully.

"ELLIANA!"

"What? It's true! Look at you both—excellent bone structure, good height ratio, compatible temperaments—"

"She's NINETEEN MONTHS OLD," Lysander said to no one in particular. "How does she know these things?!"

"First comes love, then comes marriage—" Caius continued.

"Then comes a baby in a golden carriage!" Elliana finished.

Lysander looked like he was seriously considering throwing both of them into the ornamental pond.

But then he noticed something.

Celestine was laughing. Actually, properly laughing now, her nervousness forgotten.

"They're terrible," Lysander said, but his tone was softer now.

"They're wonderful," Celestine corrected, her eyes sparkling. "I wish I had siblings like that."

"You can borrow them. Please. Take them."

"HEY!" Caius protested.

But Elliana was already tugging on Caius's sleeve. "Mission accomplished. Let's go."

"But we were just getting to the good part—"

"Trust me. We're done here."

As they started to leave, Caius called back, "Don't forget protection!"

"CAIUS AMADEUS VALENTINE!" Lysander roared.

But the two children were already running, their laughter echoing through the gardens.

Lysander turned back to Celestine, mortification written all over his face. "I am so sorry. They're—they've been—I don't know what's gotten into them—"

But Celestine was looking at him with something new in her eyes.

He'd defended her. Sort of. In his own flustered, awkward way, he'd tried to protect her from his siblings' teasing.

"You're very kind, Your Highness," she said softly. "To care so much about your siblings despite their... enthusiasm."

"They're menaces."

"They love you."

Lysander paused. "I... suppose they do."

"And you love them too," Celestine added. "I can tell. The way you try to protect them, guide them, even when they're being... spirited." She smiled. "It's admirable."

For the first time in four months, Lysander looked directly into her eyes.

And he actually saw her.

Not just his arranged betrothed. Not just a political alliance. But a real person, with kind eyes and a gentle smile and a laugh that sounded like music.

"Would you..." he started, then cleared his throat. "Would you like to take a walk? Through the rose garden?"

Celestine's blush returned, but this time she was smiling. "I would like that very much."

From a second-floor window, Queen Marianne watched the scene unfold with tears streaming down her face.

"Are you crying?" Aldric asked, walking up behind her.

"Lysander just asked her to walk with him!" Marianne sobbed happily. "In four months, this is the first time he's initiated a conversation! They're actually talking!"

"That's... good?"

"It's wonderful! And it's all because of—" She turned, scanning the gardens until she spotted two small figures sword-fighting their way back toward the palace. "Elliana."

Aldric blinked. "Our chaos gremlin daughter? Helped?"

"She did! She and Caius—they pushed Lysander and Celestine together, broke the awkwardness, made them laugh—" Marianne pressed her hands to her chest. "Our daughter just improved the political relationship with the Northern Territories through matchmaking!"

"Accidentally."

"I don't care if it was accidental! It worked!" Marianne was practically glowing. "She deserves a reward. Something special. Something to show her that when she uses her chaos for good, she should be recognized for it."

That evening, Elliana was summoned to the throne room.

She arrived riding Sparky, because of course she did, wooden sword still strapped to her back.

"You called?" she asked, looking adorably confused.

Marianne descended from her throne, still misty-eyed, and knelt before her daughter. "Elliana, sweetheart, what you did today... helping your brother..."

"Oh." Elliana shifted uncomfortably. "That. Yeah, he was being pathetic. Had to intervene."

"It was wonderful. You helped create a genuine connection between Lysander and Lady Celestine. You strengthened our alliance with the Northern Territories. You—"

"I just wanted them to stop being awkward," Elliana interrupted. "It was painful to watch."

"Regardless," Marianne said firmly, "you did something truly helpful. And that deserves recognition."

She gestured, and servants brought forward an enormous crib.

Made entirely of gold.

Encrusted with gemstones.

With Sparky's name engraved on a small plaque at the base that read: "Guardian of the Golden Princess."

Elliana's eyes went wide. "Is that—"

"Your new crib," Marianne announced. "Because you, my darling daughter, are worth your weight in gold when you actually use your powers for good instead of chaos."

"It's GOLD."

"Yes."

"An actual GOLDEN CRIB."

"Yes."

"...Can Sparky sleep in it too?"

"Absolutely not."

"Deal!" Elliana scrambled off Sparky's back and ran to examine her new sleeping arrangement, eyes sparkling. "This is the most extra thing I've ever seen and I LOVE IT."

Caius appeared in the doorway. "Wait, SHE gets a golden crib? I helped too!"

"You," Marianne said, pointing at him, "told your brother to 'not forget protection.'"

"I was talking about armor! For sword fighting! What did you think I—" He paused, reading his mother's expression. "...Oh."

From across the room, Lysander walked in, noticed the golden crib, noticed his siblings, and immediately turned around to walk back out.

"Don't you dare," Aldric called after him. "You're having dinner with Lady Celestine and her parents tomorrow."

"WHAT?!"

"You're welcome!" Elliana called from inside her new golden crib, already testing the cushions. "I accept payment in the form of fancy cribs and unlimited garden terrorism privileges!"

"You're not getting unlimited—"

"TOO LATE, YOU ALREADY GAVE ME THE CRIB!"

Marianne looked at her husband. Her husband looked at their daughter, currently arranging her golden crib to her exact specifications while Sparky tried to figure out if he could fit inside too.

"We've created a monster," Aldric said.

"A helpful monster," Marianne corrected.

"Still a monster."

"Our monster."

And as chaos continued to reign in the Valentine palace, somewhere in the Northern Territories, Duke Aldwick received a letter about his daughter's successful meeting with Prince Lysander.

He smiled.

The alliance was strengthening.

All thanks to a nineteen-month-old chaos agent with a golden crib and absolutely no concept of boundaries.
