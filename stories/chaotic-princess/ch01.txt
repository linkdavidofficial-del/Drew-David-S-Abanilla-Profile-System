Chapter 1: A VERY UNFORTUNATE FIRST IMPRESSION

The first thing I noticed was that everything was blurry.

The second thing I noticed was that I couldn't move my neck properly.

The third thing—and this is where panic really set in—was that I was tiny. Like, unreasonably tiny. My hands were these weird little potato things that wouldn't stop flailing around, and when I tried to speak, all that came out was—

Wait.

I blinked. Or tried to. My eyelids felt like they weighed a thousand pounds.

Around me, I could hear voices. Deep, rumbling. Soft, melodic. All speaking in... was that even a language I knew? It sounded vaguely European, but not quite. The words rolled and lilted in ways that felt both familiar and completely alien.

And then my vision started to clear.

I was staring up at a canopy. Not a hospital ceiling with fluorescent lights that had been my constant companion during those endless overtime nights. No—this was an actual canopy, draped in rich crimson and gold fabric, embroidered with what looked like... were those dragons? Or maybe griffins?

Oh no.

Oh no no no.

I turned my head—well, my head sort of lolled to the side like a broken bobblehead—and that's when I saw them.

A woman lay propped up against an enormous mountain of pillows, her long auburn hair plastered to her forehead with sweat. She was pale, exhausted, but smiling. Beautiful, in that ethereal way that seemed physically impossible for a normal human being. She wore a silk nightgown, and despite clearly having just gone through hell, she radiated this warm, maternal glow.

Next to her stood a man who could only be described as criminally handsome. Tall, broad-shouldered, with a jawline that could cut glass and silver hair tied back in a half-ponytail. He wore what appeared to be royal regalia—a deep blue coat with gold embroidery, and—

Was that a crown?

A literal crown sat on a nearby table.

You've got to be kidding me.

My eyes darted around the room. Ornate furniture. Actual servants in period clothing. Two boys standing near the bed—one around twelve with the same silver hair as Crown Guy, looking composed and princely; the other maybe six, with messy auburn hair and barely contained energy, bouncing on his toes.

And next to me—

Two other babies. Also tiny. Also flailing. Both wailing at the top of their tiny lungs.

Triplets.

I'm a baby. I died from overwork at twenty-eight and got reincarnated as a BABY. In what appears to be a FANTASY KINGDOM. As a PRINCESS. With TWO OTHER SISTERS.

The absurdity of it all hit me like a truck.

Actually, I would have preferred the truck.

The woman—the Queen, apparently my new mother—gazed down at the three of us with tears in her eyes. "Three beautiful daughters," she whispered, her voice hoarse. "Our little princesses."

The King placed a hand on her shoulder, his expression soft. "You did wonderfully, my love."

"Can I hold one?" the six-year-old boy piped up, practically vibrating with excitement.

"Carefully, Caius," the Queen cautioned, but she was smiling.

The older boy, meanwhile, simply observed with that same calm, analytical expression. Future Boring Prince, I mentally dubbed him.

And then the Queen's gaze landed on me.

Her smile faltered.

"Dear..." she said slowly. "Is it just me, or is this one... not crying?"

The King leaned in. "Hm. You're right."

They both stared at me.

I stared back.

Look, I had just processed the fact that I'd been reincarnated into what was clearly a trashy isekai novel setup. My sisters were doing the normal baby thing—crying, flailing, being general potatoes. But me? I'd spent twenty-eight years in corporate hell. I'd sat through four-hour meetings that could have been emails. I'd survived my boss's motivational speeches. I'd died at my desk with a half-eaten convenience store sandwich still in my hand.

I was DONE being normal.

If I was going to live a second life, I was going to do it MY way.

And that meant chaos.

The Queen's brow furrowed with worry. "She's so quiet... Is she alright?" She looked at the King. "Should we call the physician?"

"BIG BOOBA!" I shouted.

Or tried to shout. It came out more like a squeaky proclamation, but the words were clear.

The room went silent.

Even my sisters stopped crying.

The Queen's eyes went wide. "Did... did she just...?"

"BIG BOOBA!" I repeated, raising my tiny fist for emphasis, staring directly at the Queen's very generous chest situation.

Her face went from pale to bright red in approximately 0.5 seconds.

"WHAT—" she sputtered. "What did she say?!"

The King's jaw had dropped. "She... spoke?"

The twelve-year-old prince looked like his brain had just blue-screened. The six-year-old, Caius, started giggling uncontrollably.

"She spoke!" one of the wet nurses gasped. "At birth! A prodigy!"

"BIG BOOBAAAAAA!" I declared again, really committing to the bit now.

The Queen clutched at her blankets, her face now resembling a tomato. "What does that even MEAN? Is she possessed?! Cursed?!"

"No, no, dear, I think she meant—" The King paused, clearly trying to piece together the world's most awkward puzzle.

"What, Mother? What does she mean?" the older prince asked, genuinely confused.

"BIG BOOBA!" I contributed helpfully.

The Queen closed her eyes, looking like she wanted the earth to swallow her whole. Slowly, with the most mortified expression I'd ever seen, she pointed one trembling finger at her own chest.

"She... she means... these," she whispered.

Another wet nurse's jaw dropped.

The King's face was rapidly approaching the same shade as his wife's.

"BIG BOOBAAAAA!" I crowed triumphantly.

Caius was now doubled over, laughing so hard no sound was coming out.

"Uhh... yes! Yes, darling, you want milk?" the Queen tried desperately, looking at her husband with pleading eyes. "Dear, I think our daughter is a prodigy? A genius? She must be hungry, that's all—"

"BIG BOOBAAAAAA!"

"DEAR, SAY SOMETHING!" the Queen practically shrieked.

The King opened his mouth. Closed it. Opened it again.

Nothing came out.

I cackled internally. This was already the best decision I'd ever made in two lifetimes.

Forget saving the kingdom. Forget romance with boring princes. Forget being a perfect noble lady.

I was going to cause PROBLEMS.
