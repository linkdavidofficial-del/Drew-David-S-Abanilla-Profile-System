Chapter 3: NINE MONTHS OF BEAUTIFUL CHAOS

The Palace Staff Survival Guide had been updated seventeen times in nine months.

Rule #1: Princess Elliana is NOT a normal baby. Treat her like a very small, very chaotic four-year-old.

Rule #2: If you see Princess Elliana rolling down a hallway, DO NOT assume she needs help. She knows exactly where she's going.

Rule #3: If Princess Elliana is missing, check: (A) The library, (B) The armory, (C) The forbidden forest, (D) Riding Sparky through the East Wing.

Rule #4: Never, EVER make eye contact with Sparky unless Princess Elliana gives permission.

Rule #5: If Princesses Lilia and Rosaria are crying, Princess Elliana probably took their toys again.

"ELLIANA EVANGELINE AURORA VALENTINE!"

Queen Marianne's voice echoed through the palace for the third time that week.

She found her daughter exactly where the guards said she'd be: in the forest clearing behind the palace, sitting cross-legged on Sparky's back, reading a book on advanced military tactics that she'd somehow stolen from the royal library.

"Young lady, you CANNOT keep leaving the palace grounds!"

Nine-month-old Elliana looked up from her book with the expression of someone who'd been mildly inconvenienced. "The garden was boring. Sparky needed exercise."

"HE'S A CROCODILE! NOT A DOG!"

"He gets moody when he doesn't get his walks."

As if to confirm, Sparky released a satisfied rumble that sounded disturbingly like agreement.

Marianne pressed her hand to her forehead, feeling a headache coming on. This was her life now. Arguing with an infant about proper pet care for an Electro Crocodile.

"Inside. Now. We're having the monthly council meeting and I need you to at least pretend to be a normal baby."

"Pass."

"That wasn't a request."

Elliana sighed with the weariness of someone three times her age. "Fine. But Sparky comes with me."

"ABSOLUTELY—" Marianne paused. Thought about the last time she'd said no. The throne room had needed repairs for a week. "...Fine. But he stays OUTSIDE the council chamber."

"Deal."

Back in the nursery, the scene was predictably chaotic.

Princess Lilia had finally cornered her favorite stuffed unicorn, only for it to be snatched away by a tiny hand moving at impossible speed.

"WAAAAAAHHHHH!" Lilia wailed, her little face scrunching up.

"MINE NOW!" Elliana declared, already rolling across the floor at speeds that defied infant biology.

Princess Rosaria tried to intercept, her chubby legs pumping as fast as they could manage. "Ellie! No! Bad!"

"CATCH ME IF YOU CAN, SLOWPOKES!"

Elliana executed a perfect barrel roll—where did she even LEARN that?—and disappeared around the corner, the stuffed unicorn clutched triumphantly in her arms.

Both sisters dissolved into tears.

The head nursemaid didn't even look up from her embroidery anymore. "Should I...?"

"Let them figure it out," her assistant replied tiredly. "Character building."

The monthly council meeting was exactly as tedious as Elliana remembered meetings being in her past life.

Boring old men talking about taxes and trade routes and— yawn.

She sat in her special high chair (modified with extra cushions because she was still technically a baby), Sparky lounging outside the chamber doors like the world's most terrifying guard dog.

Duke Remington was speaking. Again. He always spoke. Loved the sound of his own voice.

"...and while we acknowledge Princess Elliana's... unique talents, we must address the concerns of the noble houses."

Here we go, Elliana thought, fighting the urge to roll her eyes.

"A princess of Valoria must embody grace, dignity, and refinement. She must be a proper representative of the royal family." He glanced pointedly at Elliana, who was currently trying to balance a spoon on her nose. "She must be prepared for her future role as a nobleman's wife."

Marchioness Blackwood nodded in agreement. "The child is brilliant, yes, but brilliance without decorum is merely... chaos. She doesn't behave like a noble. These wild antics with that creature—"

"His name is Sparky," Elliana interjected sweetly.

"—are unbecoming of royalty."

Queen Marianne's expression went dangerously cold. "Are you suggesting my daughter is unworthy of her title?"

"Not unworthy, Your Majesty," Duke Remington said carefully. "Merely... in need of guidance. Proper guidance."

Marianne wanted to argue. She wanted to defend her brilliant, chaotic, impossible daughter. But deep down, a small voice whispered that maybe they had a point. Not about Elliana being unworthy—never that—but about needing direction.

Elliana was incredible. Terrifyingly smart. But she was also... a lot.

King Aldric cleared his throat. "The princess is nine months old. Perhaps we're being premature in our concerns about her future marriage prospects."

"With respect, Your Majesty," the Duke pressed, "it's never too early to instill proper values."

Elliana had stopped listening three minutes ago. She'd discovered she could make the water in her goblet ripple by tapping the table at specific intervals.

Fascinating.

Two months later, the palace celebrated another milestone.

Princesses Lilia and Rosaria had taken their first steps!

The court applauded. The nobles cooed. The Queen cried happy tears.

Elliana, who had been walking since she was five months old, looked supremely unimpressed.

She sat atop Sparky in the main corridor, legs crossed in a pose that should have been impossible for an eleven-month-old, watching servants hang congratulatory banners.

"Finally," she muttered. "Took them long enough."

Prince Caius bounded over, grinning. "Ellie! Did you see? Lilia and Rosie can walk now!"

"Mm-hmm." She examined her tiny fingernails with exaggerated disinterest.

"You could be nicer, you know. They're your sisters."

"I am nice. I let them borrow my toys."

"You STEAL their toys!"

"Borrowing without permission is technically theft, but toys are communal property in a shared living space, so really—"

"You're the worst," Caius laughed, ruffling her hair. "But you're also the best. Don't ever change."

Elliana grinned. Out of all her new family members, Caius understood her the most. Sure, she loved Lilia and Rosaria—they were her sisters, adorably normal and sweet. But they were just... babies.

Caius got it. Caius appreciated the chaos.

Prince Lysander, on the other hand...

"Elliana." Her oldest brother approached with that perpetually tired expression he'd developed over the past eleven months. "Mother wants you to stay out of trouble today. We're having important guests."

"Define 'trouble.'"

"You know exactly what I mean."

"I really don't."

Lysander sighed. "The Duchess of Silvermere is visiting with her daughter. My... potential fiancée."

Elliana's eyes lit up. "Ooooh. Drama."

"No. No drama. Promise me."

"Can't promise what I can't control~"

"ELLIANA."

She was already hopping down from Sparky, pulling out a wooden practice sword she'd stolen from the training yard last week. "Sorry, can't hear you over the sound of my packed schedule!"

"What schedule? You're eleven months old!"

"Exactly! So much to do, so little time!" She pointed the wooden sword forward dramatically. "TO THE GARDENS! We must terrorize the gardeners this month! They've gotten too comfortable!"

"WHY WOULD YOU—"

But Elliana was already riding away on Sparky, cackling like the tiny chaos gremlin she was.

Lysander watched her go, then looked at Caius. "How are we related to her?"

"She's the best thing that ever happened to this boring palace and you know it," Caius replied, already running after her.

Lysander pinched the bridge of his nose.

Somewhere in the gardens, he heard a gardener scream, followed by Elliana's delighted laughter and Sparky's electric roar.

The Duchess of Silvermere was arriving in two hours.

This was going to be a disaster.

A beautiful, chaotic, completely predictable disaster.

"YOUR MAJESTY! PRINCESS ELLIANA HAS TAUGHT SPARKY TO DIG UP THE ROSE GARDEN!"

Queen Marianne didn't even look up from her tea. "Just... add it to the list."

The servant blinked. "The... list?"

"The list of things we'll discuss with her during her weekly 'you can't do that' meeting."

"There's a weekly meeting?"

"There's going to be a daily meeting at this rate."

In the distance, another scream. More laughter.

King Aldric walked by, reading a report. "The treasury needs to allocate more funds for palace repairs."

"I know."

"Specifically for Elliana-related damages."

"I KNOW."

"Love, perhaps we should—"

"If you suggest hiring a tutor, I will remind you what happened to the last three."

Aldric wisely closed his mouth.

Somewhere in the palace, Princess Elliana was living her best chaotic life, and absolutely no one could stop her.
