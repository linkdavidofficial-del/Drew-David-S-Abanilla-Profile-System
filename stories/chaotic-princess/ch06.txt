Chapter 6: THE BAPTISM OF CHAOS

The royal carriage rumbled through the cobblestone streets of the capital, its golden insignia gleaming in the morning sun.

Inside, three very different princesses prepared for their ceremony.

"Oh wow! We're going to the temple!" Princess Lilia bounced in her seat, her twin-tails swaying with excitement. Her cream-colored dress was already slightly wrinkled from her enthusiastic fidgeting. "Do you think we'll get to see the holy relics? I heard they glow!"

Princess Rosaria adjusted her glasses and turned a page in her book without looking up. "The relics don't 'glow,' Lilia. They emit a faint luminescence due to residual divine mana. It's completely different." Her braided hair was perfectly arranged, and she sat with the posture of someone who'd spent too much time in libraries.

And then there was Elliana.

She sat with her legs crossed—which their etiquette tutor had tried and failed to correct for five years—her wild, flowing hair cascading over her shoulders like she'd never heard of a brush. Her dress was technically perfect, but she wore it with the casual confidence of someone who'd rather be wearing armor.

She was grinning.

That specific grin that made her sisters instinctively nervous.

"I'm really looking forward to this," Elliana said, her eyes gleaming with barely contained chaos.

Both Lilia and Rosaria shuddered.

They'd lived with Elliana for five years now. They'd been dragged on her "adventures" through the Forbidden Forest. They'd watched her hunt down thirty-four wild boars from the back of Sparky while young adventurers questioned their entire career choices. They'd sat through tutoring sessions where Elliana answered questions so quickly that even Rosaria—the bookworm prodigy—struggled to keep up.

Lilia was athletic and kind-hearted, beloved by the common folk.

Rosaria was a magical genius, already catching the attention of the prestigious Celestia Academy of Arcane Arts.

But Elliana was... Elliana.

The only thing the three sisters could agree on was their weekly tea parties where they criticized noble fashion and gossiped. Everything else was Elliana dragging them into increasingly elaborate schemes.

"Remember," Queen Marianne said firmly from across the carriage, her hands clasped so tightly her knuckles were white, "this is a sacred ceremony. You will be respectful. You will be dignified. You will—" She looked directly at Elliana. "—you will BEHAVE."

King Aldric nodded gravely. "This is important, Elliana. The entire nobility will be watching. Foreign dignitaries are attending. The High Priest himself is presiding."

Elliana shrugged, still grinning. "Sure, sure."

"I mean it."

"I know."

"No chaos."

"Define chaos."

"ELLIANA."

"I'm kidding! Relax, Mother. I'll be perfectly well-behaved." She paused. "Probably."

"That 'probably' is what worries me," Marianne whispered to her husband.

Outside, they could hear the crowds gathering. Prince Caius's enthusiastic voice rang out as he waved to the people like he was at a festival. Prince Lysander's more composed, regal wave followed—he'd gotten much better at public appearances since his relationship with Lady Celestine had blossomed.

The carriage rolled to a stop before the Grand Temple of Talius.

The doors opened.

The crowd erupted in respectful murmurs.

The Temple of Talius was magnificent.

Soaring white marble columns stretched toward the vaulted ceiling, where frescoes depicted the gods bestowing gifts upon humanity. Stained glass windows cast rainbow light across the assembled nobility and commoners alike, all gathered to witness the Baptism Ceremony.

Every child who turned five years old was brought here to have their divine gifts appraised—the talents and abilities granted by the gods themselves, manifesting only at this specific age.

Guards lined the center aisle, creating a path for the royal family.

Nobles bowed. Commoners knelt.

And all eyes turned to watch the triplet princesses.

"Look, there they are!"

"The royal triplets!"

"They've grown so much!"

The whispers started immediately.

"Princess Lilia looks radiant! I heard she's been training with the sword."

"More than training—she outpaced children twice her age in the youth tournament!"

"And so kind! My cousin works in the kitchens—says the princess regularly brings food to the poor districts."

"A true saint in the making!"

Lilia blushed at the attention but smiled warmly, waving to the crowd with genuine affection.

The whispers shifted.

"Is that Princess Rosaria? My goodness, look at her!"

"The court mages say she's a once-in-a-generation talent."

"Already reading advanced magical theory at five years old!"

"Headmaster Aldwyn of Celestia Academy is here specifically to see her. They say he's never attended a Baptism Ceremony before!"

Sure enough, an elderly man in elaborate silver robes stood among the dignitaries, his keen eyes fixed on Rosaria with undisguised interest.

Rosaria didn't notice. She was too busy reading her book as she walked, somehow navigating the crowd perfectly without looking up.

And then...

"There she is."

"Princess Elliana."

The whispers took on a different tone.

"I heard she rides an Electro Crocodile through the palace."

"My sister's a palace maid—says the princess once reorganized the entire guard rotation because she thought it was 'inefficient.'"

"Isn't she the one who hunted those wild boars?"

"Thirty-four of them! With just her pet! The Adventurers' Guild is still trying to figure out how!"

"Unladylike, if you ask me."

"Brilliant, though. You have to admit."

"Brilliant and terrifying."

Elliana heard every word.

And she strutted down that aisle with her head held high, her smug smile firmly in place, leading her sisters like she owned the entire temple.

Lilia and Rosaria had long since given up trying to rein her in.

Queen Marianne was visibly praying. Her lips moved in what was clearly a desperate plea to every god listening: Please, please, PLEASE let this ceremony go smoothly.

At the altar, High Priest Gregorius stood waiting—an ancient man with a long white beard and robes that seemed to shimmer with divine energy. He was legendary, having presided over royal ceremonies for three generations.

The royal family took their positions.

The High Priest raised his hands, and the entire temple fell silent.

"We gather here," his voice resonated through the sacred space, "on this blessed day, to witness the divine appraisal of our children. At the age of five, the gods reveal the gifts they have bestowed upon each soul. These talents—be they magic, strength, wisdom, or spirit—shall guide them on their path through life."

The crowd murmured in reverent agreement.

"Today, we have many children to appraise. But we shall begin, as tradition dictates, with the royal family." His eyes swept over the three princesses. "Princess Lilia, Princess Rosaria, and Princess Elliana—please step forward."

Marianne's prayer intensified.

Aldric's hand tightened on his sword hilt out of pure nervous habit.

The three princesses stepped up to the altar.

Lilia looked excited and nervous.

Rosaria looked mildly interested, already analyzing the magical circle carved into the altar stone.

Elliana looked like she was about to commit magnificent chaos.

High Priest Gregorius placed his hand on the altar, and divine light began to glow.

"Let us see," he intoned, "what gifts the gods have granted these royal children."

The light intensified.

The crowd leaned forward.

And Elliana's grin widened.

Oh, she thought. This is going to be FUN.
